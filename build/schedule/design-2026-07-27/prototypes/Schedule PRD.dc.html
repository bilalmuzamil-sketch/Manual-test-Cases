1. Overview

The Schedule module gives shop managers a visual calendar to assign technicians to work order lines across days and weeks. It replaces manual whiteboards and spreadsheets with a drag-and-drop interface that respects technician capacity, surfaces conflicts, and keeps the work order system of record in sync.

1.1 Problem Statement

Heavy-duty shops manage dozens of open work orders simultaneously, each with multiple repair lines requiring different technicians and hours. Without a dedicated scheduling tool, managers rely on memory, whiteboards, or ad-hoc spreadsheets — leading to double-bookings, overtime surprises, and unbalanced workloads across the team.

1.2 Goals

  • Reduce scheduling errors (double-bookings, weekend assignments, after-hours shifts) to near zero with automatic conflict detection.
  • Give managers a single screen to see the full week's technician allocation at a glance.
  • Support multi-day "spread" scheduling for large jobs (engine rebuilds, frame work) that span 40–160+ hours.
  • Keep the work order roster in sync — assigning a technician on the schedule automatically adds them to the line's labor roster.

3. Information Architecture

The Schedule lives as a top-level nav item alongside Work Orders, Customers, Parts, and Reports. The screen is split into two regions:

3.1 Left Panel — Work Order Sidebar

  • Mini Calendar — month picker with week-highlight and collapsible grid. Clicking a date navigates the main grid.
  • Work Order List — cards showing WO ID, customer, unit, status, line/hour count, and lead technician. Searchable and filterable by assignment status, WO status, and priority.
  • Line Detail View — drilling into a WO shows its individual lines (e.g. "Brake system R&R — Est. 4h") with drag handles and technician badges. Lines are filterable by "All" vs "Unscheduled."

3.2 Main Area — Schedule Grid

  • Day View — 24-hour timeline per technician row with time-positioned blocks.
  • Week View — 7-column grid (Mon–Sun, configurable) with stacked shift chips per cell.
  • Month View — compact calendar with per-day capacity bars and shift chips.

4. Core Features

4.1 Drag-and-Drop Scheduling

The primary interaction model. Users drag a work order card (or individual line) from the sidebar and drop it onto a technician × day cell in the grid.

ScenarioBehavior
Single-line WOCreates a shift immediately, skipping the line picker.
Multi-line WOOpens the Line Picker Popover to select which lines to schedule in this shift.
Specific line dragDragging a line from the detail view creates a single-line shift directly.
Large job (> 1 day)Opens the Spread Modal to distribute hours across consecutive working days.

4.2 Line Picker Popover

When a multi-line work order is dropped onto the grid, a popover anchored to the drop cell lets the manager choose scope:

  • Select individual lines with checkboxes, or select all.
  • Each line shows its title, estimated hours, and current technician roster (avatar stack + count like "2/5").
  • Lines where the tech is already assigned show a green "on this" badge.
  • Full lines (5/5 techs) trigger a swap flow — pick someone to remove before adding the new tech.

4.3 Technician Roster & Swap

Each work order line has a roster of up to 5 assigned technicians. When a line is full:

  • A modal presents the current roster with each tech's shift count on that line.
  • The manager selects who to swap out. If the swapped-out tech has existing shifts, a destructive-action warning shows exactly which shifts will be deleted.
  • Confirm button changes to red "Delete N shifts & swap in [Name]" when the action is destructive.
  • All swap actions support Undo via a toast notification (6.5s window).

4.4 Multi-Day Spread Scheduling

For jobs exceeding a technician's daily capacity (8h default), the Spread Modal distributes work across consecutive working days:

  • Date mode — pick an end date; the system calculates how many hours fit.
  • Hours mode — specify hours to place; the system calculates the end date.
  • Automatically skips weekends, shop closures, and days the technician already has shifts.
  • Shows a week-by-week preview with per-day hour allocations and skip reasons.
  • Creates a series of linked shifts that can be deleted individually, from a point forward, or as a whole series.
Example: A 160-hour engine rebuild dropped on Aaron Keating, starting Monday. The system generates 20 shifts × 8h across 4 weeks, skipping weekends and a shop closure on day 10. The manager can trim by adjusting the end date or capping hours.

4.5 Day View — Timeline Interactions

In Day view, shift blocks are positioned on a 24-hour timeline per technician row. Blocks support:

  • Horizontal drag to move a shift's start time (snaps to 15-min intervals).
  • Edge resize — drag left or right edge to adjust duration.
  • Lane stacking — overlapping shifts automatically split into parallel lanes.
  • Now line — a red vertical indicator showing the current time, with a label on hover.
  • Business hours shading — optional grey overlay outside 8 AM–5 PM.

4.6 Shift Detail Modal

Clicking a shift block opens a detail panel showing:

  • Work order ID, customer, and unit number.
  • Color-coded category tag (editable, with custom label rename).
  • Scheduled lines with status badges (In Progress, Authorized, etc.) and labor/total figures.
  • Estimated hours with inline edit.
  • Start/end time pickers (scrollable time list, 15-min increments).
  • Notes section — add, edit, and delete per-WO notes.
  • Actions: Delete shift (with series-aware scoping), Reassign to another technician.

4.7 Events

Non-work-order time blocks (meetings, training, stand-ups) that occupy technician time:

  • Create via right-click context menu on any cell, or by clicking empty grid space in Day view.
  • Event modal: name, date, start/end time, all-day toggle, color category.
  • Drag-and-drop to reassign between technicians or move between days.
  • Day view: live preview block while creating, with drag-to-resize.

4.8 Conflict Detection

The system continuously scans for scheduling issues and surfaces them in a toolbar pill:

Conflict TypeDescription
Double-bookedTwo different work orders overlap on the same technician at the same time.
Weekend shiftShift scheduled on Saturday or Sunday (outside Mon–Fri).
Before hoursShift starts before 8:00 AM.
After hoursShift extends past 5:00 PM.

Conflicts appear as a warning badge on the affected block and are listed in a dropdown panel accessible from the toolbar. Clicking a conflict navigates to the relevant technician and day.

4.9 Capacity Visualization

When enabled in View Options, each day column header shows a capacity bar:

  • Blue fill — percentage of total technician-hours booked vs. available (sum of all techs' working hours).
  • Amber spill — extends past the bar when aggregate hours exceed capacity.
  • OT tag — appears when one or more individual technicians exceed their daily hours, even if aggregate capacity isn't breached.
  • Hover tooltip — per-technician breakdown (assigned vs. capacity), with overtime technicians highlighted in amber.

7. Interactions & Micro-interactions

  • Drag feedback — drop target cells highlight with blue inset shadow; ghost block shows the line name and hours.
  • Shift reassignment — drag a shift block from one technician row to another to reassign. Confirmation modal for cross-tech moves.
  • Right-click context menu — on any grid cell: "New Shift," "New Event," "View Day."
  • Toast notifications — all create, delete, move, and swap actions produce a toast with an Undo option (4–7s).
  • Keyboard support — Escape closes modals, popovers, and cancels armed line selection.
  • Series-aware deletion — deleting a shift that belongs to a multi-day series offers three scopes: this shift only, this and all following, entire series.

8.2 Series

A series is a group of shifts created by the spread scheduler. All shifts in a series share a series key and seriesLines array. Series support grouped deletion (one / this-and-after / all) and render as connected banners in month view.

11. Non-functional Requirements

  • Performance — grid must render smoothly with up to 15 technicians × 7 days × 6 shifts per cell. Sidebar work order list virtualizes at 50+ items.
  • Responsiveness — minimum supported width: 960px (grid scrolls horizontally below that). Sidebar collapses on narrow viewports.
  • Accessibility — all interactive elements keyboard-reachable. Focus rings follow the design system (4px blue glow). Modals trap focus and close on Escape. Drag-and-drop has an alternative click-to-arm flow.
  • Undo — every destructive action (delete, swap, reassign) is undoable for 4–7 seconds via toast.

12. Edge Cases & Constraints

  • A technician can have multiple shifts on the same day (different work orders). Overlapping times in Day view render in parallel lanes.
  • Shop closures (holidays, inventory days) are defined at the shop level and block spread scheduling from placing shifts on those days.
  • Maximum 5 technicians per line — enforced by the swap flow, not a hard block.
  • Shifts with no startHour contribute to capacity calculations but don't appear as time-positioned blocks in Day view.
  • Dragging a shift between technicians triggers labor roster updates on both the source and target tech.

14. Future Considerations

  • Technician availability / PTO — block out vacation days, sick time, and training that aren't Events.
  • Auto-scheduling — suggest optimal technician assignments based on skills, availability, and workload balance.
  • Customer-facing ETA — derive estimated completion dates from the schedule and surface them on estimates/invoices.
  • Mobile view — simplified day view for foremen on the shop floor (tablet/phone).
  • Recurring events — weekly stand-ups, monthly safety meetings as repeating calendar blocks.
  • Skill matching — warn when a technician is assigned to a line requiring certifications they don't hold.
