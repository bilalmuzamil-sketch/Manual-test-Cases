# Schedule — EXPECTED-BEHAVIOUR AUDIT, 5 August 2026

**Written BEFORE any case was repaired, so the evidence stands independently of the fix.**

## The question this audit asks

The QA lead's correction, verbatim: *"The expected behaviors are NOT the ones 'how the build is
behaving'. Expected behaviors are the ones which are either in PRD-COnfluence/Epic STories/Verified
in the Anser sheets by the PO. From the Build we are JUST doing the VIU and the processes attached to
that VIU process. I am shocked to see that how come you considered the Build behavior as the expected
behavior?"*

So every one of the **165** Schedule cases was checked on one question: **does its expected result
state a DOCUMENTED requirement, or does it describe OBSERVED BUILD BEHAVIOUR?**

Sources that may supply an expectation: **(a)** the Confluence specification (page **713031682**,
**version 23**, fetched live this pass), **(b)** the epic's stories **SV-8686…SV-8700**, **(c)** a
PO answer verified in an answer sheet. The build may supply **only** the on-screen labels (Rule 9)
and the pass/fail verdict (Rules 10/12/13).

## THE ANSWER, IN ONE PARAGRAPH

**There is a real defect and it is on all 165 cases — but it is NOT where it was feared.** The
**expected-result bodies are sound: 0 of 165 describe build behaviour as the requirement.** Every one
asserts a documented requirement, and the 27 cases where the build disagrees keep the documented
expectation and carry the deviation as a **separately labelled note that quotes the specification and
tells the tester to mark the test FAILED**. That is the correct pattern.

**The defect is the PROVENANCE LINE, and it is on every single case.** All 165 end with a sentence
that names **the build first, as the source of the expected behaviour**:

> *"This is the expected behaviour **as per the build tested on 8/4/2026 (v3.5-4873abe)**, and as per
> epic SV-8685 and the Schedule specification version 23 (§…)."*

That sentence says the expectation comes from the build. **On the 27 cases whose build contradicts
the expectation it is not merely mis-ordered — it is FALSE, and it contradicts the case's own body**,
which goes on to say the build does the opposite and to instruct a FAIL. **That is the error the QA
lead saw, in its purest form, and this is where it lives.**

**Honest note on where the wording came from:** the phrase is the one recorded in Standing Rule 54,
which took it from the QA lead's own example sentence. His correction today supersedes it (Rules
32/33 — the latest ruling wins). The fix keeps every fact Rule 54 requires — build marker, date,
spec version, anchor — and changes only **which source the sentence credits for the expectation**.

## Why category B is 0 here, when it was predicted to be the largest group

Worth stating plainly because it is counter-intuitive. Schedule was authored **spec-only**, so the
risk was real. Two things prevented it:

1. **The spec is unusually prescriptive.** It enumerates labels, defaults, increments and closed menu
   lists directly, so there was little left to invent. Measured: every one of the 165 assertions
   scores **≥ 0.38** vocabulary grounding against the specification body, and the lowest-scoring ones
   score **0.60–0.96 against the tech plan** — i.e. the wording tracks documents, not screens.
2. **The deviation-note pattern.** Where the build disagreed, the author added a note rather than
   editing the assertion. That single habit is what kept 27 cases correct.

**The ~18 unconfirmed design-pinned labels did NOT become unsourced assertions.** Every quoted
on-screen label across all 165 assertions was checked against the specification, the tech plan and
Branko's answers: **0 labels are absent from all three.**

## THE TWO CASES WHOSE EXPECTATION WAS ACTUALLY REWRITTEN — category A in the body

Found by diffing the live case text against the **pre-write snapshot taken before the 4 August live
VIU pass** (`build/schedule/viu-2026-08-04/snapshots/pre-write-cases.json`), with the provenance line
removed from both sides so a re-stamp alone could not register as a change. **37 of the 165 expected
results changed in that pass. 35 of the 37 were the ADDITION of a labelled deviation note, which is
correct. Two were rewrites of the assertion itself, and both weakened it.**

### A-1 · SCH-SCOPE-05 = [C29967](https://shopview.testrail.io/index.php?/cases/view/29967) — the clearest defect in the suite

**The specification, verbatim (live Confluence page 713031682, version 23, §4.3 Scope picker):**

> *"'Select multiple' is an opt-in control that switches the line rows into checkboxes and shows a
> confirm bar with a running tally (**"Create shift · 2 lines · 6h"**), a **"Select all" shortcut**
> (equivalent to whole order), and **Cancel** (returns to the fast single-tap list)."*

**What the case asserted BEFORE the 4 August pass** — the specification, faithfully:

> 1. The line rows switch into checkbox mode.
> 2. A confirm bar appears with a running tally … *(in the spec's example format, 'Create shift · 2 lines · 6h')*.
> 3. **'Select all' ticks every line** — the tally equals the whole order.
> 4. **Cancel** leaves checkbox mode and returns to the normal single-tap line list without creating anything.
> 5. Confirming creates ONE shift covering the ticked lines …

**What the case asserts NOW** — the build:

> 2. A bar appears at the bottom of the picker with a running tally …, **in the form '2 selected · 6h'** …
> 3. **The confirm button in that bar reads 'Schedule'.**
> 5. **There is no 'Select all' button and no 'Cancel' button.** To leave without creating anything, close the picker with its X or press Escape.

**Why this is the serious one.** The case now asserts the **ABSENCE of two controls the specification
explicitly REQUIRES**, and it replaced the specification's tally text with the build's. Before the
pass this case would have **FAILED** on this build. Now it **PASSES**. It has been silently disarmed —
precisely the QA lead's point that *a test that cannot fail is not a test*. **And there is no defect
ticket**, so the missing Select-all and Cancel are invisible to everybody.

It also fails Rule 28's title-versus-expected check against itself: the title still reads
*"'Select multiple' checkbox mode: running tally, **Select all, and Cancel**"* while its own body now
denies both exist.

**Repair:** restore the specification's five assertions verbatim in substance; keep the build's real
labels only where a label is all the build supplies; mark `READY - EXPECT FAIL`; **file a defect.**

### A-2 · SCH-LINE-03 = [C29950](https://shopview.testrail.io/index.php?/cases/view/29950) — an assertion turned into a tautology

**The specification, verbatim (§3.1 Line drill-down):**

> *"Only approved work order lines are visible in the schedule sidebar; unapproved lines do not
> appear. Includes a back control, **the WO id plus line count**, a line search box…"*

**Before:** *"3. The header's line count matches **the approved lines only**."*
**Now:** *"3. The line count in the drill-down header matches **the number of lines actually
listed**."*

**Why it matters.** *"The count matches what is listed"* is very nearly a tautology — a build could
list unapproved lines and still pass it, because it would then count them too. The original claim
(count == approved lines) was the testable one. Items 1 and 2 were also reworded, but that rewording
is **legitimate Rule 9 work**: it names the real badges a tester will see (`Authorized`, `Complete`)
without changing what is claimed.

**Repair:** restore item 3 to the approved-lines claim, keep the badge names.

**This pass fixes that**: the steps are VIU'd against the labels observed on `v3.5-be42149`, and the
two rewritten expectations are restored to the specification.
