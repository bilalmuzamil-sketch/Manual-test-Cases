# Lessons from 2026-07-31 — what went wrong, what it cost, and the control that now prevents it

**Written:** 2026-07-31 · **Audience:** QA lead + engineering/product leadership · **Scope:** the
three active projects (Report Suite, Schedule, Filters) plus the cross-project TestRail state.

**Why this document exists.** The QA lead's instruction, verbatim: *"I just want my test cases to be
current and accurate and should be the ones nobody can raise the finger at, you need to learn from
what we faced today and make the rules for each and everything you learned today to follow to avoid
similar embarrassment we faced today."*

This is a factual account. Every item below is stated with its evidence file, its concrete cost, and
the **standing rule that now prevents it**. Nothing here is softened, and nothing is dramatised —
several of the day's findings were things our own checks caught before anything was delivered, and
those are recorded with the same weight as the misses.

**One honest framing note up front:** none of the day's work was live-build verified. None of the
three active projects has a QA branch yet, so every verdict in every referenced document is source
reconciliation, not observation of the shipped product (Standing Rules 12 / 22). That limit is
itself one of the outstanding items.

---

## 1. The five things that went wrong

### 1.1 We were working from stale specs — 8 versions behind on Filters, 5 on Schedule

**What happened.** The Filters baseline in `build/filters/requirements.md` was spec **V1.0**
(Confluence version 4, 2026-05-14) and even carried a header asserting it was confirmed current. The
live page was **v1.6** (Confluence version 12, 2026-07-28) — **8 Confluence versions ahead**. The
Schedule baseline was Confluence **v18** (2026-07-22); live was **v23** (2026-07-30) — **5 versions
ahead**.

**What it cost.**
- Filters grew from **78 requirements to 127** while our baseline stood still. Of the 127, **37 had
  no case citing them** at the point we re-pulled the spec (12 of those were effectively covered by
  cases that cited the engineering tech plan instead of the ratified spec anchor — so the coverage
  existed but the traceability did not). The largest holes were Story 13 (page search) and Story 14.
- We spent effort **asking the PO three questions his own spec had already answered** — Imported
  exclusivity (answered by `S2-R7`, v1.4), shared-URL runtime-only behaviour (`S11-R6`, v1.4), and
  the ratified control label *"Back to my view"* (`S11-R7`, v1.5). All three were withdrawn.
- Six Filters cases carried the phrase *"spec v1.3 export awaited"* in their metadata. The spec was
  never awaiting export; it was live and readable the whole time.
- On Schedule, two rulings we were holding as open questions had **already been settled by the spec**
  (events counting toward capacity; the removal of modal "Reassign").

**A trap worth naming, because it is why this was invisible.** The Schedule page's in-body "Version"
field reads **1.0** in every version — the author never bumps it. The **Confluence version number is
the only reliable marker**. A document that says "v1.0" can be twenty-three versions old.

**Control now in place.** **Standing Rule 31** (already written earlier the same day) makes a
currency check of *all five source types* the mandatory first action of any project task, with a
**SOURCE-CURRENCY block** on every deliverable. **Rule 43** (new) closes the follow-through half: a
detected requirement must now receive an explicit coverage verdict, so re-pulling the spec cannot
quietly end at "we read it".

**Evidence:** `build/filters/spec-current-2026-07-31/SPEC-DIFF.md` ·
`build/schedule/spec-current-2026-07-31/SPEC-DIFF.md` ·
`build/filters/ahtesham-review-2026-07-31/VERIFICATION.md` §"What WE got wrong".

### 1.2 Our own cases contradicted each other, and an outside reviewer found it first

**What happened.** Across the Filters suite, **three cases** said the Status filter chip is *shown,
greyed out and pre-filled* on the Estimates and Completed tabs, while **two cases carried the word
"hidden" in their TITLE**. Both readings were live in the same test run. The behaviour itself was
never in doubt — the PO ruled on it (2026-07-17) and the QA lead ruled on it (2026-07-30) — but a
tester opening the run could not tell which case to trust.

**What it cost.** Credibility, mostly: our own Rule-28 quality audit had scored all 110 of those
cases as individually sensible and **did not flag the clash**, because the sense check was applied
case-by-case in isolation. A junior QA reading the run cold caught it in one pass. He also (correctly)
raised a further genuine gap — the *"Back to my view"* action's query-clearing clause and its negative
case `S11-N3` were untested anywhere in the suite.

**Control now in place.** **Rule 28's Dimension 2 gained a mandatory Stage 2b cross-case consistency
sweep** — group cases by the control they assert on, diff their expected results, run an
opposite-assertion keyword sweep, check **every case's title against its own expected results**, and
cluster by shared `refs` anchor. A suite may not be delivered with an unresolved contradiction.
**Rule 33** governs how such a disagreement is resolved: a reviewer's finding is an input to be
judged on evidence — where he is right we adopt it and say so; where it contradicts a recorded PO or
QA-lead ruling, the ruling stands and the cases are aligned to the ruling.

**Evidence:** `build/filters/ahtesham-review-2026-07-31/VERIFICATION.md` (claim-by-claim: 1 fully
correct, 4 partly correct, 1 incorrect with a correct corollary — and **zero** false alarms).

### 1.3 The testers' runs had been frozen since 17 July

**What happened.** TestRail runs built from a fixed case selection (`include_all = false`) **never
pick up newly created cases**. Every case authored and pushed since each run was created was simply
absent from the tester's list. **113 active cases were missing across 6 runs.** The Filters run (352)
was created **2026-07-17** and last touched 2026-07-22; it held 79 cases while the active suite had
grown to 94.

**What it cost.** A reviewer reported *"no case exists"* for requirements we had already authored and
pushed — a **false coverage gap** that consumed a review cycle and made the suite look thinner than
it is. Left unfixed, this would have understated coverage to leadership on every active project.

**Control now in place.** **Standing Rule 34** makes a run-sync check the last step of every
authorized `add_case` pass, with the union-not-partial safety rule (a partial `case_ids` write to
`update_run` **deletes** the omitted tests and their recorded results). Three active runs were synced
the same day under authorization — Filters 79 → 94, Schedule 143 → 165, Report Suite 458 → 465 — with
**zero result records lost** in any of them. Runs belonging to completed projects were deliberately
left alone by user ruling.

**Evidence:** `build/testrail-run-sync-2026-07-31/RUN-SYNC-AUDIT.md`.

### 1.4 The worst defect of the day: a requirement applied on screen and never in the exports

**What happened.** The PO's 2026-07-29 suite-wide **Location column** change was applied by
authoring **six new on-screen cases** — one per report. **The export cases were never revisited.**
The governing anchor, SBR **`S14-R20`**, appears **nowhere** in the deltas document that acted on the
spec diff, even though the diff itself had correctly detected and quoted it.

**What it cost.** Two of our cases — **SBR-EXP-10 = C30285** and **SBR-EXP-11 = C30286** — kept
enumerating the CSV headers *"exactly"* **without** the Location column. On a correct
multi-location build, a tester following our cases would have **failed a passing build and raised a
false bug**. The same on-screen/export split existed on **three further reports** (Parts Velocity
`S6-R11`, Technician Utilization `S7-R13`, Inventory Value `S10-R15`), where the export cases covered
only the `"Locations:"` metadata line.

**Two aggravating details, stated plainly.** First, **both cases had been opened that same day** —
operations 46 and 47 of the authorized push — purely to apply a one-word header rename. The pass
edited the very line that lists the headers and did not notice the list was stale. Second, the
closed-list wording (*"the headers, in order, are exactly …"*) was a time bomb from the day it was
written: correct until the spec added one column.

**Controls now in place — three new rules, each aimed at one link in that chain.**
- **Rule 40** — a requirement that spans surfaces must be traced across **every** surface (screen,
  PDF, CSV, print, API, mobile, print, column-selector, empty state), with a **per-surface verdict**;
  the deliverable ships a **surface matrix**, not a list of the cases we happened to touch. Phrases
  like *"in all four exports"* or *"wherever it is shown"* are hard triggers.
- **Rule 41** — **touch a case, re-verify the whole case.** Any case opened for any reason is re-read
  end-to-end against the current spec before saving, and the pass must log *"re-verified whole
  against &lt;spec + version&gt;"* per case. Opening a case is the cheapest chance to notice it is
  stale; a surgical edit throws that away and stamps a misleadingly fresh "Updated" date on it.
- **Rule 42** — **no closed enumerations without a version-pinned anchor**; prefer *"includes X in
  position Y when Z"*, and give the tester the plain conditional (*"if you are looking at only one
  location there is no Location column — that is correct"*).

**Evidence:** `build/contradiction-analysis-2026-07-31/SBR-CSV-LOCATION.md` ·
`build/report-suite/coverage-rederivation-2026-07-31/COVERAGE-REDERIVATION.md` rows 2–5.

### 1.5 We found the export defect only because someone else's case disagreed with ours

**What happened.** An automation engineer authored a case asserting the Location column *does* reach
the SBR CSV exports. Our two cases said the opposite. **His case was right and ours were wrong —
against our own spec.** His case carried **no references at all**, which is precisely the signal that
could have been used to wave it away.

**What it cost.** Nothing, in the end — but only because the disagreement was investigated rather
than dismissed. Had it been dismissed, four reports' export coverage would have stayed wrong.

**Control now in place.** **Rule 44** — another author's contradicting case is a **bug report against
our suite until disproven**. The first move is to re-derive our own position from the current
sources; if our source is stale or misread, ours is the defect and we fix ours. Only then does it
become a question to them, escalated with both sides' sources (**Rule 39**). Seniority, authorship,
manual-versus-automation and a missing references field are **never** grounds for dismissal. Their
cases remain untouched by us in every scenario (**Rule 38**).

**Evidence:** `build/contradiction-analysis-2026-07-31/SBR-CSV-LOCATION.md` ·
`build/testrail-foreign-cases-2026-07-31/FOREIGN-CASES.md`.

## 2. What we got right — stated with the same weight

### 2.1 The cross-case sweep worked as soon as it existed

Once Stage 2b was added, the Schedule suite's sweep **found and resolved 7 contradictions** — 6 in
the consolidation pass and one further one (**X7**) in the closing pass, where a case quoted a
hardcoded *"outside Mon-Fri"* conflict string that existed in no source, contradicted **its own**
second expected result, and was inconsistent with its sibling case. The same sweep **cleared 13
candidate pairs as false alarms** rather than churning cases unnecessarily, and confirmed a
previously-closed contradiction cluster was still closed. Final Schedule tally: **164/164 sensible, 0
unresolved contradictions, 0 nonsense, 164/164 genuine and layman-runnable.**

### 2.2 The epic check proved the alarming dates were harmless — and found what actually mattered

Both active epics showed "updated" dates later than our ingest, which looked like drift. It was not:
in both cases the last edit was the **QA Assignee field being set from the QA lead's own Jira
account** — administrative, no product content. Schedule's epic (SV-8685) was **completely unchanged**:
same 15 stories, same statuses, no text edits, no comments. Verified two independent ways with no
paging remainder.

The same pass found the things that *did* matter: **6 Report Suite stories we had written off as
obsolete had been reopened** on 2026-07-29, one genuine coverage gap (QuickBooks + fractional-quantity
precision), a live **"Simple Flow V2" epic (SV-8683)** against a project recorded as completed, and
confirmed absence — not a failed lookup — of any Filters epic after enumerating **all 170 SV epics**.

**The lesson embedded in this:** a surface "updated" date is not evidence. The **Jira changelog** is.
That is now Rule 31's explicit instruction, and Rule 37's two-tier epic check keeps the cheap
currency check separate from the expensive full re-read.

### 2.3 Every pass caught its own errors before anything was delivered

- The Report Suite coverage re-derivation flagged the **same four-report export gap independently**,
  in parallel with the contradiction analysis — two different methods converging on one defect.
- The Schedule spec diff was validated by pulling **every intermediate Confluence version** (v17→v23)
  and diffing pairwise, so no delta could be mis-dated; it also proved that **most** of the five
  missed versions had **already been absorbed** into our cases from the Jira epic and the tech plan —
  the local spec file had gone stale, not the test cases. Net new information: exactly two items.
- The Report Suite spec-capture pipeline was validated by **re-fetching the six previous versions and
  reproducing our earlier committed files byte-identically (6/6)** — so every reported difference was
  a genuine edit by the PO, not a capture artifact.
- The run sync was executed add-only with pre-write snapshots and a post-write re-audit: **395 / 429 /
  539 result records before and after**, unchanged.
- Every pass stated its limits explicitly: no live build, read-only where another worker owned the
  tree, zero TestRail writes except where authorized and logged per operation.

### 2.4 The behaviour rulings themselves held up

Where the reviewer argued our cases were wrong on the Status chip and the mobile combined filter
sheet, they were **not** wrong: both were backed by a PO ruling, the ratified design, and the
engineering plan. What needed fixing was our **internal consistency** and the **PO's own spec text**,
which still contradicts his ruling eight versions later. Distinguishing "our cases are wrong" from
"our cases disagree with each other" from "the spec is behind its own author's ruling" is exactly
what the precedence order in Rule 33 exists to do.

## 3. The five new rules, in one line each

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| **40** | A requirement that spans surfaces must be traced across **every** surface — screen, PDF, CSV, print, API, mobile, selector, empty state — with a per-surface verdict, and the deliverable ships the **surface matrix**, not just a case list. |
| **41** | **Touch a case, re-verify the whole case.** No surgical edits: any case opened for any reason is re-read end-to-end against the current spec and logged as *"re-verified whole against &lt;spec + version&gt;"*. |
| **42** | **No absolute enumerations without a version-pinned anchor.** Closed lists ("exactly these headers") must cite their governing requirement + spec version and be written scope-conditionally where the spec makes the list conditional. |
| **43** | **Spec-diff processing must emit a per-requirement coverage verdict** — one explicit row per added/changed/removed requirement; a narrative summary is not acceptable, and coverage matrices are re-derived per spec version, never patched. |
| **44** | **Another author's contradicting case is a bug report against our suite until disproven** — re-derive our own position first; a missing references field is never a reason to dismiss it. |

---

## 4. The through-line

Four of the five failures share one shape: **the detection worked and the follow-through did not.**
The spec diff found `S14-R20`. The audit read all 110 Filters cases. The push had both stale cases
open. The suite had the coverage the reviewer said was missing. In every instance the information was
in our hands and nothing forced it through to a verdict.

That is why the new rules are all **verdict-forcing rather than effort-adding**: a per-surface
verdict (40), a logged whole-case re-verification (41), an anchor that ties a closed list to the
requirement that can invalidate it (42), a per-requirement coverage row (43), and a mandatory
re-derivation of our own position before we defend it (44). Each one converts something we already
knew into something that cannot silently go unrecorded.
