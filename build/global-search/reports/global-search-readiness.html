Global Search v2 — where we stand

Everything the development side has handed over has been tested in full. What remains is closing the defects testing found, and finishing the stories that are still being built.

Prepared 18 September 2026 Tested on the QA branch, build v26.36.7-29ca209 186 checks — 121 on the new feature, 65 regression Every status read live from the tracker on the day
The bottom line

Thirteen of the twenty‑one stories in this release have been handed to QA, and every one of them has been tested — 159 of 186 checks pass. Testing has found 33 defects: 18 are already closed and 15 are still being worked through. Eight stories are still being built and have not reached testing yet, so the feature is not finished — it is ready to release once those stories land, are tested, and the open defects close.

Six of those thirteen are signed off with nothing outstanding. The other seven are tested and are each waiting only on their defects closing. Of the 33 defects, 18 are closed — 16 fixed and re-tested, one settled by a decision and one withdrawn — and 15 remain open. Of the twenty‑one, eight stories are still to be completed by the development side. A further five stories were withdrawn from this release in September — four of them on the 16th — and are not counted in the twenty‑one. This is the normal find-and-fix cycle, and the open list is small and specific.

Three quarters of everything found came from the regression work — and by its nature it caught the things nobody would have thought to look for, because they already worked and nobody expected them to stop. A sample of what it caught, each one raised as its own defect:

  • Customers that had become unfindable By postcode. By website address. By part of a telephone number. By a contact’s job title. Every one of these worked in the old search.
    4 raised, all fixed
  • Vendors that had become unfindable By postcode, by the state they are in, and by the second line of their address — each of which still worked for customers, so the gap was only visible by checking both.
    3 raised, all fixed
  • Vehicles, parts and work orders missing from their own sections A vehicle found by its VIN, a vendor found by a contact’s email, a part found by its part number and a work order found by part of its number all appeared in the overall list but were absent from the section they belong to.
    4 raised, all fixed
  • Vehicles unfindable by number plate, or by part of a VIN Two of the most common ways a person at the counter identifies a vehicle.
    2 raised, all fixed
  • A newly created work order taking about 85 seconds to become findable Measured, not estimated. In a shop that means a work order created at the counter cannot be pulled up while the customer is still standing there.
    Raised and fixed
  • A keyword that threw an error on screen Typing it produced “Oops! An error occurred” rather than results.
    Raised and fixed
  • Work orders no longer findable by the stage they are at Raised, and answered with a decision: searching by stage is not something the product will offer. The check that expected it has been withdrawn, so the two records now agree.
    Settled by a decision
  • Still open from the regression work A catalogue part never held in stock cannot be found; imported invoices are not searchable; a correctly spelled name brings back unrelated records; a vehicle cannot be found by year and make together; part of a word finds a company by its name but not by its street; and moving the mouse changes which record Enter opens.
    6 still open

Sixteen of the twenty-four regression findings are already fixed and re-tested. The remainder are in the open list above. The new-feature checks found eight more — the result rows, three on ranking, the missing “Show all” link and the no-results wording.

What would move this forward

  1. Close the 15 open defects. As each one closes, its story becomes signable. Three of the fifteen sit on the result rows and three on the ranking, so those two stories move fastest.
  2. Finish and hand over the eight stories still being built. Nothing can be tested until it reaches QA; each one is picked up the day it moves over for testing, and the suite is already written for what it covers.
  3. Assign the two that are not yet assigned, and clear the blockers on two more. Four of the fifteen cannot progress until that happens.
  4. Settle the five withdrawn items. Their requirements are still in the specification and still in eleven checks. Either they return to scope, or the specification and the checks are updated to match.
  5. Keep signing off each story as its defects close, so the outstanding picture stays accurate and as small as the facts allow. Six are already through.
  6. Move the Schedule item onto its own track — it is not part of search, and counting it here understates how close this feature is.
