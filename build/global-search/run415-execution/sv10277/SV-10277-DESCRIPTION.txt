*Split from SV-10161.* The fix on that ticket works and should stay. This ticket is about what stopped working because of it.

----

h3. Description

h4. How search worked before the fix

Search gives every result a score and shows the highest score first. Two things make up that score:
* *how well the result matches* what you typed
* *how useful the result is likely to be* — a part that is in stock, a customer with open work orders, a record somebody opened recently

Because the second part could be worth almost as much as the first, a company that matched only on a person's name or email address could beat a company whose *own name* matched what you typed. That is the problem reported on SV-10161.

h4. What the fix corrected

The credit given to a match on a record's *own name* was raised. A company whose own name matches now appears above a company that matches only on a contact's details. Checked on 20 different person-name searches across 230 customer rows — correct every time. *This part is right and should stay.*

h4. What the fix broke

A score cannot go above the maximum. The credit was raised so far that a result whose own name contains the word you typed now reaches *the maximum score on the match alone*, before anything else about it is counted. Nothing can lift it any further and nothing can hold it back — so being in stock, having open work orders, having been used recently, and how closely the name matches all stop making any difference.

When several results sit on the maximum score together, the one shown first is simply whichever was changed most recently.

For example,
* a part that is *in stock* and a part that is *out of stock* score the same
* a company whose name *starts with* the word and one where the word is in the middle score the same
* typing two everyday words returns *every* list with nothing separating the rows

*Example 1 — an out-of-stock part sits level with one that is in stock*

# Press {{Ctrl}}+{{K}}
# Type {{shoe}}
# Open the *Parts* tab

{quote}Parts. In stock (>0) → +0.20; bin location present → +0.05; sold/used in last 30 days (frequency, log-scaled) → up to +0.15; viewed recently → +0.10.{quote}

*Actual Result:* all 20 rows score the same in *Work orders*, *Customers*, *Vendors*, *Part sales*, *Purchase orders* and *Vendor invoices*, and all 20 in *Assets* score the same as each other. The order you see is whichever record was changed most recently.

*PRD 6.1, exactly as written:*
{quote}Each candidate gets a numeric score combining match quality and entity-specific recency/importance signals.{quote}
{quote}The score is clamped to a sane range; ties are broken by recency (most recently updated wins).{quote}

h3. Why This Happens

The maximum score is 1.00. Finding the word in a record's own name is now worth 0.50 by itself, on top of the 0.50 for finding the word at all — so a name match arrives at 1.00 with nothing else counted. Everything PRD 6.1 adds after that has nowhere to go.

1.00 really is the maximum, and not just the highest score returned that day: across 230 results nothing went above it, and plenty of searches finish well below it — the customers returned for {{brake}} stop at 0.32.

*The list can still look correctly ordered*, because results on the same score fall back to whichever was changed most recently. It is that fallback holding the order, not the specification.

h3. Suggested Fix

# Give a record's own name enough of a lift to beat a match on somebody's contact details, but *not* enough to reach the maximum score — so there is still room above it for in stock, open work orders and recent use to do their job.
# Update PRD 6.1, which still says this lift is *+0.10* while the product is applying at least *+0.50*.
