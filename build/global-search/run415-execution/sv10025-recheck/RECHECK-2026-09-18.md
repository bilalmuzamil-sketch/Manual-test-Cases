# SV-10025 re-checked against Nikola's question (18 September 2026)

**His comment, 17 Sep 11:17:** *"can you verify if this one is still relevant after I added a small
section to the row, where we show how we match the result."*

Re-measured on `sv9160`, build `v26.36.7-29ca209`, through the screen and through the product's own
answer. Evidence: `marlene-now.png`, and the result on
[C55685](https://shopview.testrail.io/index.php?/tests/view/2984234).

## What his change did

Each fuzzy row now carries **`≈ close match:`** and each field-level hit says which field it matched
(`Part name: ≈ close match: …`). Our own case for that indicator,
[C44848](https://shopview.testrail.io/index.php?/cases/view/44848), passes on this build.

**It is a display change.** §7 already required it — *"When a match is fuzzy rather than exact, the
matched token in the row is still highlighted, and a subtle ≈ or italicized treatment indicates the
soft match."* So it closes the indicator requirement; it does not touch which rows are returned.

## What it did not change — measured today, not assumed

Typing `Marlene` still returns **18 results across 5 groups**, and **16 of the 18 do not contain the
word typed**:

| Returned | Why it is there | Similarity | Score seen today |
|---|---|---|---|
| ZZAUTOTEST Marlene Freight Lines | the name really is Marlene | 1.000 | 0.6 (word match on name + primary-name bonus) |
| ZZAUTOTEST Bridgeport Hauling | its contact really is Marlene Okonkwo | 1.000 | 0.7 (prefix on contact email) |
| Darlene ×5 (a customer, and four companies whose contact is a Darlene) | 1 letter | 0.857 | 0.3428 |
| Charlene ×2 (both in street addresses) | 2 letters | 0.750 | 0.3 |
| Martens ×3 (two companies + a vehicle they own) | 2 letters | 0.714 | 0.2857 |
| MARINE ×5 (four battery parts + a purchase order carrying one) | 2 letters | 0.714 | 0.2857–0.5357 (part rows carry stock/bin/sales boosts) |
| Coeur d'Alene (a supplier) | 2 letters | 0.714 | 0.2857 |

**The similarity figures are identical, to three decimal places, to the ones recorded on 14 September**
(`score ÷ 0.40 = similarity`). The matching layer has not been touched.

## The strict verdict

1. **This is not a defect against the specification.** §7 accepts a candidate at **similarity ≥ 0.70**
   for queries of four characters or more. `Marlene` is seven letters, so 0.70 permits two letters to
   differ, and every word above falls out of that arithmetic. The build is doing what is written.
2. **It is a real difference from the product being replaced.** V1 matched on substring, not
   similarity, so it could not return Darlene, Marine or Martens at all. Under the replacement
   standard (Rule 110) that is a behaviour loss, which is exactly why the ticket puts the question to
   the Product Owner rather than calling it a fault.
3. **His change makes the noise explainable, not smaller.** The user still reads eighteen rows; they
   can now see why each is there. That is worth having and it is a genuine improvement — but the
   complaint in the ticket was the length and composition of the list, and that is unchanged.
4. **One correction in the developer's favour, which the original report's picture overstates.** The
   customer being looked for is **second of eighteen**, not buried. It is second rather than first
   only because our seeded name starts with `ZZAUTOTEST`, which turns the typed word into a mid-name
   match (0.50 + 0.10) instead of a prefix match (0.70 + 0.10). With an ordinary customer name it
   would be first. And the row above it is itself a genuine Marlene.

## So: should they fix it?

**Not as a bug — there is nothing to fix against the written requirement.** As a product decision
there are three honest options, and the numbers for each are already established:

| Option | What it removes | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Leave it, now that rows say why they matched | nothing | the list stays eighteen long |
| Raise the name similarity bar to about **0.80** (`SEARCH_FUZZY_SIMILARITY_MIN` in `search.yaml` — a setting, no code change, no deploy) | Martens, Marine, Coeur d'Alene, Charlene | none: the intended typo cases score 0.88 (`petersn`) and 0.92 (`frieghtliner`) and are untouched |
| Hold back near-spellings when a strong match already exists | all of them, **including Darlene** | needs code; Darlene survives any threshold because it is genuinely one letter away |

**My recommendation:** the second option, as the cheap move that clears three quarters of the noise
today; the third if the Product Owner wants the old behaviour back properly. Either way the decision
is the Product Owner's, and the ticket should stay open until it is made — **it should not be closed
on the strength of the display change alone.**
