## What was observed

Screen order (`c55711-order.png`) and the product's own scores agree:

| # | Record | Score | Why it sits there |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `P9160-261` today, mine | 1.0 | recency + created by me |
| 2 | `P9160-260` yesterday, mine | 1.0 | recency + created by me |
| 3 | `P9160-262` today, **someone else's** | 1.0 | 26 seconds NEWER than row 1 and still third |
| 4 | `P9160-246` 28 Aug, **Paid** | 0.890636 | |
| 5 | `P2-13` 13 Mar, **Paid** | 0.85 | |
| 6 | `P9160-243` 27 Aug, not paid | 0.836595 | |

* **Recency dominates** — everything from the last two days is above everything older.
* **Created-by-you lift** — rows 1 and 2 sit above a record created 26 seconds after row 1. The only
  difference between rows 1 and 3 is who created them: same customer, same day, same status.
  The scores clamp at 1.0, so the ORDER is the evidence, not the number.
* **Paid lift = +0.05, exactly as specified** — `0.890636 − 0.836595 = 0.054`, between two records a
  single day apart with the same creator. A day of decay at that age is worth ~0.003, so the
  remainder is the Paid lift. It is also why a Paid sale from March outranks an unpaid one from
  August: the recency boost has faded to nothing on both.
