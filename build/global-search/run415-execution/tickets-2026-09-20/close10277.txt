h3. Closing this — the reported behaviour is not a defect

This was raised on an observation that every result in a list came back with the same score of *1.00*, and on the concern that the other ranking signals could therefore no longer affect the order.

The development team explained that *1.00* is a cap on the number in the response, while the engine sorts on the real value, which can be higher. QA tested that rather than accepting it on trust.

h3. How it was tested

Two parts were created, identical except for stock, with the tie-break deliberately pointed the other way: the part *with* stock was created *first*, so it is the *older* of the two, and the empty one *second*, so it is the *newer*.

* if the list were ordered by the *reported* number, both are 1.00 and the newer part should come first
* if it is ordered by the *real* value, the part with stock should come first

!SORT-EVIDENCE.png|width=760,height=490!

*Result: the part with stock came first, on both readings.*

Confirmed independently on real data: searching {{shoe}} returns 20 parts all reporting *1.00*, and the seven showing *0 Available* sit at the bottom of the list rather than scattered through it — which only sorting on the real value explains.

h3. Conclusion

Ranking is working. The identical figures in the response are the capped value being displayed, not a symptom of anything. Closing as obsolete.
