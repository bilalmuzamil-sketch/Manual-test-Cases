h2. 1. What is fixed

!PIC1-fixed.png|width=760,height=850!

*Steps to reproduce*
# Press {{Ctrl}}+{{K}}.
# Type {{deshawn}}.
# Open the *Customers* tab.

The company whose own name is Deshawn is now first. Checked across 20 different person-name words and 230 customer rows: wherever a company's own name matched exactly, as a whole word or as a prefix, it ranked above every contact match.

*PRD 6.1, exactly as written:*
{quote}Bonus for match on the primary display name vs. a secondary indexed field → +0.10. A match on a contact field of a customer or vendor is a secondary-field match and does not receive this bonus (§4).{quote}

The measured bonus is *at least +0.50*, not +0.10. *The behaviour above is the right one and should stay* — it is the PRD that needs updating to describe it.

h2. Why both of these happen

A whole-word match on a name is now {{0.50 + 0.50}} = the top of the scale, and a prefix on a name is {{0.70 + 0.50}}, also the top of the scale. Everything PRD 6.1 adds after that is absorbed. The cap is real: nothing in 230 rows scored above 1.00, and other searches top out far below it ({{brake}} customers peak at 0.32).

*The visible order can still look right*, because ties fall back to recency — but it is that fallback holding it, not the credits in PRD 6.1. Our nine ordering checks still pass; seven of them now pass on a tie rather than on a score difference.

h2. What we suggest

# *Keep the fix.* The result in section 1 is what the product should do.
# *Update PRD 6.1* so it says that a match on a company's own name beats a contact match — today it still describes the behaviour this ticket was raised against.
# *Re-pick the bonus value* so a name match clears a busy stranger without landing on the top of the scale, leaving headroom for the credits in section 2 to keep ordering the rest. Applying match quality and the entity signals in two stages would do the same job.

h2. Checked and *not* broken

* Tie-breaking — {{ties are broken by recency (most recently updated wins)}}: the twenty {{brake}} parts are in descending order of last use.
* Clamping itself — {{The score is clamped to a sane range}}: a cap is allowed by the PRD.
* PRD 6.2's pinned row: a work order number still pins correctly, and an ordinary name match is not wrongly pinned.
* Secondary-field weighting: a prefix hit on a contact's name still scores 0.50, not the 0.70 the PRD reserves for the primary name.
