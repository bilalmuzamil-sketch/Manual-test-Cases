h2. QA re-check of the deployed change

Re-tested on {{v26.36.8-d146c39}} (branch {{sv9160}}) on 20 September, against the Global Search PRD as it reads today (page {{576978945}}, version 17).

The change raised the extra credit a record gets when the word you typed sits in its *own name*, from *+0.10* to *at least +0.50*. I measured that on a clean pair built for the purpose: two companies identical in every way except where the word sits — one with the word in its *name*, one with it in its *address*. The name one comes back at *1.00*, the address one at *0.50*. A whole word is worth +0.50 in both cases, so the difference between them *is* the bonus.

h2. What the change has solved

!ANN-A-solved.png|width=760,height=701!

Typing a person's name now returns the company actually called that name first, above a company that merely has a person of that name on file. Measured: *1.00* against *0.95*.

This is not a one-word fix. I ran *20* different person-style words and looked at *230* customer rows. In *every* head-to-head where a company's own name matched the word exactly, as a whole word, or as a prefix, that company ranked above every contact match. Three searches returned a contact match above a name match, and in all three the name match was only a *near-miss spelling* — which §6.1 says should lose to a solid match. So those three are correct, not a relapse.

*One thing to flag about the fix itself.* As the PRD reads today, this result is not what should happen. §6.1 gives a customer {{Has ≥1 open WO → +0.20}} and {{total open WO count, log-scaled, up to +0.15}}, while the name bonus is only {{+0.10}}. Measured head-to-head: a *quiet* company — no open work orders, never viewed — matching on its own name scores *1.00*, while a company with *ten* open work orders matching on a contact scores *0.95*. Under the documented weights those would be *0.60* and up to *0.95*, i.e. the busy one first — exactly the behaviour this ticket reported as wrong. *The PRD therefore still describes the old behaviour and needs updating to match what everyone agrees the product should do.*

h3. The two match strengths, side by side

!ANN-B-tiers.png|width=760,height=350!

Two companies at the same address with no open work orders. The word *starts* one name and sits *inside* the other. The PRD separates those by two tenths. The product returns both on exactly the same score.

h3. What that looks like in real data

!ANN-C-brake.png|width=760,height=701!

Typing {{brake}} returns *twenty* parts, *all twenty on an identical score*. A shoe kit last used three weeks ago earns a recent-use credit under §6.1; a drum last used in May earns none — yet the two are level, and so are the other eighteen.

h3. Why, in one line

The score is capped at *1.00*. I confirmed the cap is real: nothing in 230 rows went above it, and other searches top out well below it ({{brake}} customers peak at 0.32), so it is a genuine ceiling and not simply the best row being called 1. A whole-word match on a name is now {{0.50 + 0.50}} = the ceiling, and a prefix on a name is {{0.70 + 0.50}}, also the ceiling. Everything §6.1 adds after that is absorbed.

h2. What I checked and found *not* broken

* *Tie-breaking.* §6.1 says {{ties are broken by recency (most recently updated wins)}}. The twenty {{brake}} parts do come back in descending order of last use, so ties resolve as specified.
* *Clamping itself.* §6.1 says {{The score is clamped to a sane range}}, so a cap is allowed. The problem is where an ordinary name match now sits relative to that cap, not that a cap exists.
* *The pinned top row.* §6.2 pins the top result when it scores above 0.95. Typing a work order number still pins it correctly, and an ordinary name match is *not* wrongly pinned even though dozens of them now score above 0.95. Worth noting all the same: §6.2 describes a score above 0.95 as {{effectively an ID match}}, and that is no longer what such a score means.
* *Secondary-field weighting.* A {{starts with}} hit on a contact's name still scores at the whole-word level, not the 0.70 the PRD reserves for the primary name. Correct as written.

h2. Suggestion

Two separate things, and they are both needed:

# *Update §6.1* so the intended behaviour — a company's own name beating a contact match — is what the document actually says. Today the document still describes the behaviour this ticket was raised against.
# *Re-pick the bonus* so a name match clears a busy stranger *without* landing on the ceiling, leaving headroom above it for the in-stock, open-work-order and recent-use credits to keep ordering the rest. Applying match quality and the entity signals in two stages, so the clamp cannot swallow the second stage, would do the same job.
