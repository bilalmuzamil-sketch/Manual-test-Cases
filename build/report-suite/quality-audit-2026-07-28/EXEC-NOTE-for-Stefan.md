# Paste-ready note for Stefan Mitrovic (Report Suite test-case audit — usefulness + sense, 2026-07-28)

We took your "maybe 200 of the 515 are useful" seriously and audited every single case against the specs and your kickoff video — no sampling, every case got a keep/merge/cut verdict with a written reason (spreadsheet attached). Here is the honest result: 56 cases (11%) are genuine waste — 6 we would delete outright and 50 that are the same check written two or three times, which we would fold together, shrinking the suite from 515 to 459 with nothing lost. Another 50 are legitimate but low-value (exact pixel sizes, toast wording) and are flagged case-by-case. The rest holds up: every calculation rule (labor delta, demand counting, earned/remaining, inventory valuation, snapshot math), every permission gate, every filter's effect on the data, the hyperlink targets, and "exports match what's on screen" are each covered by exactly one case, and those map directly to what you and Chris walked through on the video. Where your 200 number is fair: only about 350 of these carry repeat regression value — the other ~160 are one-time "does the build match the spec's labels and layout" checks that should run once at acceptance and then be parked, and we've tagged every case accordingly. So: we're consolidating to 459, tiering the rest, and we can defend every surviving case line-by-line if you want to spot-check.

**You also said some tests "just do not make sense" — we tested that claim separately, on all 515.** We re-read every case cold, the way you would, and failed anything a manual tester couldn't actually run: steps that can't be executed, expected results that don't follow, contradictions, controls that exist in neither the spec nor your video, math that's wrong for how a shop works, or cases where you can't tell what "pass" looks like. The result: **2 cases out of 515 (0.4%) genuinely don't make sense** — one needs the tester to hand-edit an undocumented browser-storage value, one asks a human to spot a one-pixel font-size change in a PDF — and **both were already on our own delete list before this check**. Another 9 (1.7%) are sound tests with confusing wording (for example, saying hour totals should match "to the cent") and we've written down the exact fix for each. The other 504 (97.9%) read cleanly: the preconditions say how to set the data up, the steps run in order, the worked examples recompute correctly, and every button and column named traces back to the spec or your video. So on the "doesn't make sense" half: if you open 20 cases at random, on average not even half of one will fail that test — and the ones that do, we found first.
