# SV-8910 — RE-TEST, designed to DISPROVE our own finding

**QA lead's ask, verbatim: *"I have a doubt about it. Can you test it again."*** So this run was
built as a **falsification test**, not a confirmation: two amounts were chosen that cannot be
confused with each other or with any earlier run, and the two possible outcomes were written down
**before** the receive was submitted, so the result could not be read favourably after the fact.

## VERDICT: THE DUPLICATION IS REAL. SV-8910 STANDS.

---

## What actually happened

**Both delivery rows recorded `$110.14`. The sum is `$220.28` against a real invoice of `$110.14`.**

Evidence: `evidence/ANNOTATED-3-retest-confirms-110.png` — the Vendor Invoices page filtered to
`ZZ-CHK-M`, showing `S-15895 $110.14` and `S-15894 $110.14`.

### The control, in the same run, on the same invoice screen

`ZZ-CHK-P` — the **partial receive of one purchase order only**, submitted minutes earlier in the
same run, by the same script, on the same environment — recorded **exactly `$7.11`**. So the
single-purchase-order path is exact and the merged path is not. That control is what rules out a
rounding fault, a display fault, a stale cache, or a mistake in my own amounts.

Three earlier single-purchase-order controls from the SV-8781 pass were also exact: **$25.00**,
**$30.00**, **$51.00**.

---

## Why the earlier doubt was reasonable, and what this run adds

The first report rested on a scenario where the two amounts were closer together, so a reader could
reasonably ask whether the totals had simply been misread or whether the invoice really carried both
parts. This run removes that reading entirely:

- the amounts are **deliberately far apart and non-overlapping** ($7.11 vs $103.03)
- the **expected-if-correct** and **expected-if-broken** figures were **fixed before submission**
- a **same-run, same-screen control** ($7.11) proves the measurement method is sound
- the duplicated figure **$110.14** is the *invoice* total, which is the tell: each purchase order's
  delivery row is being stamped with the **whole submission's** value rather than its own share
