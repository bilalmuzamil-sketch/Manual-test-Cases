# SV-12666 Verify: tier-aware price resolver, exact rounding, fixed line totals and the invoice part-line snapshot (Phase 3)

## Description

**Covers:** TP-S10-R1–R7, R11–R22, N1, N2, E2–E8; TP-S12-R4, R15, R18, R19; TP-S13-R1; TP-S17-R1, R3, N1, E1; TP-S18-R1, R3–R6, N1, N2; NFR-001, NFR-002, NFR-005, NFR-006, NFR-007; decisions D4, D9, D10, D11, D12, D15, D22, D23, D27

**Checklist**

1. Release day, nothing changed by the client (TP-S1-R3, TP-S10-R5): as `admin` on a client with untouched pricing, add an inventory part, a special-order part and a canned line to a new work order. Each sell price equals what the previous release gives for the same cost and category. The only allowed difference is a half-cent tie, which now rounds up: 15% markup on a $1.70 cost is $1.96 (the previous release gives $1.95).
2. Exact math (D23, NFR-006): a cost with more than two decimals is rounded to the cent, half away from zero, before a range is chosen: $10.005 prices as $10.01, $10.0049 as $10.00, $1.6950 as $1.70 (TP-S3-E3). % Off rounds the Default price first: a Default price of $10.005 shows as $10.01, and 10% off gives $9.01 (TP-S10-R15). A cost that is empty, $0.00 or below gives $0.00 with no + $ added (TP-S10-N1). Evidence: the backend test that runs every `priceCases` entry of the shared vectors file passes, and so does `InventoryValueSellExpressionParityTest`.
3. Pricing order (TP-S10-R1, TP-S10-R4, TP-S10-R16–R21): a typed price wins ("Manual"), then a fixed price ("Fixed price"), then the work order's tier ("Tier, or Default when the customer is on Default"). Markup is cost × (1 + markup), margin is cost ÷ (1 − margin), and + $ is the cost plus the amount, per unit; % Off and $ Off come off Default's price on the same side, and $ Off never goes below $0.00. A fixed sell price belongs to the location, so the same customer gets the fixed price at one location and their tier price at another. A tier on Same as previous tier in a category's matrix is priced from that matrix, never from the General Matrix; Same as previous tier skips archived tiers and always ends at Default (TP-S10-E8). A tier of cost + 0% markup sells at cost, and the line shows that tier (TP-S10-E5). Labor-type fee and diagnostic lines are not priced by tiers (TP-S10-E4).
4. Inventory and catalog screens (D4): the inventory part page, the inventory lists and the catalog show the fixed or Default price exactly as before, and the Inventory Value report's sell value is unchanged on an untouched client, including a matrix whose first range starts above $0.00 and a part that costs less than that start (TP-S1-E12).
5. Each priced line records what priced it (D9, D15, TP-S12-R4, TP-S12-R15, TP-S12-R19): the source, the tier itself (by id, not only its name), the customer it was priced for and the details. A line priced through Same as previous tier records the work order's tier as its source and the tier whose rule was used as a detail. The work order part minted at receipt carries the same record.
6. `GET /api/pricing/calculate-sell-price` answers `{sellPrice, pricingSource, tierName}`, prices by the work order's tier when given `workOrderId`, and refuses a user without 'See Financial Data' with 403.
7. Fixed line total (D10, TP-S10-R13, TP-S10-E6): the parts amount is the fixed total less labor. The split is by cost, with unit prices cut to the cent, and the leftover cent goes on the first part with a quantity of exactly 1 in the order the parts were added, else on the line's labor. Parts that all cost $0.00 split by quantity. The screen, the invoice and the snapshot show the same split, the shares add up to the parts amount, and a cost change never changes the fixed total.
8. Invoice part-line snapshot (D11, NFR-007): invoice a work order with a plain part, a core, an environmental charge, a fee on a single part and a fixed-total line. `invoice_part_line` has one row per billed part row; the net amounts add up to `invoice.parts_sell` and the costs to the parts cost; the core, core credit and environmental rows carry their parent part's category, pricing source, tier and priced-for customer; the single-part fee sits in that part's `line_adjustment_cents`; fees on the whole work order are not in the rows. A recompute replaces the rows and reversing the invoice deletes them. A mismatch only logs an ERROR and never blocks invoicing. Evidence: the invoice-create functional test that checks the rows reconcile passes.
9. Accounting regression (TP-S18-R1, TP-S18-R3, TP-S17-R1, TP-S17-E1): sync that invoice to QuickBooks (and AccountingHub where connected). Each part line goes out with its sell price exactly as on the invoice and no new fields, and part revenue books to the line's category's item; a credit on it uses the inventory part's current category. Totals, Stats and taxes come from the saved line prices, and a customer's Fees & Discounts parts discount still applies at the end, after the tier price.
10. Speed and gates (TP-S10-R22, NFR-005): on a client near the largest client's size (about 270 categories, 10 tiers and 20 cost ranges), typing a cost on a work order part line fills the sell price in about 0.3 seconds, with at most three pricing-configuration queries per request. `bin/smoke-test.sh` shows no 500s, and the backend log is clean.
