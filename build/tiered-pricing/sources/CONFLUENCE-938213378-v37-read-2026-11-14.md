# Tiered Pricing

## 1. Business Case

Heavy-duty shops agree different parts prices with different customers: fleet deals and wholesale rates. ShopView prices parts only by category, so advisors re-price parts by hand on every work order and Part Sale, owners can't see whether an agreement was applied, clients move parts into customer-named categories and lose category reporting, and fleet terms drift until the fleet's own audit finds overcharges. Clients have cancelled, put their launch on hold or become at risk over it. This PRD adds numbered customer pricing tiers that live inside every pricing matrix, set up by category, with a Part Sale matrix beside each work order matrix. Every price stays as it is until the client sets something up.

### Workarounds

- **Editing the sell price by hand on each line** — service advisors and parts counter staff, on every work order and Part Sale for those customers. The most common by far: in the last 30 days, 30% of matrix-priced part lines sold at another price, 24% below it, and 39% of Part Sales part lines were priced by hand. It is slow, misses parts a technician adds, and leaves no record of which agreement applied.
- **Giving up** — owners charge everyone the same price, price outside ShopView, or lose the deal. Five accounts that needed customer pricing cancelled or went on hold, and one held its launch.
- **Customer-named categories with their own matrix** — taught by CS since 2024; about 20 clients use them. The advisor switches each part's category on the line by hand. It breaks category reporting and still depends on someone remembering.
- **Fees & Discounts as a tier** — admins save a parts discount on the customer profile so it comes off every part sold to that customer (30 customers in 3 clients). It can only express one flat discount on everything, not terms by category.

## 2. Feature Overview

**Tiers**

- A tier is a numbered price level shared by many customers. Default is tier 1, and every customer starts on it. Tiers have no limit and can't be reordered.
- A new tier opens on its own page and prices as Same as previous tier in every matrix until it is given rules.
- On a tier's page, Set Rule gives the tier one rule in every checked matrix, with Undo.
- A tier with customers can't be archived or deleted; a tier that has priced a line is archived, not deleted. Archived tiers keep their rules and can be reactivated.

**Matrices**

- A matrix is a reusable set of cost ranges with a Default column and a column per active tier. Several categories can share one; a matrix with no categories is Inactive and prices nothing.
- A tier's column is Same as previous tier, Off default price (% Off, $ Off) or From cost (+ $, Markup %, Margin %), set per cost range.
- Each matrix has a Work Order Matrix side and an optional Part Sale Matrix side.
- The matrix page is the only matrix editor. The grid is edited in place; saving a shared matrix asks to change prices in all its categories. A value that sells below cost shows a warning, never a block.
- The Price Part Simulator under the grid shows each tier's price and profit for a typed cost.
- The General Matrix, today's default matrix renamed, prices every category without a matrix of its own.

**Categories**

- The Categories page shows each category's matrix and every tier's rule. A category opens as a page with its matrix's grid.
- A matrix shared by several categories is edited from the matrix, not from a category.
- Uncategorized is a system category on the General Matrix. Deleting a category moves its parts and lines there.

**Customers**

- Each customer has a work order tier and a Part Sales tier, set in Edit Customer or in bulk from a tier. The tier fields show once the client has more than one active tier.
- The customer card on the customer page gains a Pricing section.

**Work orders and Part Sales**

- One lookup prices each part: a typed price, then a fixed price on the part, then the work order's tier in the matrix of the part's category. Fixed line totals work as today.
- A price on a line never changes because a setting, tier or customer changes. Editing a line's cost or category reprices it; a tier-priced line received at a new cost is repriced by the tier it recorded.
- An open work order or Part Sale keeps the tier its customer had when it was made, archived or not; parts added later use that tier's current rules. Moving a customer to another tier affects only work orders and Part Sales made afterwards, or split off.
- Each line records what priced it: the price opens its details, a Priced by column names the source, and a gray icon marks Manual prices. None of it reaches the customer.

**Reports**

- Totals, invoices and accounting use the saved line prices, as today.
- Sales by Tier and Sales by Category ship in the same release under their own PRD.

**Audit Log**

- Changes to matrices, tiers and categories are recorded from release, in an Audit Log on each one and on each list.

**Release day**

- Nothing changes until the client sets something up: every customer is on Default and every price stays the same. There is no feature flag.
- CS helps clients using customer-named categories move onto tiers and delete those categories.

**Out of Scope**

- **Contracts and per-customer part prices.** A later release; a customer who needs their own pricing goes on a one-customer tier. A fixed sell price on catalog parts ([SV-7264](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-7264)) is separate.
- **Cost floor.** A later release; the matrix only warns (Story 15).
- **List price on the invoice**, showing a discount ([SV-9648](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-9648)). A later release.
- **Vendor pricing, price files, a matrix importer and bulk price upload** ([SV-3978](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-3978)). With the import overhaul.
- **Fixed price management** in Settings › Pricing and across locations ([SV-4485](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-4485), [SV-5447](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-5447); epic [SV-4400](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-4400)). Fixed prices work as today.
- **Changing the cost a matrix prices from** ([SV-6607](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-6607)). Tiers use the cost on the line.
- **Showing a work order's tier on the work order, and moving its parts to the customer's current tier in bulk** (a fast follow). In this release the work order's tier shows only in the Priced by details; splitting is the way to use a customer's new tier (S11-E6).
- **Repricing saved lines in bulk** (S11-R6). Faster matrix updates and caching ([SV-5163](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-5163), [SV-5231](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-5231)) are for the tech plan.

- **A sell price entered at receiving, and Quick Add markup per line** ([SV-10601](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10601), [SV-9860](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-9860)). If built later, a price typed at receiving counts as Manual.
- **Keeping a sell price typed before the part has a cost** ([SV-10594](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10594)). The calculated price replaces it, as today (S11-R16).
- **Invoices, estimates, printouts, PDFs, the customer portal, QuickBooks, AccountingHub and the Open API** show and receive only the sell price, as today (S12-N3, S18-R1, S18-R4).

- **Core charges.** Priced outside the matrix, as today; tiers don't price them.

## 3. Jobs to be Done / Goals

> **When** I add parts to a work order for a fleet or wholesale customer, **I want to** see which agreed price applies to each part, **so I can** trust the invoice without checking every line. (Service advisor)

> **When** I agree pricing with a fleet or wholesale customer, **I want to** set it up once and give it to that customer, **so I can** trust that every work order honors it. (Owner, admin or parts manager)

> **When** my brake margins are too thin, **I want to** open Brakes and see and change every tier's price for brakes in one place, **so I can** fix a category's pricing without hunting through tiers. (Parts manager)

> **When** many categories should price the same way, **I want to** build one matrix and give it to all of them, **so I can** change their pricing once. (Parts manager)

> **When** I sell parts to a wholesale or fleet customer, **I want** their price to come up on its own, **so I can** quote quickly. (Parts counter)

> **When** a fleet customer audits my invoices against our agreement, **I want to** show that every part was charged the agreed price, **so I can** keep the deal and get paid without disputes. (Owner, admin, service manager or the client's finance/AR person)

*Out of scope for this release:* when a customer's pricing changes or the wrong customer was picked, update the work order's parts to the right prices in one step (Service advisor).

**Goals**

- A part added to a work order or Part Sale for a customer on a tier is priced by the work order's tier with no hand edit.
- No pricing settings change alters a work order or Part Sale already made: open ones keep their tier, and every price already on a line stays.
- Every part line shows what priced it: a tier, Default, a fixed price, a fixed line total or a hand-entered price.
- From the Categories page, a parts manager sees every category's price for every tier without opening anything.
- Hand pricing halves on work orders. The clients measured are those with a tier pricing parts at 90 days. Their baseline is the share of their work order part lines whose sell price differs from the price the lookup gives, in the 30 days before release. The same query on the same clients at 30 and 90 days after release is the result. Target: half the baseline.
- The counter prices on its own: the same measure on the same clients' Part Sales part lines falls to about half its baseline.
- Clients who want tiers use them. Adoption has no target: 58% of clients have never edited the seeded default matrix and are not expected to set up tiers. At 90 days, report how many clients have a tier pricing parts, and CS checks in with the held and at-risk accounts.
- Every goal is measured with the same read-only production queries as the business case, at 30 and 90 days after release.
- Clients who set nothing up see no change: every price, receiving and canned lines included, and every report works exactly as it does today.
- A client can see in the matrix every value that sells a part below cost.
- Customer-named categories are gone within 90 days of release: every client on CS's list of clients using them has deleted them with CS's help, and the parts that came back as Uncategorized have their real categories.

## 4. Key Decisions

- **Tiers live inside matrices, and a category picks one matrix.** Every matrix has a Default column and a column for every tier; a tier without rules of its own is Same as previous tier, down to that matrix's Default, and every new tier starts that way. **Rationale:** parts managers set margins one category at a time and need every tier's price side by side, and no tier is ever without a price. **Rejected:** a tier owning its own matrices; a tier-wide fallback rule in the General Matrix.
- **The General Matrix is today's default matrix, renamed.** It prices every category without a matrix of its own, Uncategorized included. **Rationale:** no price changes on release day, and "Default" stays the tier's word. **Rejected:** a new, empty fallback matrix; the name "Default Matrix".
- **A customer has a work order tier and a separate Part Sales tier, and a matrix can have a separate Part Sale side.** **Rationale:** a fleet can be on Fleet for shop work while a driver buying at the counter pays another price. **Rejected:** one tier for both channels; a client-wide Part Sales default.
- **One lookup prices every part: a typed price, then a fixed price on the part, then the customer's tier.** The line records what priced it. **Rationale:** a fixed price is set for a reason the system can't see, and the sell-price clean-up ([SV-11165](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-11165)) needs the same resolver. **Rejected:** a tier overriding a fixed price; two resolvers that could disagree.
- **Pricing settings never change work orders and Part Sales already made.** A price already on a line never changes because pricing settings, a tier or the customer change. An open work order or Part Sale keeps the tier its customer had when it was made, archived or not; parts added later use that tier's current rules. Moving a customer to another tier affects only work orders and Part Sales made afterwards or split off. Editing a line's cost or category reprices that line. A line priced by a tier other than Default and received at a new cost is priced again by the tier it recorded; a Default or before-tiers line keeps its margin. **Rationale:** a customer may already have approved the work order's pricing, and a client who sets nothing up sees no change. **Rejected:** repricing open lines; open work orders following the customer's new tier; a shop-wide receiving switch; the customer's current tier at receiving.
- **A matrix that prices more than one category is edited from the matrix, never from a category.** Saving its grid asks Change All. **Rationale:** from a category, a user assumes they change only that category. **Rejected:** Change All or Save As New Matrix from a category's page.
- **Rule types are grouped by what they work from, with no negative values.** Off default price: % Off, $ Off. From cost: + $, Markup %, Margin %, with today's markup and margin math. **Rationale:** the group names the base once, and prices from existing rules can't move. **Rejected:** rules that take negative amounts; a new margin engine.
- **Matrix, tier and category changes are recorded from release and shown in an Audit Log.** ShopView records none of them today, so the recording is new; the display reuses ShopView's existing audit log. **Rationale:** pricing is where clients most need to see who changed what. **Rejected:** recording with no client screen; matrices and tiers only.
- **Contracts are deferred: a customer who needs their own pricing goes on a one-customer tier.** **Rationale:** a one-customer tier covers the need, and the release stays focused. **Rejected:** customer-specific pricing with dates in this release.

**Permissions**

- **No new permission is added.** Each surface uses existing permissions, named as in settings:
    - Every rule here is enforced, not only hidden (S16-R5). S16-R4, S16-R11 and S16-N5 say what a user with only some of these permissions sees.
    - **Rationale:** each surface already has a permission that fits, and ShopView already requires 'See Financial Data' for parts pricing data. **Rejected:** a new pricing permission; showing matrix rules to users without 'See Financial Data'.

## 5. Terminology

- **Client** → a business that has bought ShopView. **Customer** → a client's own customer, who buys parts and service from the client.
- **Tier** → a named, numbered price level shared by many customers, with a column in every matrix. Not a customer category.
- **Default (tier)** → tier 1, which every customer starts on. Clients can rename it; screens show its current name. Not the General Matrix.
- **Matrix** → a reusable set of cost ranges with a Default column and a column per tier, used by one or more categories. Not owned by a tier.
- **General Matrix** → the one matrix that prices every category without a matrix of its own, Uncategorized included. Not the Default tier.
- **Work Order Matrix / Part Sale Matrix** → the two sides of one matrix. Part Sales use the work order side while the Part Sale side is off.
- **Same as previous tier** → a tier column that pays exactly what the tier before it pays in the same matrix, ending at Default. A tier's rule picker reads "Previous tier"; grid cells and summary labels read "Uses previous tier".
- **Off default price** → % Off or $ Off, taken from Default's sell price. **From cost** → + $, Markup % or Margin %, on the part's cost.
- **Mixed rules** → the label of a column whose cost ranges use different From cost types. Never a choice in a picker.
- **Margin % / markup %** → margin is a share of the sell price, markup a share of cost: on a part costing 10, 30% markup sells at 13 and 30% margin at 14.29.
- **Active / Inactive (matrix)** → Active when at least one category uses it (the General Matrix always is); Inactive prices nothing and keeps its rules.
- **Uncategorized** → the system category every new part starts in, always on the General Matrix. Not "no category".
- **Channel** → work orders or Part Sales. Each customer has one tier per channel.
- **Work order's tier** → the tier the customer had for that channel when the work order or Part Sale was made; Default for one already open on release day. It prices every part added while it is open, even after the tier is archived; a work order or Part Sale split off takes the customer's current tier.
- **Pricing source** → what priced a line: Tier, Default, Fixed price, Fixed line total or Manual.
- **Priced by** → the column that names the pricing source. For a tier price it names the work order's tier, even when the rule came from an earlier tier through Same as previous tier.
- **Manual price** → a sell price or margin typed on the line that differs from the calculated one.
- **Fixed price** → a sell price set on the inventory part (Fixed rules). Only a typed price beats it.
- **Before tiers** → lines priced before the release, in price details ("Priced before tiers") and reports.
- **Customer-named category** → a category a client made to price one customer's parts, identified by CS. The system treats it like any other category.
- **Audit Log** → the dialog, from a matrix's, tier's or category's three-dot menu or its list's, showing who changed what and when.
- **Phone / tablet** → a phone is a screen narrower than 768 px; a tablet is 768 px and wider.
- **Vocabulary** → *location* and *customer* in this PRD are *workplace* and *company* in the backend.

## 6. Assumptions

- The largest client has about 270 categories; a matrix has up to 20 cost ranges, most under 5 (S10-R22).
- Most clients need a handful of tiers, and none needs more than 9 by today's customer discounts. The grid fits about 10 tier columns and scrolls sideways beyond that (S4-R27).
- The Open API writes nothing to work orders, and invoices imported from previous software carry no part lines (S11-R7). Engineering verifies this in the tech plan.
- Today's pricing behaves as written under "Pricing today" below. Wherever this PRD says "as today", that is the baseline.

### Pricing today (baseline)

Read from the code and production on 2026-10-06/07.

**How a part's sell price is decided today**

1. A sell price typed on the line wins, if the user can change pricing.
2. Otherwise, a fixed sell price on the inventory part.
3. Otherwise, if the part has a category and a cost above zero: the matrix that holds the part's category, else the default matrix, finds the cost range and applies its markup.
4. Otherwise, no matrix price.

This PRD keeps this order and adds the customer's tier inside step 3.

**Arithmetic**

- Cost is the cost entered on the line, per unit.
- One rule type exists: markup by cost range. Price = cost × (1 + markup), rounded once to the cent, half away from zero.
- Ranges include both ends; a cost below the first range uses the first range's markup, above the last uses the last.
- Negative markup is rejected, so a matrix never prices below the cost it was given. 181 rules in 70 shops are 0% (sell at cost).
- Work order margin = (sell − cost) ÷ sell, per part. Typing a margin on a line prices cost ÷ (1 − margin).
- Editing a cost or category on a line re-runs the matrix lookup (SV-10035). Receiving a special-order part at a new cost keeps the line's margin as a ratio, hand-typed prices included (SV-9965). A part received with no prior cost is priced from the matrix.
- There is no floor between ranges: wherever the markup drops at a boundary, a part that costs more sells for less.

**Categories and matrices**

- Each category has at most one matrix; one matrix can serve many categories.
- Matrices, categories, the default matrix and customers belong to the client, not the location. Inventory parts, fixed rules, Fees & Discounts and labor rates are per location.
- Customer fees and discounts are copied onto a work order when it is created and frozen there.
- A category's tax-exempt setting is copied onto the line.

### Story 1: Release day

As an owner, I want the release to change nothing until I set something up so that no price moves without my say.

Requirements:

- S1-R9: Each existing category matrix becomes a matrix in the library with the same name, rules and categories. No category moves to another matrix. A client with no category matrices has only the General Matrix. A category matrix with no rules (today its categories are priced by the default matrix) gets a copy of its client's General Matrix rules on release day, so no price changes; afterwards it keeps its own rules, and later General Matrix edits don't reach it.
- S1-R10: Apart from the rename in S1-R1 and the name suffixes in S1-E9 and S1-E13, no matrix, rule or price changes on release day. No matrix has a tier column other than Default, no matrix has its Part Sale side on, and the client has one tier, Default. The below-cost warning (Story 15) only informs.
- S1-R11: Uncategorized already uses the default matrix and can't be renamed today, so the rename to General Matrix changes nothing for it.
- S1-R2: Every existing customer is on the Default tier for work orders and for Part Sales.
- S1-R3: For a client who changes nothing, no sell price changes on release day. Parts already on work orders and Part Sales keep their prices (S11-R6); they never show the Manual icon, and their details read "Priced before tiers" (S12-R6). - S1-R6: Fixed rules are unchanged on release day and work as today.
- S1-R7: There is no feature flag. Everything in this PRD ships to every client on release day.
- S1-R8: Before release, the release note (the in-app What's New entry) and the client email explain these changes in plain words:
    1. Settings › Pricing has Tiers, Matrices and Fixed Rules tabs. The default matrix is now called the General Matrix, unchanged.
    2. Settings › Categories shows each category's pricing matrix and every tier's price, and edits it in place.
    3. Matrices gain tier columns, a Part Sale Matrix tab, the % Off, $ Off and + $ rules, the Price Part Simulator, the below-cost warning and an Audit Log. Tiers and categories get an Audit Log too, and the Matrices, Tiers and Categories lists each have one, deleted ones included.
    4. Part lines on work orders and Part Sales show a Priced by column, and the price itself opens the details of what priced it.
    5. The customer card on the customer page gains a Pricing section, and Edit Customer gains the two tier fields once the client has more than one active tier.
    6. A part on a line priced by a tier other than Default, received at a new cost, is priced again by that tier's current rules: the tier the line was priced for, archived or not, not the customer's current one. A line priced by Default keeps its margin, as today (S11-R9).
    7. For a customer on a tier other than Default, every part on a canned line is priced by that tier when the canned line is added, not at the sell price saved on the canned line. For a customer on Default, nothing changes (S11-R7). A part with no cost keeps the saved price until its cost comes in (S11-E8).
    8. Change Customer says that existing parts keep their prices.
    9. The new Sales by Tier and Sales by Category reports under Reports › Parts (S17-R2).
    10. For clients using customer-named categories: how to move onto tiers with CS, that deleting those categories moves their parts and past lines to Uncategorized (S1-E1), and to delete them before using Set Rule with % Off or $ Off (S1-E2).
    11. A parts discount saved in a customer's Fees & Discounts still applies on top of their tier price (S17-E1). Clients who used it as a tier remove it when they move the customer onto a tier; CS's heads-up covers the 3 clients who use it today.

Moving a customer to another tier, or archiving a tier, changes nothing on work orders and Part Sales already made: an open one keeps the tier it was made with, for parts added later too, and one open on release day keeps Default. Lines split onto a new work order or Part Sale are priced by the customer's current tier (S10-R14, S11-E6).

Negative Cases:

- S1-N1: None. Nothing in this story is a user action, so there is nothing to refuse.

Edge Cases:

- S1-E1: Clients using customer-named categories keep working as before until they move. Deleting one moves its parts and past lines to Uncategorized (S8-R8, S8-E1); nothing recategorizes them.
- S1-E2: ShopView doesn't detect customer-named categories and blocks nothing in their matrices; Set Rule reaches them too when their matrices are checked (S2-R24). - S10-R21: A tier never borrows from another matrix: the General Matrix prices only categories without a matrix of their own. A tier on Same as previous tier in a category's matrix is priced from that matrix, even when the General Matrix gives the tier something else.
- S10-R2: Default's column always prices a part that has a cost, so every tier, through Same as previous tier, always has a price. Fees & Discounts apply after the line prices, as today (S17-E1).
- S10-R3: Inside a column, the rule whose cost range contains the part's cost applies. A cost above the last range's start is priced by the last rule ([SV-4409](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-4409)). Start and end are both inclusive, and ranges have no gaps (S3-R16), so every cost falls in exactly one range.
- S10-R4: Each calculation gives the sell price this way:
    - Markup % or Margin %: the value entered drives the price, with the same math ShopView uses today (markup: cost × (1 + markup); margin: cost ÷ (1 − margin)). % Off, $ Off and + $ are the only new calculations;
    - \+ $: the cost plus the amount, per unit;
    - % Off: the price the Default column gives that part at that moment, on the same matrix side, less the percent;
    - $ Off: that Default price less the amount, per unit; a result below $0.00 is $0.00.
- S10-R5: Sell prices are rounded to the cent, half away from zero, exactly, so the simulator, the grid, line prices and reports always agree. Today's markup math rounds about 1 in 1,300 half-cent ties down; those prices are one cent higher for parts priced from release day. Every price shown is rounded to the cent.
- S10-R15: % Off rounds the Default price to the cent first, then applies the discount and rounds again. So the discount is always exactly the percentage off the Default price the client sees. Example: a Default price of $10.005 shows as $10.01, and 10% off gives $9.01 (rounding once would give $9.00).
- S10-R6: + $ amounts apply per unit and are plain amounts, like every price in ShopView today. ShopView doesn't convert currencies. The line total is the unit sell price times the quantity.
- S10-R7: The cost used is the cost on the line, per unit, as matrices use today.
- S10-R11: A customer's tiers price the same at every location. They are client-wide, like matrices, categories and customers.
- S10-R16: What is set per location is unchanged. A fixed sell price belongs to the location's inventory part, so the same customer can get a fixed price on a part at one location and their tier price at another. Fees & Discounts and labor rates follow the location, as today.
- S10-R12: Special-order and catalog-only parts are priced by the same order as inventory parts. They can't carry a fixed price, so a typed price applies first, then the customer's tier. A special-order part with no cost is priced when its cost is entered or it is received (S11-R1, S11-R9).
- S10-R13: A part on a line with a fixed line total keeps its own sell price, as today. The customer pays the line's fixed total. The line stores the fixed total's parts amount (the fixed total less its labor). No tier or Default prices those parts, and their pricing source is Fixed line total. Sales by Tier reports the line's parts amount in its Fixed line total row (Sales by Tier S1-E4). The line also records each part's share of its parts amount, split in proportion to the parts' costs at invoicing, in cents (by quantity when every part costs $0.00), so reports and credits always read the same split. Prices are per unit, so any leftover cent goes on a part with a quantity of 1, or on the line's labor when there is none, as today.
- S10-R14: A work order or Part Sale keeps the tier its customer had for that channel when it was made, even if that tier is archived later. Every part added to it is priced by that tier's current rules; receiving follows S11-R9. A work order or Part Sale already open on release day was made before tiers, so it keeps Default for both channels, whatever tier its customer is later put on; to use the customer's tier, the advisor splits the lines onto a new work order or Part Sale (S11-E6). Moving the customer to another tier affects only work orders and Part Sales made afterwards, or made by splitting (S11-E6). Changing the customer on a work order or Part Sale doesn't change its tier either: new parts keep being priced by the tier it was made with (S14-R3).
- S10-R22: When a cost is typed on a work order part line, the sell price fills in as quickly as today (about 0.3 seconds) at the largest client's size: about 270 categories, 10 tiers and 20 cost ranges. Settings actions (Set Rule on a tier's page, Change All) have no speed target.

Negative Cases:

- S10-N1: If the part has no cost (empty, $0.00 or below), no rule prices it: the sell price is $0.00 and no + $ amount is added. This is today's behavior for a part added with no cost, and it now also applies when the cost of a part already on a line is edited to $0.00, which today keeps the old sell price. The line is logged under the work order's tier (Default included) at $0.00, so Priced by and the details name that tier. It is priced by its rule once its cost is entered (S11-R1). A part with no cost on a canned line is the exception (S11-E8).
- S10-N2: If the user lacks 'See Financial Data', the price, margin, Manual icon, Priced by column and details are hidden, as prices are today.

Edge Cases:

- S10-E2: If a rule gives a price below cost (for example a deep % Off on a cheap part), the price stands. The margin shows negative, as a manual price below cost does today. No approval step is added; the matrix warns about it beforehand (S15-R5).
- S10-E3: If two admins change a matrix while a part is being priced, the part uses the configuration saved at the moment it is priced.
- S10-E4: Fee and diagnostic lines that are labor (time at a labor rate, such as a service call, travel or a diagnosis) are not priced by tiers.
- S10-E7: A fee entered as a part (shop supplies, a fuel surcharge, a card fee) is a part like any other. A fixed sell price keeps it fixed (S10-R1). Otherwise, the customer's tier prices it by its category. No outreach is planned to clients with fee parts that have no fixed sell price; a client who sets up tiers owns the change to those fees' sell prices. About 1,500 fee part lines a quarter, across 69 clients, have no fixed sell price.

There is no separate internal price path in the pricing order ([SV-9414](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-9414)). Parts priced at cost this way move as normal. The canned line's labor isn't repriced;
    - inspections;

- S16-R8: The Categories page's pricing columns, its matrix grid and every edit to a matrix need 'Settings — Parts' and 'See Financial Data'. Without 'Settings — Parts', there is no Categories page, as today.
- S16-R5: Viewing and editing tiers, matrices, the Price Part Simulator and category pricing needs both 'Settings — Parts' and 'See Financial Data'; so does the Audit Log (S19-R21). Without 'See Financial Data', price details and the pricing source are withheld the same way line prices are today.
- S16-R11: A user with 'Settings — Parts' but not 'See Financial Data' sees no tiers, matrices or simulator: Settings › Pricing shows only the Fixed Rules tab. - S18-N2: Internal and no-charge work orders are sent as the Internal Work Orders PRD specifies; tiers add nothing to what is sent.

### Story 19: Shared behavior for every new page, dialog and list

As any user, I want the new screens to behave like the rest of ShopView so that nothing surprises me.

Design: No page of its own; its screens are on the other pages, marked "Also Story 19" Jira: [SV-11709](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-11709) Form factor: Settings pages desktop only by rule; work order and Part Sale behavior mobile-optimized.

Prerequisites:

- None beyond each screen's own.

Requirements:

- S19-R1: Saving, dialogs, field messages and session expiry work as they do across ShopView today: while saving, the primary button shows a spinner and Save, Cancel and the X are disabled, so a double click can't save twice; dialogs close only with Cancel or the X; required and number fields use today's messages ("{Field} is a required field", "{Field} cannot be higher than {max}", "{Field} must be a valid number"); an expired session logs the user out with "Session expired, please log in again." and unsaved work is lost.
- S19-R13: Tiers, matrices and categories are created and edited on their own pages, never in dialogs; no matrix is edited in a dialog anywhere. Dialogs are used only to confirm, to pick customers (Manage Customers), to show the Audit Log and where today's screens already use one.
- S19-R14: Every create flow (New Tier, New Matrix, New Category) can be cancelled before it is saved, and cancelling creates nothing.
- S19-R15: The new pages show no explanatory text or help banners. Explanations (cost ranges, how a price is found, the Part Sale switch) sit in short info-icon tooltips beside the item they explain, one or two plain sentences that never repeat the label. Tier names have no tooltip.
- S19-R3: Leaving a matrix page, the New Category page or a grid in edit with unsaved changes asks "Discard these changes?" (Keep Editing / Discard Changes). With no changes, it leaves straight away.
- S19-R5: The Manual icon and the grid's warning icons have a text name for screen readers. The Priced by column and price details always name the source in words, never by color alone.
- S19-R6: Every new control is reachable by keyboard, including every grid cell in edit. Enter in the New Tier name field saves the tier.
- S19-R7: Every new page, list, tab and grid shows a loading skeleton while it loads; today, core lists show only a loading bar. The price details show the popover spinner.
- S19-R10: A failed load shows "Ooooops! An error occurred" with Try again, and keeps the search, filters, sort and page. Today, the core app shows this message only as a toast.
- S19-R8: Every change to a matrix, a tier or a category is recorded, with what changed (each value before and after), who made it, when, and for which client:
    - A matrix: created, renamed, deleted, its rules (Default's and each tier's, on either side), its categories, and the Part Sale switch.
    - A tier: created, renamed, archived, reactivated, deleted, a rule set for the entire tier or undone, and customers added to it or moved off it.
    - A category: created, renamed, deleted, its matrix, Tax Exempt, and its QuickBooks mapping. A deleted category is recorded with its name, matrix, QuickBooks mapping and the number of parts and lines moved to Uncategorized.

  Clients see these in the Audit Log (S19-R19, S19-R21), with no new permission.
- S19-R12: A client's Audit Log keeps every matrix, tier and category change for all time, as a work order's audit log does, so a client can see what changed at any point. ShopView's own internal copy of these records follows the existing audit log cleanup (90 days today). Engineering confirms in the tech plan what ShopView's existing change log already captures for these records.
- S19-R19: Audit Log is in the three-dot menu of each matrix's page and the Matrices list, each tier's page, its row on the Tiers list and the Tiers list, and each category's page and the Categories list. It opens ShopView's existing history dialog as it is today, titled "Audit Log: \<matrix\>", "Audit Log: \<tier\>" or "Audit Log: \<category\>" for one, and "Audit Log: Matrices", "Audit Log: Tiers" or "Audit Log: Categories" for a list (every matrix's, tier's or category's changes, deleted ones included, each detail starting with its name). It keeps today's search box and columns, without the Line column, newest first, with each change in the details as "\<label\>: \<old\> → \<new\>", and Close. It loads 50 entries at a time, with more loading as the user scrolls, as today. With nothing recorded it shows "No history here. Yet." It shows every change since release (S19-R12).
- S19-R20: Audit Log events are named:
    - A matrix: "Matrix Created", "Matrix Renamed", "Matrix Changed", "Matrix Deleted", "Rule Changed", "Categories Changed", and "Tier Rule Set", "Tier Rule Undone" or "Tier Deleted" when a tier's Set Rule, its Undo or a tier's deletion changes the matrix.
    - A tier: "Tier Created", "Tier Renamed", "Tier Archived", "Tier Reactivated", "Tier Deleted", "Tier Rule Set", "Tier Rule Undone", "Customer Added", "Customer Changed", "Customer Removed".
    - A category: "Category Created", "Category Renamed", "Category Deleted", "Category Matrix Changed", "Tax Exempt Changed", "QuickBooks Mapping Changed".
- S19-R21: Opening any Audit Log (a matrix's, a tier's or a category's, or the Matrices, Tiers or Categories list's) needs 'Settings — Parts' and 'See Financial Data', like the pages it is on. A tier's Audit Log and the Tiers list's show customer names in their customer events.
- S19-R22: In every sortable list in this PRD, the first click on a number column sorts it highest first.
- S19-R23: Each save makes one Audit Log entry on the log of what was saved, summing up everything that save changed, and nothing is logged for edits that weren't saved. For example, one matrix save that adds a range and sets its rules reads "1 range added; rules set for Default and 5 tiers". Set Rule makes one entry on the tier's log ("Rule set in 7 matrices") and one on each matrix it changed; Undo does the same. A bulk Manage Customers save makes one entry on the tier's log ("3 customers added, 1 removed"). A category delete makes one entry on the Categories list's log, with the number of parts and lines moved.
- S19-R18: Every search in this PRD matches a case-insensitive substring of each field it names and ignores leading and trailing spaces; results keep the list's sort. In a customer's tier pickers (S9-R30), a typed whole number matches that tier number exactly and lists it first, then the tiers whose names contain the text.
- S19-R9: Every column title, modal header, page title, tab and button uses Title Case. Articles, short prepositions and conjunctions are lower case unless they start the text, for example "Work Order Tier", "Part Sales Tier", "Manage Customers on Fleet Platinum". Sentences in messages and tooltips stay in sentence case.

Negative Cases:

- None beyond S16-R2 (read-only pages).

Edge Cases:

- S19-E2: If someone else saved first, the second save is refused and nothing is saved. Everything typed is kept. When the save became invalid (a matrix or tier name now taken, a category moved to another matrix, a tier archived), the page shows the reason inline, naming the conflict. When someone else saved the same matrix, it shows "\<matrix\> was changed by someone else. Reload to see their changes."

Error Handling:
