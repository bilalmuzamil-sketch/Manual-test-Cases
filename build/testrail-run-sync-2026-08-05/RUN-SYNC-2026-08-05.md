## 4. The declared normalisations, and why neither fired

`case_title` and `case_refs` on a result record are **read-time echoes** of the case's *current*
title and references — playbook §J declared normalisations **#2** and **#2b**. They legitimately
differ when a case has been retitled or had its `refs` edited, which happened on all three projects
earlier today.

**Neither moved on any of the 1 407 records in SYNC 1**, and that is exactly what should be
expected: **the sync-1 task made no case writes at all**, so no case title or `refs` changed under the
run. The echoes are recorded here so the *zero* is understood as a positive result rather than a
check that was skipped — and, per the brief, they were **not** available as an excuse: any field
other than those two moving on a prior result would have stopped the batch.

**SYNC 2 recorded the same zero** (`echoes_moved = {}` on all 535 run-359 records) — **and there it is
the more interesting result**, because sync 2 ran inside a Report Suite pass that *did* write cases.
Those writes simply did not touch the title or `refs` of any case already carrying a result in run 359.

## 8. What I changed in the reusable checker, and why

The prior pass left `build/testrail-run-sync-2026-07-31/run_sync_audit.py` (read-only) and
`sync_runs_EXECUTOR.py` (never executed). I read both first and built on them rather than starting
over (Rule 27). Seven things are different, and two of them matter a great deal:

1. **Scope is hard-coded to the three ACTIVE runs.** The old `PLAN` also listed 278, 324 and 325.
   Rule 47 now puts those out of scope, so they are unreachable from the new script.
2. **⚠️ Foreign-case exclusion is derived live from `created_by`** instead of a hard-coded id list.
   The old executor had no concept of a foreign case at all — it would have added
   **C38919–C38923 straight into run 359**, breaching Rule 38 on its first use.
3. **⚠️ Verification is by id and field by field.** The old one accepted
   `len(res2) >= len(results)` — which **cannot detect a destroyed result**, because a run that
   loses 30 old records and gains 30 new ones passes that test. It also compared only
   `status_id`, and stored a 3-field digest rather than the records, so the evidence to
   re-check later did not exist.
4. **Snapshots are full and persisted into the repo**, not a digest in a scratch folder.
5. **Case-id sets are proven equal in both directions**, never by matching totals.
6. **The run's own record is diffed field by field**, so a stray change to name, description,
   milestone or assignee would be caught rather than assumed absent.
7. **The sequence stops dead on the first failed verification** and does not touch a later run.
   The old loop carried on and merely printed `*** CHECK ***`.

The 2026-07-31 read-only auditor is still the right tool for the *cross-project* question ("which
runs exist and how complete is each"); this one is the narrower, safer executor for the three active
runs.
