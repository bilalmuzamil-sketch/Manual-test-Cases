1 · Settings: what is there and turning it on
1 · Settings: what is there and turning it on
Settings > Work Orders. One toggle, Internal Work Orders. Nothing else lives on this tab.
First turn-on: pick the internal customers once. Nothing is preselected (the location is not always a customer). Afterwards the flag lives on the customer, not here.
Setting on. Two hint lines and a link to the Internal Work Orders page where the categories are managed.
2 · Categories: two lists the shop manages
2 · Categories: two lists the shop manages
Internal categories: General Internal as default plus the shop's own (Company Vehicle Maintenance, Shop Equipment Maintenance). An expense account per category.
No Charge categories: Warranty, Goodwill, Comeback to start plus the shop's own (Policy dropped as a default). Third column: requires the original work order (Warranty, Comeback).
A category: name, QuickBooks expense account per location, the flag. Archive, never delete; old work orders and reports keep their category.
3 · Where the categories show up
3 · Where the categories show up
QuickBooks ShopView Accounts: the same mapping, one row per category under Cost of Parts. Edit here or on the category. Nothing is created in QuickBooks; an empty row posts to ShopView Review as today.
Customer: Internal customer checkbox plus a Default internal category.
Asset of an internal customer: its own internal category. The QuickBooks mark sits in the explanation: categories are mapped to QuickBooks expense accounts. Priority: asset, then customer, then General Internal.
New work order for an internal customer: Internal badge, nothing to pick. The category comes from the asset, else the customer, else General Internal; the tooltip names it. Removed after the CEO review: the Internal category select.
Pick Forklift 2 and the tooltip names the asset's own category, Shop Equipment Maintenance.
New work order for a regular customer with No Charge ticked: Category required (the No Charge list), Original work order when the category requires it, optional here.
4 · Changing a work order
4 · Changing a work order
Three dots on a billable work order: Convert to No Charge. Same permissions as fees and discounts.
Convert asks for the category and the original work order. New: an optional Original line select under the original work order; the line sets the original technician for the comeback and warranty numbers (else the lead technician of the original work order).
Change category: same two fields.
Convert to Billable asks first.
Every one of these writes a system note on the work order and an Audit Log row: who, when, category, original work order, what was removed.
5 · Closing
5 · Closing
Close: the original work order is required for Warranty and Comeback, with Not in ShopView for jobs done before ShopView. The original technician is stored with it. New: an optional Original line select under the original work order; the line sets the original technician for the comeback and warranty numbers (else the lead technician of the original work order).
Close confirmation names the category's account. Parts cost debits it; the inventory credit stays as today.
Internal Work Order document: stamp and category under the masthead, clear of the logo.
NO CHARGE document: stamp, Category and Original work order under the masthead.
Closed work orders in the list, next to invoiced ones.
6 · One no-charge line on a billable work order
6 · One no-charge line on a billable work order
On a billable work order, while Internal Work Orders is on and the work order is not invoiced. Same permissions as Convert to No Charge. The item replaces the earlier "Move to a No Charge work order" (dropped Nov 5: a shop that wants it separate splits the work order as today).
Clicks Mark as No Charge. Category and Original work order are the same fields as Convert to No Charge; the line stays where it is. New: an optional Original line select under the original work order; the line sets the original technician for the comeback and warranty numbers (else the lead technician of the original work order).
The line carries a No Charge · Warranty chip next to its name; Rate, Total and its parts show $0.00, hours and status unchanged. The original work order is in the notes and the Audit Log, not on the line. No badge on the work order: a normal billable work order with one no-charge line. Financial Info is the cost view and does not change: the cost of the no-charge line stays and posts to the Warranty account at invoicing.
The marked line offers Change category and Mark as Billable. Mark as Billable prices the line at the customer's current pricing and removes the category.
Every line change writes a system note and an Audit Log row: who, when, category, original work order, the price removed, the cost and hours kept.
The invoice of a billable work order: no INTERNAL or NO CHARGE stamp, no badge. The no-charge line prints with its hours and parts at $0.00 and one word under the title, Warranty. Totals, tax, deposits and portal approval on the billable amounts. The customer sees zero; the books see the cost: the line's parts cost posts to the Warranty expense account when the invoice is created.
7 · Finding it and the books
7 · Finding it and the books
List chip: All, Billable, Internal, No Charge.
One report for all free work: whole internal and no-charge work orders, and now one row per no-charge line of a billable work order (Line column, Classification "No Charge line", invoiced date). Summary strip per category above the table. Money reports exclude these rows without a filter.
AccountingHub Closed record with Classification, Category, Original work order, Original technician. One mapping row per category, seeded. Accounts per the AccountingHub owner (Nov 5): 6500 General Internal, 6430 Warranty & Goodwill (existing in the hub chart), Comeback Expense is a new account whose number the hub confirms; migration maps the starting categories automatically, never an empty row for them.
Held names the category row that is not mapped; Dismissed when a Reopen withdraws a Close.
8 · Not built, and why
8 · Not built, and why
Moving a line to its own work order: dropped (Nov 5)

The CEO review settled the line case the other way: a no-charge line stays on the billable work order (row 6). One invoice, the warranty line at $0.00, the rest paid as usual.

What that adds to the build: a classification and category per line; $0.00 pricing of the line on the work order, the invoice, the portal and the Finance tab; the line's parts cost posted to the category's account at invoicing; the hub record carrying the line-level category and cost. Whole-work-order No Charge stays as in rows 3 to 5.

A shop that wants the free work on its own work order splits the work order as today, then converts it.
V2, right after V1: labor posting and technician rates

V1 posts the parts cost at Close: a real inventory movement with a known cost. Labor is already paid through payroll, so posting labor on a warranty job means moving a cost out of payroll into the category's account: an internal labor cost per technician, a rule the accountant signs off, and a mapping row per category per kind (parts, labor).

V1 already stores the category, the original work order, the original technician and the hours on every internal and no-charge work order and shows them in the report. V2 adds the labor posting and the comeback and warranty rates per technician on top. Doing it in V1 would hold the whole feature for the accounting rule.
Separate features, not V1 or V2

Manufacturer warranty as a receivable: claiming the cost back from the manufacturer is a claim process with its own accounting.

Retail value of free work on the document or the report: pricing and reporting, no change to classification or posting.

Goodwill approval flow: who may give work away for free is permissions and approval, a workflow feature.
