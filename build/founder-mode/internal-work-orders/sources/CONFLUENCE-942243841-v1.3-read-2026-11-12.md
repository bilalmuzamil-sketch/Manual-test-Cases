# Internal Work Orders

## 1. Business Case

Shops do work that is not a sale: repairs on their own service trucks and shop equipment, PMs on an internal fleet, and comebacks, goodwill or policy repairs on a customer's unit that the shop eats. ShopView has no way to say that a work order is not billable, so every work order ends in an invoice, every invoice is revenue, and every part on it is cost of goods sold. To get such a job out of the open list the service advisor zeroes every sell price by hand and creates a $0 invoice. That invoice is auto-paid, lands in accounts receivable as a paid $0 sale with a $0 payment record, reaches QuickBooks Online or AccountingHub as a $0 invoice, and pushes the part cost into cost of goods sold. The sales and margin reports then show jobs at a 100% loss, technician efficiency is distorted, and the receivables list carries junk rows. At month end the accountant goes through every $0 invoice, adds up part cost and labor, and posts a manual journal entry that moves the cost out of cost of goods sold into a repairs or vehicle expense account; at a multi-location group that is one to two days of work each month, done after the fact, so the books are wrong for most of the month. This PRD adds one way to make a work order internal or no charge, close it with nothing owed, and have its cost land in the right expense account. Everything else about how a work order is estimated, approved, worked and reviewed stays unchanged.

Customers asking for it: PowerTools theme #514 "Internal Job Cost Tracking for Non-Billable Work" (122 insights from 77 companies), with duplicate themes #390 and #1031 and the adjacent warranty-tracking theme #171 (23 insights, 22 companies). The exact ask exists as backlog tickets SV-5877 and SV-8369. In one QA organization 83% of all invoices are $0, which is what this workaround looks like in the data. A direct competitor sells "Close Without Invoicing" as a named feature.

### Workarounds

- Zero the prices and invoice at $0. Every shop that tracks internal work does this, on every internal job. It is documented in our own account setup call as the way to run internal fleet work. It costs the advisor a line-by-line edit per job, and it leaves thousands of $0 invoices and "Internal" or "Shop Fleet" customers that this design tolerates and must not break on release day.
- Manual month-end reclass journal entry. The accountant or bookkeeper, once a month, builds a journal entry from the $0 invoices to move cost out of cost of goods sold. One to two days per month at a multi-location group. Shops without an accountant skip it and leave the books wrong.
- An "Internal" department, a $0 labor rate or a reduced internal labor rate on the fleet customer. The service manager sets one up and reads internal hours out of the technician reports. The labor rate data then encodes job type instead of a price.
- A separate location with its own QuickBooks company for internal repairs. Large groups only. The internal company's revenue becomes the group's expense. It costs a second QuickBooks subscription and a second set of books.
- Leave the work order at Complete and never invoice it. The advisor avoids the $0 invoice, but the job sits forever in the awaiting-invoice lists and in work in progress.
- A 100% discount or a credit for goodwill and comebacks. Keeps the sale and the cost of goods sold on the books and the discount in a contra-income account, so the shop never sees what free work costs it.

## 2. Feature Overview

Two kinds of work order, one way to close them.

**Internal work orders come from internal customers.** The shop flags its own company (and any sister company) as an internal customer once: in the modal that opens the first time the Internal Work Orders setting is switched on (nobody is preselected: the location is not necessarily a customer and the names differ), or later with the Internal customer checkbox at the bottom of Edit Customer. Settings \> Work Orders holds the toggle, that one-time modal and two hints: a link to the categories page and a link to the QuickBooks mapping. Every internal customer carries a default internal category, General Internal unless changed on Edit Customer; an asset of an internal customer can carry its own category (Edit Asset). A new internal work order takes the asset's category, else the customer's, else General Internal. Nobody picks a category or an account on the work order; the Internal badge and its tooltip say where it posts.

**No Charge is a mark with a category.** A whole work order on a regular customer can be No Charge (the checkbox at creation, Convert to No Charge later, Convert to Billable back), and so can a single line of a billable work order (Mark as No Charge in the line's three-dots menu, or the No Charge checkbox in Edit Line and New Line; Story 12). Both carry one No Charge category from the shop's list (Warranty, Goodwill, Comeback to start, plus the shop's own) and, on categories that require it, the original work order the job comes from and, optionally, the original line on it, which sets the original technician. Categories are shop-managed: Administration \> Service \> Internal Work Orders holds two lists, Internal categories and No Charge categories; a category has a name, a QuickBooks expense account per connected location and, on No Charge, the flag Requires the original work order (on for Warranty and Comeback). Categories are archived, never deleted, so old work orders and reports keep theirs. The same accounts show on Settings \> Integrations \> QuickBooks \> ShopView Accounts as one row per category; editing either place writes the same mapping. Nothing is created in QuickBooks.

**Both look and work like a normal work order until the end.** Estimates, approvals, parts ordering, picking, receiving, clocking and the review step work exactly as today. Lines show quantity, cost and hours and no sell prices: no labor rate, no part price, no totals, no tax, no fees, no discounts. Shop supplies is one flat amount (below). Nobody zeroes anything. One badge, "Internal" or "No Charge", shows next to the status wherever the work order appears, and on the detail page the header card is outlined in orange so nobody invoices it by habit.

**Both close the same way.** Where a billable work order shows Create invoice, these show Close Work Order. Close Work Order takes one press and opens no confirmation, like Create invoice. Before it, the Finance tab shows the posting date, the parts cost and the account it posts to, special-order parts and sublet, the shop supplies amount, and the labor hours on the lines, which are not posted. The press closes the work order: Closed · Internal or Closed · No Charge. Nothing is owed, nothing goes to receivables, no payment is recorded, no email, portal notification or invoice webhook is sent (the work order status webhook of Story 11 is), no tax or fees are added. The closed work order keeps a downloadable document stamped NO CHARGE, with parts at cost, labor in hours and, on a no-charge work order, the category, as proof of repair for a warranty claim or an auditor. Reopen Work Order reverses exactly what was posted and returns the work order to Complete.

**Accounting is one expense account per category.** In QuickBooks the accountant maps each category once per location, on the category itself or on the ShopView Accounts page, where the categories show as one row each under Cost of Parts; an empty row posts to ShopView Review as every unmapped row does today, with no fallback to another row. In AccountingHub, Account Mappings holds one row per category, automatically mapped for the starting categories (General Internal to 6500 Vehicle Expenses; Warranty and Goodwill to the existing 6430 Warranty & Goodwill Expense; Comeback to 6440 Comeback Expense). The rollout migration and new-customer setup reuse suitable accounts, create missing required accounts, preserve valid existing mappings and resolve number conflicts with an available number, so all starting mappings are ready before the first job posts (S6-R2); a category the shop adds gets an empty row that holds the event until it is mapped; an archived category keeps its row. The shop supplies credit is unchanged (Story 10). At Close of a whole internal or no-charge work order the parts cost debits the category's account and the credit stays on today's inventory account by part category and stock location. On a billable work order with a no-charge line, that line's parts cost posts to the category's account when the invoice is created; the rest of the work order posts as today.

**Labor is hours only.** The Internal Work Orders report gives hours per job, and Technician Efficiency with its chip on Only internal gives hours per technician, for a manual labor entry. Posting labor cost at technician pay is a later option.

**Shop supplies are a flat amount.** An internal or no-charge work order carries one Shop supplies amount, defaulted from the customer's Min shop supplies charge and editable on the work order until Close. At Close it posts as one entry: debit the work order's expense account, credit the income account of the existing "Shop supplies" item (Products And Services) in QuickBooks, or the existing Shop supplies expense row in AccountingHub. No new mapping row for it. No percentage in this version.

**Reports: dollars out, hours in, per work order and per line.** Whole internal and no-charge work orders stay out of the money reports as before. A no-charge line on a billable work order is left out the same way by every number that sums revenue, cost, margin, invoiced hours or units per line (Story 9 names each report); its clocked hours stay in the time reports; the work order itself stays in with its billable amounts. The Internal Work Orders report lists whole work orders and single lines with classification, category, original work order and line, original technician, parts cost and hours, with a per-category summary strip and a By technician tab (completed lines, comeback lines, warranty lines, comeback rate, redo hours, redo parts cost).

**Not in this version.** Labor posting (the parts and labor split: an internal labor cost per technician, the accountant's reclass rule, a mapping row per category per kind in QuickBooks and the hub) and the technician comeback and warranty numbers in dollars. This version stores on every internal and no-charge work order and line the category, the original work order and line, the original technician, the hours and the parts cost, and shows counts, hours and rates in the By technician tab, so those later additions are rules and rows on top of the same fields.

**Out of Scope**

- A percentage shop supplies charge on internal and no-charge work orders. v1 is a flat amount (Story 10); a percentage, based on hours at the location labor rate as on customer jobs, is a later option.
- Billing a manufacturer or a third party for warranty work. The NO CHARGE document serves as proof of repair; a bill-to party is a separate feature.
- Monthly batch posting, backdating to the cost date, or a separate accounting approval screen. Closing posts immediately, and the existing Require Review Before Completion setting is the review step.
- Splitting cost by vehicle or class in QuickBooks, department profit and loss, and separate accounts for own trucks versus shop equipment. Theme #438 and a later row in the classification to account mapping.
- Sell prices, an internal pricing tier or an internal labor rate. These work orders carry cost and hours only. A shop that charges a sister company or a department keeps using a billable work order with a real invoice.
- Capitalizing rebuilds, use tax calculation, co-pay or partial warranty. Use tax on parts the shop consumes is handled in the accounting system on the vendor bill, as today.
- Cost per asset. v1 creates the data (one internal work order per asset with parts cost and hours, listed on the asset's Invoices tab) and does not build the per-asset cost report or add cost columns to that tab. v1.1, owned by the PM.
- Automatically re-marking past $0 invoices. An optional one-time tool may follow; the books are never touched.
- Moving labor cost at technician pay. A v1.1 option on the mapping screen.
- Internal work orders in the customer portal (an estimate to approve and the closed document, for a flagged sister company) and a copy of the NO CHARGE document there. v1.1 candidate.
- A filter on the customers list. v1 shows the badge; search finds the flagged customers.
- Part sales. A part sale to an internal customer is a regular billable part sale: no badge, no Convert action, no Close, no NO CHARGE stamp. Components shared with part sales change only for work orders.
- Closing several work orders at once. Each is closed on its own with its own posting date (S3-R2); a shop that parked Complete work orders closes them one by one.
- Regular work orders, invoicing, payments, discounts, credits and the customer portal behave exactly as they do today.
- Policy as a starting No Charge category. The shop can add it as its own category.
- Moving a line to a separate work order from the line menu. A shop that wants free work on its own work order splits the work order as today and converts the new one.
- Labor cost posting (the parts and labor split), technician comeback and warranty rates in dollars, and AI insights on comebacks: not in this version; the fields they need are stored from the start (section 2).

## 3. Jobs to be Done / Goals

> When I open a work order for one of our own trucks, I want it to be not billable from the start, so I can run it like any other job without zeroing every price by hand.

> When an internal job is done, I want to close it without creating an invoice, so I can get it off my open list and out of receivables.

> When a customer's truck comes back for something we got wrong and we are not charging them, I want to mark that work order as our cost, so I can eat it without it looking like a sale or a discount.

> When an internal job is closed, I want to see its parts cost land in our repairs expense account in the books, so I can stop re-posting it by hand at month end.

> When I look at sales and margin for the month, I want to see our own repairs and our free work left out, so I can see how the business actually did.

> When I review a technician, I want to see the hours they spent on shop trucks and comebacks counted as work, so I can judge them fairly.

> When I need to prove a repair was done on one of our units, I want to get a document for the job with parts and hours on it, so I can file a claim or show an auditor.

**Goals**

- An advisor can take an internal or no-charge job from creation to Closed without editing a single sell price.
- A closed internal or no-charge work order creates no receivable, no payment and no customer message.
- For a shop on QuickBooks Online or AccountingHub, the month-end reclass journal entry for internal and no-charge jobs is no longer needed.
- Sales and margin reports exclude internal and no-charge work orders; one report of their own shows them with cost, hours and category.
- Setup is one toggle, one flagged customer and the expense account mapping; everything else works with the defaults.

**Usage tracking.** The GA4 events for this feature (setting turned on, customer flagged, internal and no-charge work orders created, lines marked, Close, Reopen, report opened) are defined by the PM when the feature is on staging, from the built code, following the tracking-plan process of the GA4 epic SV-11561. They are not part of the engineering handoff; the PM creates the ticket when the feature reaches staging.

## 4. Key Decisions

- **Internal is a flag on the customer, not on the work order, not a work order type and not a new status.** Rationale: a work order is internal because of whose equipment it is, and the customer already says that. One flag on the customer makes every work order for the shop's own units internal with no extra click, keeps history and assets on one customer, and gives accounting one place to look. A new status would touch every list, filter, report and status recalculation in the product, and the Status field already carries three meanings. Rejected: a checkbox per work order for own-equipment work; a new Closed work order status; a new work order type. Basis: PM, 2026-10-19, after review with the accounting side; confirmed by accounting 2026-10-21. KB facts `f-status-field-three-jobs`, `f-estimate-wo-invoice-one-object`.
- **Goodwill, comebacks, policy and warranty work on a customer's unit are one No Charge mark on that customer's work order, with a category, set at creation or switched from the three-dots menu; not a discount and not a customer flag.** Rationale: a discount keeps the sale and the cost of goods sold on the books and hides what free work costs; moving the job to the internal customer loses the history and the asset. One mark that can be converted back until Close is the smallest control that covers "we did not know who pays". The actions sit in the work order menu next to Add Work Order Fee / Discount, where work order actions live, instead of a checkbox on the customer card. The category is captured when the mark is set and posts to its own account (Stories 5 and 6); a single line of a billable work order carries the same mark and category (Story 12). Rejected: handling it with a 100% discount or a credit only; a second customer flag; a checkbox on the customer card of the work order detail. Basis: PM, 2026-10-22; the category and the menu actions after the CEO reviews of 2026-10-26 and 2026-11-05.
- **Under the hood, Close creates an invoice marked internal or no charge. The screen says Closed · Internal or Closed · No Charge.** Rationale: invoice creation already does everything close needs: relabels the picks to the record and posts their cost to the books (parts leave stock when they are picked), makes the document, keeps the asset history, locks the work order, and can be reversed. Users asked to close without invoicing, and the label gives them that. The parts of invoice creation that mean "a sale happened" are skipped for these records: no payment, no customer transaction, no portal notification, no invoice webhook, no QuickBooks invoice. The internal invoice is neither Invoiced nor Paid: it is a third invoice state, Closed, with no customer transaction, no payment and no receivable. The work order itself stays in the Invoiced status underneath (no new work order status, as decided 2026-10-13), and every screen, list filter, report and the Open API show it as Closed. Everything the app does for an invoiced work order (the locks, the snapshot in the work order history, the search index, the Invoiced tab of Technician Efficiency) applies to a closed one; everything it does for a paid one (the paid log entry, the Paid filter, the paid webhook, the ledger line, the $0 payment a $0 invoice writes today) does not. Rejected: a separate close path that rebuilds all of that; showing the status as Invoiced; letting the record fall into Paid as a $0 invoice does today. Basis: PM, 2026-10-13; label Closed, PM (decided 2026-10-15); side effects listed after the codebase review 2026-10-22; the Closed invoice state decided by the PM 2026-10-27 after the fifth pass found that a $0 invoice is Paid today. Follows `d-label-names-the-action`.
- **Internal and no-charge work orders have no sell prices; cost is shown under the existing financial-data permission.** Rationale: nobody is billed, so a price can only be misread as value and leak into reports. Cost and hours are the data accounting and the owner want, and part cost is financial data today, so it stays behind 'See Financial Data'. Rejected: prices computed as today but not charged; an internal pricing tier; internal labor rate and parts margin settings; cost visible to everyone on internal work orders. Basis: PM, 2026-10-15, re-confirmed 2026-10-22.
- **Part cost goes straight to the expense account, not to cost of goods sold first, one entry per part as invoicing posts today.** Rationale: that is exactly the journal entry the accountant posts by hand today (debit the expense account, credit Inventory Asset); two entries where one is enough only adds noise to the books. The entry granularity and numbering follow the existing inventory journal entries so the accountant sees one familiar pattern. Vendor bill lines follow the same rule at bill time. Rejected: post to cost of goods sold at close and reclass afterwards; one summary entry per work order. Basis: PM, 2026-10-13; confirmed with accounting 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-21; granularity aligned to the code 2026-10-22.
- One expense account per category, mapped per location in QuickBooks (as every ShopView Accounts row is) and per organization in AccountingHub (as every Account Mappings row is), with the starting categories mapped automatically in the hub (S6-R2). Rationale: the category is the split the accountant wants on the profit and loss (own equipment, warranty, goodwill, comeback), and the mapping stays a category to account table, so a kind (parts, labor) can be added later as rows without changing the contract. The QuickBooks connection and every ShopView Accounts row are per location today, and the category rows follow. Rejected: one account for everything; two fixed accounts (internal, no charge) as in v0.17 and before; an account per part category; an organization-level mapping in QuickBooks, which does not exist. Basis: PM, 2026-10-22; one account per category with the CEO, 2026-11-05; the hub accounts and the migration with the AccountingHub owners, 2026-11-05.
- **Labor is hours only in this version.** Rationale: ShopView does no payroll and wages are already expensed through it. Most shops track internal hours and nothing else; moving labor cost at technician pay is absorbed-labor accounting that few shops of this size run. The Technician Efficiency report with the chip gives the hours for a manual entry. Rejected: always posting labor; posting labor at a labor rate. Basis: PM, 2026-10-15; confirmed by accounting 2026-10-21.
- **Close always succeeds (the one refusal is a no-charge work order whose category requires the original work order and has none, which the dialog of S3-R2 asks for). A missing account never blocks the advisor: QuickBooks posts to the ShopView Review account as it does for every other unmapped account today; AccountingHub holds the event with a Retry.** Rationale: the advisor is not the accountant, and the QuickBooks sync already has one rule for a missing mapping (post to the auto-created ShopView Review expense account and let the accountant reclass) and one for an account later deleted or deactivated in QuickBooks (the entry fails and shows in QB Unexported); a second rule for the category rows would surprise the accountant, and accounting asked for one rule for all rows. The hub has no fallback account, so there the event is held where the accountant already works and resends when the mapping is saved. Rejected: blocking Close until the account is mapped; holding in QB Unexported with a resend on mapping save; dropping the entry silently. Basis: PM, 2026-10-22; accounting confirmed hold on 2026-10-21; the QuickBooks rule was checked in the code 2026-10-27 (the review fallback is live for every account row and is not gated by the QuickBooks fallbacks toggle) and is with the hub owner for confirmation, as accounting asked. Follows `f-qb-sync-nonblocking-replayable`, `d-follow-incumbent-convention` and `d-never-force-keep-escape-hatch`.
- **The classification can change until the work order is closed or invoiced. From then on it is locked.** Rationale: "we did not know who pays" is handled by the Convert actions or a customer change, all allowed today until an invoice exists. Change Customer is not offered on an invoiced or paid billable work order today, and that stays (S2-R7). A closed internal or no-charge work order does not offer the change: it goes through Reopen, change, Close, because the internal invoice and the AccountingHub record keep the customer they were created with, and an internal work order moved to a regular customer after Close would come back from Reopen internal with no way to convert it (S2-R9). Locking the classification at Close or invoice is the one new lock; locking earlier, at Complete, would be another the product does not have. Rejected: Convert to internal / external actions on internal work orders (an internal work order becomes billable by changing its customer); locking the customer once a vendor bill exists; locking at Complete; editing the classification after Close. Basis: PM, 2026-10-19; the bill case decided with accounting 2026-10-21; lock point aligned to the code 2026-10-22; the customer change after invoicing aligned to the code 2026-11-06 (Change Customer is hidden on invoiced and paid work orders); the change blocked on closed internal and no-charge work orders by the PM 2026-10-28 after the tech plan review. Follows `d-never-force-keep-escape-hatch`.
- **The classification is written on the work order, never read live from the customer flag.** Rationale: if reports read the flag at query time, removing a flag would rewrite history and silently change open work orders; the product already shows that effect with the billable-technician flag. Writing Billable / Internal / No Charge on the work order when it is created, when its customer changes and when it is converted, and on the closed record, keeps history fixed. The user never sees this rule, only its effect: nothing changes under an open or closed job. Rejected: deriving the classification from the customer at read time. Basis: PM, 2026-10-22.
- **Flagging a customer offers to make its open billable work orders internal; removing the flag is free and leaves open internal work orders internal.** Rationale: on release day every shop that tracks internal work already has an "Internal" or "Shop Fleet" customer with open work orders, and those must become internal without a last round of $0 invoices; a sister company must be able to get a real invoice one day and be internal the next, without closing anything first. Because the classification is written on the work order, both directions are safe: a bulk make-internal applies the same rules as a customer change, and a removed flag changes nothing on open work orders. Rejected: open work orders silently becoming billable or internal on their next change; locking the flag while internal work orders are open (written earlier on 2026-10-22, replaced the same day); a Mark as billable action on internal work orders. Basis: PM, 2026-10-22.
- **The existing Require Review Before Completion setting is the accounting review step.** Rationale: the shop already has a review step and the accountant can own it; shops that turned review off get no extra step. Rejected: a separate accounting review and approve screen. Basis: PM, 2026-10-13; confirmed by accounting 2026-10-21. Follows `f-review-step-wo-level-optional`.
- **Closing posts immediately and takes one press, like Create invoice.** Rationale: immediate posting is right for the common case and keeps the books correct during the month; the header Create invoice completes and invoices in one press and with no confirmation today (SV-10312), so Close does the same; what the press will post is readable on the Finance tab before it. Rejected: monthly batch posting; backdating to the cost date; a preview step; a confirmation that lists the postings (the advisor knows the cost and the hours already, and the postings are on the document and in the books). Basis: PM, 2026-10-13; one press 2026-10-22; no confirmation 2026-11-12.
- **One report for free work; the money reports leave internal and no-charge work orders out with no filter.** Rationale: a filter on the Sales report invites mistakes and clutters the report that must stay the shop's revenue; a dedicated Internal Work Orders report gives the owner and the accountant one place with cost, hours, classification and category per job, and it is the "one place totalling free work" accounting asked for. Technician Efficiency keeps a chip because hours are work and "how much time went to our own trucks" belongs to that report. Rejected: a Hide / Only internal / Show all chip on Sales, Sales By Customer, Sales By Representative and Advisor Analysis; a separate non-customer-pay costs report with its own numbers model (the new report reads the same closed records). Basis: PM, 2026-10-26; Technician Efficiency chip at accounting's request 2026-10-21.
- **Shop supplies on internal and no-charge work orders are a flat amount, defaulted from the customer's Min shop supplies charge, posted at Close as a debit to the work order's expense account and a credit to the account the shop already maps for shop supplies: in QuickBooks the income account of the existing "Shop supplies" item (Products And Services), in AccountingHub the existing Shop supplies expense row.** Rationale: fleets want a consistent charge on their internal units; the percentage charge is based on the labor price, which these work orders do not have; the per-work-order amount and the customer Min already exist in the product, and both integrations already have a shop supplies mapping, so the feature adds no row for it. In QuickBooks the credit lands on the income account behind the Shop supplies item, the same account the shop supplies line of a billable invoice credits, so internal shop supplies are treated like the shop charging itself. Rejected: no shop supplies at all; a percentage of a notional labor price; a third QuickBooks row "Shop Supplies Expense" crediting a separate expense account (accounting's preference of 2026-10-22, dropped 2026-10-23 to keep QuickBooks at two new rows; the shop supplies amount is small and the accountant can reclass it). Basis: accounting's answer of 2026-10-22; PM (decided 2026-10-22; credit target decided 2026-10-23).
- **Built for QuickBooks Online and AccountingHub both, plus exports for shops with neither.** Rationale: QuickBooks shops are not pushed to the hub. The AccountingHub stories are written by the hub owner from this PRD once the stories are generated, and ship with the core release. Rejected: AccountingHub only. Basis: confirmed by leadership (decided 2026-10-13); hub process agreed 2026-10-19.

## 5. Terminology

- Internal work order → a work order whose customer carried the Internal customer flag when the work order was created or last changed customer. Not a work order type and not a status.
- No-charge work order → a work order on a regular customer marked No Charge at creation or converted from the menu, with a category from the shop's list (Warranty, Goodwill and Comeback to start). Same behaviour as an internal work order, different expense account, history stays on the customer.
- Internal customer → one of the shop's existing customers flagged as the shop's own company or a sister company. Set in the modal that opens the first time the setting is switched on, or at the bottom of Edit Customer; nowhere else. Several allowed. Not a system customer: ShopView creates nothing.
- Classification → the three values a work order carries: Billable, Internal, No Charge. Written on the work order; the list filter and the export column use these words.
- Close Work Order → the finish action on an internal or no-charge work order, labelled in Title Case like Create Work Order, everywhere it appears. It stands where Create invoice stands on a billable work order. Regular work orders are invoiced as today. Reopen Work Order is its reverse (Story 4). This PRD says "Close" and "Reopen" for the actions in running text.
- Closed · Internal / Closed · No Charge → how running text, the Finance tab, the success alert and the Reopen dialog name a closed internal or no-charge work order. On screen the status reads Closed and the one badge next to it reads Internal or No Charge, so the classification shows once. Underneath it is a work order with an internal invoice in the Closed state; the locks of an invoiced work order apply.
- Internal invoice → the record created by Close. It is a third invoice state, Closed, neither Invoiced nor Paid, with no customer transaction and no payment. It carries the work order number (as every invoice does today; no number is consumed or skipped), has nothing owed, and is never sent to the customer, the customer portal or an invoice webhook, nor listed in the Open API invoice lists; QuickBooks gets no invoice; AccountingHub receives it as a Closed record with no revenue (S8-R1). Its date is the posting date.
- No Charge → the text used where a price would be (the Total Price cell, the document stamp) and the name of the mark (a checkbox in New Work Order, Convert actions in the work order menu).
- Reason → the earlier name of the No Charge category (v0.17 and before). Superseded by Category.
- Hours → the labor hours on the lines of the work order, as the document shows them. The Finance tab posting summary, the NO CHARGE document and the Internal Work Orders report use them. Clocked hours per technician are in Technician Efficiency.
- Internal Work Orders → the name of the setting under Settings \> Work Orders and of the report under Reports; both cover internal and no-charge work orders, as does the Technician Efficiency chip "Internal work orders: Hide / Only internal / Show all" (tooltip "Internal and no-charge work orders", S9-R3). On a flagged customer's work order the badge reads Internal, never No Charge.
- Category expense account → the expense account mapped to a category per location: one ShopView Accounts row per category in QuickBooks (S5-R1), one Account Mappings row per category in AccountingHub (S6-R1). Replaces the two fixed rows Internal Work Orders Expense and Customer No-Charge Expense of v0.17.
- Shop supplies account → where the shop supplies amount of an internal or no-charge work order is credited: in QuickBooks the income account of the existing "Shop supplies" item (Products And Services) (mapped today for billable invoices; no new row), in AccountingHub the existing Shop supplies expense row.
- ShopView Review → the expense account the QuickBooks sync creates in the shop's QuickBooks and posts to whenever a mapped account is missing, deleted or inactive. Existing behaviour.
- Posting date → the date field on the Finance tab of an internal or no-charge work order (where a billable one has Invoice Date), default today; the date of the expense entry.
- Inventory part / special-order part / sublet → a part picked from stock; a part ordered from a vendor for this work order and received against it; work done by an outside vendor and billed to the shop. Special orders and sublets have vendor bills. Sublet is work bought from another vendor and entered as a special-order part; the product has no separate sublet line or part type, so every sublet rule in this PRD is the special-order rule.
- Statuses are named as the app names them: Estimate, Approved, In Progress, Review, Complete, Invoiced, Paid and Declined. This feature adds the label Closed, which only the internal invoice carries underneath (Key Decisions); Authorization is a line status, not a work order status.
- Vocabulary. This PRD uses the frontend vocabulary, location and customer, which map to workplace and company in the backend.
- Category → a shop-managed entry in one of two lists on Administration \> Service \> Internal Work Orders: Internal categories (where the cost of work on the shop's own equipment goes) and No Charge categories (why a customer's work order or line is free). Each carries a QuickBooks expense account per location and, on No Charge, whether it requires the original work order. Replaces "reason".
- Original work order → the earlier work order a Warranty or Comeback job comes from, or "Not in ShopView" when that work was done before ShopView or elsewhere. Optional in every dialog, required before Close or Create invoice on categories that require it.
- Original line → the line on the original work order the job comes from. Optional. It sets the original technicians (every technician assigned to it); without it, the technician assigned to the original work order (S2-R17).
- No Charge line → a line of a billable work order marked No Charge with a category. It shows $0.00 on the work order, the estimate, the portal and the invoice; its hours, parts and story stay; its parts cost posts to the category's account when the invoice is created. The work order carries no badge.

## 6. Assumptions

- A shop that already keeps a "Shop Fleet" or "Internal" customer keeps it and flags it; its assets and history stay where they are. No data migration.
- Internal and no-charge work orders carry no tax, fees or discounts, so the integrations are never asked to handle those for these jobs; shop supplies is one flat amount with its own entry (Story 10).
- A multi-location group turns the setting on once for the organization; each location maps its own QuickBooks row per category, as it maps every other row.
- Changing the customer or converting No Charge after a vendor bill has posted is rare; the warning and the reclass cover it.
- The user turning the feature on and flagging the company is an owner or office manager with the Settings and Customers permissions; the user closing a work order is the service advisor who invoices today.

### Story 13: Release day, seeding and migration

As the product team, I want the starting categories, the QuickBooks rows and the AccountingHub accounts and mappings in place for every organization before the release is visible, so that no shop turns the setting on and finds an empty category list or an unmapped row.

Design: Nothing to draw. The results are the screens of Story 1 (categories), Story 5 (QuickBooks rows) and Story 6 (hub rows).

Jira: [SV-12295](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-12295) (created 2026-11-06).

Form factor: Not applicable.

Prerequisites:

- The release build is deployed.
- The AccountingHub operator command of S6-R2 is ready to run; the Comeback account is 6440 Comeback Expense (confirmed 2026-11-09).

Requirements:

- **S13-R1:** A one-time migration creates the starting categories for every organization (S1-R13): General Internal as the default Internal category; Warranty, Goodwill and Comeback as No Charge categories, with Requires the original work order on for Warranty and Comeback. Nothing is created in QuickBooks and nothing is turned on: the Internal Work Orders setting stays off for every organization.
- **S13-R2:** The same migration creates one empty ShopView Accounts row per category at every location with a connected QuickBooks company (S5-R5); the rows carry no hint while the setting is off. A location connected later gets its rows when the connection is made, as other rows do.
- S13-R3: The AccountingHub setup of S6-R2 runs as an operator command, not a deploy migration: organization by organization in isolation, with a list of failures and a verify mode; it runs after the hub release and again at launch. For each organization it reuses suitable accounts (an active expense account whose name matches), creates missing ones (6430 on a legacy chart too, 6440 Comeback Expense), populates every starting-category mapping, preserves valid mappings and resolves number conflicts with a free number. New organizations get the same set from the seeder.
- **S13-R4:** Every step is safe to run again: a rerun creates no duplicate category, row, account or mapping and overwrites nothing a shop has changed. The steps run before the release is visible to users, in this order: categories, QuickBooks rows, hub accounts and mappings.
- **S13-R5:** Verification after the run, per organization: four categories, four QuickBooks rows per connected location, four hub mappings populated (Comeback included), the setting off. The rule-zero verify of S9-R14 runs the same day. If engineering ships the feature behind a deploy switch until launch day, the switch is removed with the launch and is not a product setting (Key Decisions, No feature flag).
- **S13-R6:** Shops that keep an "Internal" or "Shop Fleet" customer today are not touched: the migration flags no customer and reclassifies no work order (Assumptions); S1-R11 handles it when the shop flags the customer.

Edge Cases:

- **S13-E1:** A hub organization whose chart already uses the Comeback default number for an unrelated account gets Comeback Expense at a free number (S6-R2); the mapping is populated all the same.
- **S13-E2:** An organization created between the migration and the release gets the set from the seeder, not from the migration; a rerun of the migration finds it complete and changes nothing.

Negative Cases:

- **S13-N1:** A failure for one organization does not stop the run for the others; the failed organizations are listed by the command and rerun on their own, and the release is not visible until every organization passes S13-R5.
