> # Title and Description, Product Spec
> 
> ---
> 
> ## 1. Business Case
> 
> A work order line has two text fields. They are labeled "What are you doing?" and "Why are you doing it?", which are the questions we ask the person writing the line, not the names of the things they are writing. Inside the dialog that reads fine. Everywhere the values travel afterwards it does not.
> 
> Both of those questions are printed into the line text that reaches a shop's accounting records. A line arrives in QuickBooks reading "What are you doing?: Replace front brake pads;Why are you doing it?: Customer reports grinding;Tech Story: ". A bookkeeper sees our interview questions inside their books.
> 
> Two customers have asked for this in writing. NCCHD, 15 users, asked us to remove "internal/service-specific wording such as 'What are you doing?' and 'Why are you doing it?'" from line descriptions (SV-6862, still in the backlog). KMS Mechanics, 34 users, asked for the same and named Tech Story alongside the two questions, describing their invoices as legally binding documents with no room for informal wording (SV-6582).
> 
> Our own customer invoice never prints any of this. It prints the line name and its description, with no field names in front of them, and that is what the accounting record should receive.
> 
> > *Context note: SV-6582 was closed OBSOLETE against SV-7473. SV-7473 is a different defect, about a hidden statement memo carrying review notes, so that closure appears mis-linked and the customer's request was never delivered. SV-6862 is still open.*
> 
> ## 2. Feature Overview
> 
> **Core functionality**
> 
> - The two work order line fields are named **Title** and **Description** everywhere a person reads them.
> - The questions survive as placeholder text inside the two free-text boxes, so the guidance is still there for a writer who wants it and gone the moment they type.
> - The line text that reaches QuickBooks carries no field labels at all. It reads as the title, the description and the technician's narrative, joined by a spaced hyphen: "Replace tie rod - excessive play - Both outer ends worn, replaced and aligned". A segment that is absent takes its separator with it.
> - That text is built in **one place** and is used by both routes into QuickBooks: the live QuickBooks Online sync, and the downloadable Customer Invoice report. One change fixes both, and the two can never drift apart.
> - The historical invoice import (Administration → Invoices) renames its two columns to match, and still accepts the old column names, so no spreadsheet a shop already holds stops working. It is the only live import that carries these columns.
> - Nothing about the values changes: same fields, same storage, same customer documents.
> 
> **Out of scope**
> 
> - The customer-facing estimate, invoice and credit documents. They have never printed either question and are untouched.
> - Field names in the database and the API. This is a naming change on the surfaces a person reads, not a schema change.
> - Anything already in QuickBooks. Nothing is re-sent, re-exported or rewritten.
> - **Whether a technician's narrative belongs in a shop's accounting records at all.** It is carried today, it is carried after this change, and that question is not reopened here. What changes is only how it is joined and labeled, which is the same wording problem the two questions were. (@chris ruling, 2026-09-16, reversing the 2026-09-15 call that put the segment entirely out of scope.)
> - **Credit memos.** A credit memo builds its own line descriptions from the credited line items and from the credit event itself, so it never carried the two questions and nothing about it changes here.
> - Any other QuickBooks mapping work. SV-6862's broader field-mapping request stays where it is; only the wording half is answered here.
> 
> ## 3. Key Decisions
> 
> - **Title and Description, not a rewording of the questions.** The two fields hold a short name and a longer explanation. Those are the words the rest of the product already uses for that pair, including on the printed document, so the dialog now matches the document.
> - **The questions stay as placeholders.** They are good prompts; they are just not names. Keeping them inside the box preserves the coaching for a new writer and removes it from everywhere the value is read back.
> - **The line text carries no labels for any of the three values.** It reads "{Title} - {Description} - {Tech story}", for example "Replace tie rod - excessive play - Both outer ends worn, replaced and aligned". The goal is that a shop's accounting records read the way our own invoice reads, and our invoice prints all three with no field names in front of them. **Rejected:** keeping shortened labels, "Title: ...;Description: ...;Tech Story: ...". It is an improvement on the questions, and the Batch 1 branch first built it, but it still prints our field names into someone else's books, which is the actual complaint. That build never reached develop or a release.
> 
> - **The narrative carries no label either, and "Scope of work" was considered and rejected.** The two invoice views disagree about the label and agree about everything else. The legacy view prints the narrative as a plain paragraph under the description with no label. The modern view prints it under a "Scope of work" heading. Both print the title and the description unlabeled, both put the narrative last, and both drop it entirely when it is absent. Printing no label is the only choice that matches what a customer sees in either view. **Rejected:** "Scope of work", which only half our customers see, and "Tech Story", which is the one label a customer has objected to in writing (SV-6582).
> - **A spaced hyphen joins the segments, and appears only between two segments that are both present.** The separator is a space, a hyphen, a space. A line with no description and no narrative carries the title alone. A line with no narrative ends after the description, with no trailing separator and no trailing space. **Rejected:** stacking the three on separate lines to mirror the printed invoice exactly. QuickBooks Online does hold line breaks, but Intuit documents that a CSV import merges them back into one line, so the shape would not survive every route into the books.
> 
> - **Whether a line has a narrative at all is a shop setting, so its absence is normal rather than exceptional.** Administration, Work Order Settings, **Require Tech Story**: "Tech story will be a required field before a line can be completed on a work order." With it on, the completion gate refuses an empty value and every invoiced line carries one. With it off, a line is completed and invoiced without one, and the text simply ends after the description.
> 
> - **The "Tech story missing." placeholder is never sent.** The line dialog writes that literal sentence when nothing was typed. Both invoice views already refuse to show it to a customer, and the accounting records should not be the one place it appears. A line whose narrative is that placeholder is treated as having none.
> - **One builder serves both routes, and it stays that way.** The live sync and the downloadable report read the same built line text. Fixing one place fixes both, and it is why the two routes cannot disagree about what a line says. **Rejected:** changing the report only, which was the original scope and would have left every live-integration shop, the population who actually complained, unfixed.
> - **An over-length line is shortened until it fits, title first, and is never cut in the middle of a character.** The joined text is shortened from the end until it fits QuickBooks' limit, so the title always survives. The narrative is last and is by far the longest of the three, so it is what gets clipped, and title then description then narrative is the same order both invoice views print them in. **Rejected:** cutting a fixed number of characters. A character can cost up to four bytes, so four thousand characters can be four times that many bytes, and if the limit QuickBooks enforces is a storage limit the whole invoice is rejected. That is the SV-9921 failure this rule exists to prevent, so the fix must not reintroduce it. **Rejected:** cutting a fixed number of bytes with no regard for where characters begin and end, which is what the build does today and which can send a final character that is broken in half. **Rejected:** sending it and letting QuickBooks reject the payload. That is the current failure mode in SV-9921: the record sits in Unexported Items and the error does not name the field, so a line that synced yesterday fails today with no explanation.
> - **Nothing already in QuickBooks changes.** No backfill, no re-send, no re-export. The new text applies to invoices synced or exported from release day onward. A reversal produces a new invoice, so it carries the new text, which means an invoice and its reversal can read differently in the same books. Accepted deliberately.
> - **Renaming an import column never breaks an existing file.** The importer validates headers strictly, so a plain rename would have failed every spreadsheet a shop has saved. It now maps the old header onto the new one before it validates. A file may even mix the two spellings.
> 
> ## 4. Assumptions
> 
> - Import templates are kept and reused by shops for years, so header stability matters more than tidiness.
> - Nothing downstream parses this line text looking for the old question strings. The "Title:" and "Description:" labels were only ever on the Batch 1 branch and never reached a shop, so nothing downstream has seen them either.
> - Intuit documents 4,095 for a QuickBooks Online line description without saying whether that counts characters or storage. The product carries its own limit of 4,000, which is under 4,095 on either reading, so the rule in S3-R7 is safe whichever it turns out to be. Establishing which unit Intuit means is worth one test against the sandbox someday, so the limit is chosen on evidence rather than on caution. It does not block this change.
> - QuickBooks Online line descriptions are plain text. There is no bold, no italic and no markup of any kind, so a long joined line cannot be made easier to read by formatting it. Verified 2026-09-16 against Intuit's own guidance; the only formatting a description field accepts is a line break.
> - A work order line's description can be far longer than that limit: ShopView allows 65,535 characters, against a 255-character line name. In practice descriptions are short phrases, so the cut is an edge case rather than a routine one.

> **Design:** [Design canvas](https://claude.ai/artifact/Pz1Q6BfvreYFpQwVQZPTE6) **Jira:** [SV-10130](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10130)

> **Prerequisites:**
> 
> - User is adding or editing a line on a work order.
> 
> **Requirements:**
> 
> - **S1-R1:** The first field is labeled exactly "Title".
> - **S1-R2:** The second field is labeled exactly "Description".
> - **S1-R3:** The Title box carries the placeholder "What are you doing?" and the Description box carries "Why are you doing it?". Placeholder text shows while the box is empty and disappears as soon as there is a value.
> - **S1-R4:** The label does not change with the state of the field. It reads "Title" whether the canned-line picker is empty or filled, which replaces a rule that swapped the label between the question and "Title" depending on that state.
> - **S1-R5:** The canned-line picker at the top of the dialog is labeled "Title" and carries the placeholder "What are you doing?" in its typeahead box, the same as the free-text Title field. The dialog focuses this field on open, so the placeholder is visible the moment the dialog appears and clears as soon as the user types or picks a canned line. (Corrected 2026-09-10: the first build gave the picker no placeholder, so the prompt never appeared on a new line.)
> - **S1-R6:** Leaving Title empty blocks the save with the message "Title is a required field", replacing "Line Name is a required field". Every required-field message in the app uses the "X is a required field" form, and this one keeps it.
> - **S1-R7:** Nothing else about the dialog changes: same fields in the same order, same validation, same values saved.
> 
> **Negative cases:**
> 
> - **S1-N1:** No existing line is altered. The rename is on the labels, not on the data.

> ### Story 3: What Reaches QuickBooks
> 
> **As a** bookkeeper, **I want** our line text to read like an invoice line, **so that** our accounting records do not carry another product's field names.
> 
> **Design:** [Design canvas](https://claude.ai/artifact/Pz1Q6BfvreYFpQwVQZPTE6) **Jira:** [SV-10132](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10132)
> 
> **Prerequisites:**
> 
> - An invoice's line text is being produced, either by the live QuickBooks sync or by the downloadable Customer Invoice report.
> - The text is produced for an **invoice**. An estimate never reaches QuickBooks: the live sync runs against one invoice at a time, when that invoice is created.
> - Downloading the report requires **Invoicing & payments - View**. The gate is enforced on the server, not by hiding the entry in the Export Reports dialog.
> - The live sync needs no user permission of its own. It runs automatically when an invoice is created, for a shop whose QuickBooks connection is active.
> 
> **Requirements:**
> 
> - **S3-R1:** A line's text joins the title, the description and the technician's narrative with a spaced hyphen and no labels: "Replace tie rod - excessive play - Both outer ends worn, replaced and aligned".
> - **S3-R2:** The separator is exactly one space, one hyphen-minus, one space.
> - **S3-R3:** A separator appears only between two segments that are both present. A line with no description joins the title straight to the narrative. A line with no narrative ends after the description. A line with neither carries the title alone. In every case there is no trailing separator and no trailing space.
> - **S3-R4:** The same text is used by the live QuickBooks sync and by the downloadable Customer Invoice report. The two can never show different text for the same line.
> - **S3-R5:** The technician's narrative is the third and last segment. It carries no label of any kind, and no semicolon appears anywhere in the line text. The reasoning is in §2.
> - **S3-R6:** A line with no narrative ends after the description, with no trailing hyphen and no trailing space. A narrative is absent when the value is empty, or when it is the literal placeholder "Tech story missing." that the line dialog writes when nothing was typed; the two are treated identically, and neither reaches QuickBooks.
> - **S3-R6a:** A narrative is absent legitimately, not only exceptionally. The organization setting Administration, Work Order Settings, Require Tech Story governs whether the completion gate gets in the way, so a shop with it switched off invoices completed lines with no narrative as a matter of course.
> - **S3-R7:** When the joined text is longer than 4,000 bytes it is shortened from the end to fit, and it is never cut in the middle of a character. The live sync and the downloadable report are both cut at that same limit, so S3-R4 holds on a long line too. Today only the live sync cuts, and it cuts by bytes without regard for where a character ends; the report has no cut at all. Because the title leads and the narrative trails, the title always survives and the narrative is what gets clipped first.
> - **S3-R7a:** The limit is a storage limit rather than a count of what a reader sees, so a line written with accented or non-Latin characters may be shortened more than an English line that looks the same length on screen. Two lines that look equally long can therefore be cut at different points. That is correct behavior and not a defect.
> - **S3-R8:** The values themselves are unchanged. Nothing is trimmed, truncated, re-cased or escaped that is not trimmed, truncated, re-cased or escaped today, other than the shortening described in S3-R7.
> - **S3-R9:** A title, a description or a narrative that itself contains a hyphen, including a spaced hyphen, is carried as written. The separator is not escaped and the text is not quoted, so a reader cannot always tell our separator from theirs. This is accepted deliberately: the alternative is printing a field name to mark the join, which is the thing being removed.
> - **S3-R9a:** A semicolon typed inside any of the three values is ordinary punctuation and is carried through untouched. It was ambiguous only while the semicolon was the separator.
> - **S3-R10:** For a user who cannot see financial data, the downloaded report masks its money columns (rate, amount, tax and average cost) as it does today. The line text is not a money value and is not masked, so the same words reach every reader of the file.
> - **S3-R11:** No other report or export reads this line text. Changing it changes these two routes and nothing else.
> - **S3-R12:** An imported historical invoice never reaches the live QuickBooks sync, so its line text is unaffected whatever the import wrote into it.
> 
> **Negative cases:**
> 
> - **S3-N1:** Invoices already in QuickBooks keep the text they were sent with. Nothing is re-synced, re-exported or rewritten.
> - **S3-N2:** No line falls back to the old labels or the old questions, including lines created before this change.
> - **S3-N3:** A parts line, a shop supplies line and a fee or discount line are unaffected: none of them carries a title-and-description pair.
> 
> **Edge cases:**
> 
> - **S3-E1:** A line with a title, no description and a narrative reads "Replace tie rod - Both outer ends worn, replaced and aligned": one separator, not two, and no gap where the description would have been.
> - **S3-E2:** An invoice that is reversed and re-invoiced after release day carries the new text on the new invoice, while the original keeps the old text. A bookkeeper can therefore see both forms in one set of books, on one job.
> - **S3-E3:** A description long enough to be cut still syncs. The invoice reaches QuickBooks; it does not land in Unexported Items.
> 
> > *Context note: the Batch 1 branch first built this change as "Title: ...;Description: ...". It never reached develop or a release, so the released product and develop both still send "What are you doing?: ...;Why are you doing it?: ...;Tech Story: ...", and this change goes straight from that to S3-R1. There is no intermediate format to migrate from. The complaint being answered is not which field names print, it is that any of our field names print at all. That is also why the narrative lost its "Tech Story:" label on 2026-09-16: the label was the same defect, sitting in the same field.*
> 
> > *Context note on S3-R7: SV-9921 is the live example of the alternative. A value too long for a QuickBooks field put a customer's record in Unexported Items with a raw validation message that does not say which field failed.*

> ### Story 4: The Imports
> 
> **As a** shop importing history, **I want** the column names to match the product, **so that** the template makes sense, **and I want** my existing spreadsheet to keep working.
> 
> **Design:** [Design canvas](https://claude.ai/artifact/Pz1Q6BfvreYFpQwVQZPTE6) **Jira:** [SV-10133](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10133)
> 
> **Requirements:**
> 
> - **S4-R1:** **Withdrawn.** There is no separate work order import. The only live import carrying these columns is Administration → Invoices, which writes to the imported work order history and is covered by S4-R2. The column names this requirement renamed exist only in code nothing calls, and nothing ships for them.
> - **S4-R2:** The historical invoice import's two columns (Administration → Invoices) are named "\\\*Line Title" and "Line Description", replacing "\\\*Line Title - What are you doing" and "Line Description - Why are you doing it".

> - **S4-R4:** A file may mix the two spellings, carrying one column under its old name and the other under its new one.
> - **S4-R5:** The old name is accepted as the column it became: it is neither reported missing nor reported as a column to remove.
> - **S4-R6:** The downloadable sample file and the on-screen column list both show the new names.
> - **S4-R7:** Every other column name, and the required order of columns, is unchanged.
> 
> **Negative cases:**
> 
> - **S4-N1:** A genuinely unrecognized column is still rejected. The compatibility rule covers exactly the two renamed columns and nothing else.
> 
> > *Context note: the importer compares headers strictly, so a rename without S4-R3 would have failed every spreadsheet a shop had saved from the old template.*

> ## Form Factor
> 
> The work order line dialog is a core work-order surface and is used on a phone by technicians, so it carries a mobile variant already. This spec changes two labels and two placeholders inside it and nothing else: the mobile variant shows the same words, in the same fields, with no separate design work owed. Every other surface here is either a downloaded file or a background sync, neither of which has a form factor.

> ## 7. Open Questions
> 
> Nothing here blocks build. Both questions that did block were answered on 2026-09-15 and are recorded in §2 and §3.
> 
> - Who downloads the Customer Invoice report, and why? Searching Jira and Slack found no customer using it to feed QuickBooks Desktop, and every complaint on record about this line text is about the live sync. There is no telemetry to settle it: opening the Export Reports dialog fires a navigation event, but nothing records which report was pulled. This does not block the change, because both routes share one builder and are fixed together. **Owner:** the owning PM, with customer support. **Blocks:** nothing.
> 
> **Answered and closed**
> 
> - \*Should the export carry field labels at all?\* No. Answered 2026-09-15, see §3 and S3-R1.
> - \*Does the fix reach the live QuickBooks sync as well as the downloadable report?\* Yes, and it always did, because one builder feeds both. Answered 2026-09-15, see S3-R4.
> - \*Should the Tech Story segment change too?\* Yes, after all. Answered 2026-09-16, reversing the 2026-09-15 answer: it loses its label and its semicolon and becomes the third hyphen-joined segment (S3-R5). The question of whether a narrative belongs in a shop's books at all remains out of scope, see §2.
> - \*What happens to a semicolon typed inside a description?\* Nothing, it is carried through untouched. The question existed only while the semicolon was the separator. Closed 2026-09-16 by S3-R9a.
> - \*Does QuickBooks support bold or italic text, so a long joined line could be made easier to read?\* No. Line descriptions are plain text, with no bold, no italic and no markup of any kind; the only formatting the field accepts is a line break. Verified 2026-09-16 against Intuit's own guidance.
> - \*Does the release note cover one change or two?\* One note, covering both routes, since one change moves both. A shop on the live sync sees its QuickBooks line text change shape, and a shop that downloads the report sees the same text change there.
