The title leads the joined string, so whatever survives always begins with it. A shop that writes essays loses the tail of the essay, never the name of the job.
The order matters more now that the narrative is the third segment. It is the longest of the three by a wide margin, and it sits last, so it is what gets clipped. Title, then description, then narrative is also the order both invoice views print them in, so a cut takes the least important thing first in every case.
Answered 2026-09-16 · now S3-R7 and S3-R7a
Shorten until it fits, and never split a character
What the build does today
…biellette de direction côté passa▾[half of é]
What the rule asks for
…biellette de direction côté passa
An accented character costs two bytes, so a cut at the 4,000th byte can land inside one and send a character broken in half. The rule now shortens from the end until the text fits and stops on a character boundary, so that cannot happen. GL Mécanique, the customer already sitting in SV-9921 with a rejected QuickBooks payload, is exactly the shop this reaches first.
Cutting a fixed number of characters was considered and rejected. A character can cost up to four bytes, so four thousand characters can be four times that many bytes, and if QuickBooks' limit is a storage limit the whole invoice is rejected. That is the SV-9921 failure this rule exists to prevent, so the fix must not reintroduce it.
