Extreme cases
What the joined line reads when the inputs are awkward. Three segments, one separator, and a separator only ever appears between two things that are both present.
S3-R3 · no description
Title
Replace tie rod
Description
(empty)
Tech story
Both outer ends worn, replaced and aligned
Line reads
Replace tie rod - Both outer ends worn, replaced and aligned
One separator, not two. The empty description leaves no gap and no double hyphen behind it.
S3-R9 · the title already contains a hyphen
Title
Replace tie rod - passenger side
Description
excessive play - customer waiting
Tech story
Torqued to 74 ft-lb, alignment set
Line reads
Replace tie rod - passenger side - excessive play - customer waiting - Torqued to 74 ft-lb, alignment set
Carried as written. Nothing is escaped or quoted, so a reader cannot tell our separator from theirs. This is the cost of the choice and it is accepted deliberately: the alternative is printing a field name to mark the join, which is the thing two customers asked us to stop doing.
The four flavours of tech story that reach the books
Written at the completion gate · Require Tech Story ON
Replace tie rod - excessive play - Both outer ends worn, replaced and aligned
Require Tech Story OFF · the shop never wrote one
Replace tie rod - excessive play
The “Tech story missing.” placeholder · suppressed, as both invoice views suppress it
Replace tie rod - excessive play
Imported from a previous system · may be empty
Replace tie rod - excessive play
Three of these four end after the description, and none of them leaves a trailing hyphen. The placeholder case is the one worth arguing about: “Tech story missing.” is written by the line dialog when nothing was typed, and both invoice views already refuse to print it to a customer. The books should not be the one place it shows up.
Closed by this change · a semicolon typed inside a description
Title
Replace tie rod
Description
excessive play; also checked the drag link
Tech story
Drag link within spec
Line reads
Replace tie rod - excessive play; also checked the drag link - Drag link within spec
This was an open question while the semicolon was the separator: a typed one was indistinguishable from a new segment, and anything splitting on it broke. With the semicolon gone, a typed semicolon is ordinary punctuation and carries through untouched. The question does not need answering any more.
