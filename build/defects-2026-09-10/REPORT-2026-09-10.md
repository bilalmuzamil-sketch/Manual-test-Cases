left half-checked and nothing is sitting untried.

## 2 · The faults, in plain words

| # | What a user would see | How bad |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A declined job can still be changed.** On a job that has been declined, the buttons for adding a part and for changing an existing part are all still there, and they work — a part added to a declined job really is saved. On a paid job and on a completed job they are correctly gone. | The worst of the four. Six of the failing checks trace back to this one behaviour. |
| 2 | **Typing a part with no price puts it on the job at nothing.** If the part chosen has no price recorded against it, the cost and price boxes fill in with 0.00 instead of staying empty, and it saves at 0.00 without anyone being asked. | Money. This is what the customer ends up being charged from. |
| 3 | **Typing letters into the cost box gives the wrong message.** It says "enter a cost and sell price" — as though the box were empty — instead of saying the value is not a number. Typing a negative number is handled correctly. | Small. Confusing, not harmful. |
| 4 | **The printed page has no line between one job and the next.** The blank band to write notes in is there and is right. The heavy rule that should sit above it is missing — and oddly, the fainter lines *inside* a block did ship. | Small, but it is the one thing this whole printing feature was asked for: room to write, clearly divided. |

Each of these is written up in full, with the exact wording from the written requirements quoted
beside what the build actually does, and with the steps anyone can follow to see it. I have not raised
any of them. Say the word on one and I will raise that one and stop until you have seen it.

## 3 · What needs a decision from you or the product owner — not a fix

| # | The situation | What I need |
|---|---|---|
| A | **The printing requirements contradict themselves about a job with no work lines.** The summary of decisions at the top says Print should be greyed out when a job has no lines. Two of the numbered requirements further down describe exactly what such a job should print. The build does the second — it prints, and says "No lines on this work order" with zero totals. | Which one is right? If Print should be greyed out, that becomes a fault to fix and two checks are retired. If printing is correct, I correct the wording of one check and it passes as it stands. Either way nothing needs building until you say. |
| B | **A job cannot exist with no customer, and cannot exist with no vehicle.** Two checks describe what should print in those cases. The product refuses to create such a job at all — it says the customer and the vehicle are required. | Should the product allow it, or should those two checks be retired? |
| C | **There is no "Cancelled" state for a job line.** A line offers only Authorization required, Declined, Authorized and Complete. One check describes what a cancelled line should print. | Should a Cancelled state be added, or does that requirement mean Declined and the check should be reworded? |

**If you say nothing:** the four faults stay unraised and nothing reaches the developers. The three
questions above stay open and four checks stay unresolved, but nothing else is held up by them — both
features are otherwise fully run and the results are already written down where the testers read them.

## 4 · Things I had got wrong, and have corrected

I want you to see these rather than find them later.

- I had written that a job with no work lines cannot be printed at all, and had parked two checks as
  impossible on that basis. It prints perfectly well. Both checks are now done and both are good.
- I had a check marked as good that had only been half-checked. It is now correctly marked as needing
  the decision in row A above.
- I twice thought I had found a fault when the fault was in how I was looking — once measuring the
  printed page the wrong way, once by signing myself out of the site and reading that as being refused
  access. Both were caught before they reached you, and both checks came out good in the end.
