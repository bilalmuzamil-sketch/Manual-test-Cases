2. The problem

When an invoice is emailed to a customer from the customer portal, the email names the business as Bravo Mechanical Services — in the subject, in the first line of the body, in the contact line, and in the footer.

The invoice attached to that same email, and the invoice on screen, name the business as Staging Heavy Duty - 9919.

Both names belong to this one account. "Bravo Mechanical Services" is the account's Company Name (Settings). "Staging Heavy Duty - 9919" is one of its Locations, and it is the location the work was done at. So the email and the document the customer opens give two different business names for the same invoice.

3. Steps to reproduce

  1. Open the customer portal for this account: https://staging.portal.shopview.com/invoices
  2. Open invoice S-32981.
  3. Click the mail icon at the top right and send the invoice to an address you can read.
  4. Open the email, and open the PDF attached to it.
  5. Compare the business name in the email with the business name printed on the PDF.

To see where each name is set:

  • Settings → Company Name reads "Bravo Mechanical Services".
  • Settings → Locations holds "Staging Heavy Duty - 9919", which is what the invoice prints.

5. Current behaviour

  • The email subject reads: "Invoice #S-32981 - Bravo Mechanical Services"
  • The email body opens: "Bravo Mechanical Services has shared an invoice with you."
  • Lower in the body: "If you have any questions about this invoice, please don't hesitate to contact Bravo Mechanical Services directly."
  • The email footer reads: "Sent by ShopView on behalf of Bravo Mechanical Services"
  • The attached invoice prints "Staging Heavy Duty - 9919" as the letterhead and "Staging Lethbridge - 4310" as the remit-to address.
  • The attachment file name is "Invoice-S-32981.pdf", which is correct.

6. Expected behaviour

  • A customer opening the email and the invoice should see one business named, not two.
  • The name in the email should match the business named on the invoice the customer opens.

Which of the two names is the right one to show a customer is a product decision that has not been written down anywhere (see Sources). Whatever is chosen, the email and the invoice need to agree.

7. Sources

(a) Correction to the "Note for dev" this ticket was raised with. The original note read, verbatim:

Worth confirming whether that string is hardcoded or read from another organisation's record. If the latter, it's a cross-tenant data issue.

It is neither. It was checked on 12 September 2026 on the build above, and the name is this account's own:

  • The portal page for invoice S-32981 is served with an account record whose id is d55bc308-e61a-438d-b5f1-c7a73c89d49f and whose name is "Bravo Mechanical Services".
  • The invoice's own organisation id on that same page is the same d55bc308-e61a-438d-b5f1-c7a73c89d49f.
  • In the shop app, Settings for this account shows Company Name = "Bravo Mechanical Services", with the Tax ID "GST# 812694966 RT0001" — the same tax number that prints on invoice S-32981 (screenshots 2 and 3 above).

So there is no other tenant involved and nothing is hardcoded. The email is reading the account's Company Name; the invoice document is printing the Location. That is the whole of the difference, and it means this is a wording/consistency defect rather than a data-isolation one.

(b) What the written requirements say about it: nothing. The Invoice Design Selection specification — Confluence page 845447188, "Invoice Design Selection — Product Spec", version 27, last edited 2026-09-11, read on 12 September 2026 — was searched for "company name", "organisation name", "email subject", "on behalf of", "sender", "location name" and "shop name". It contains no requirement on which business name a customer-facing email or document carries; it governs how the documents look. The choice therefore needs a product answer, not just a code fix.
