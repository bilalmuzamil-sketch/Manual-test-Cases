## 6. Is the critic right? — the honest answer, both halves

Stefan Mitrovic's claim has two halves, and Rule 28 requires answering both.

**Half 1 — "more than 70% useless test cases".** **Not right about this pass, and the numbers are
the proof, not the rhetoric.** Of the 10 cases created or changed here, **10 are KEEP and 0 are
WEAK-KEEP or CUT.** More to the point, this pass **removed** more than it added: **9 cases left
the suite** (scope, not weakness) against **1 added**, taking Filters from **118 → 110 active**,
on top of the **27** the 2026-07-31 audit had already cut. **145 → 110 is a 24% reduction in ten
days, all of it recommended by our own audit rather than a reviewer.** And the one case added is
precisely the kind a slop-generator never writes: an inverse/regression guard (*did the redesign
silently drop a filter shops use today?*) that no other case in the suite covered.

**Half 2 — "some tests just do not make sense".** **He is right in principle, and this pass proves
the gate catches it.** Three of the ten cases (**30%**) failed the cold read on the same fail
condition — *the expected result asserted behaviour the steps never drove* — and, worse, the
**plan's own per-item pre-check had scored all three SENSIBLE.** The suite-wide Stage-2b gate is
what caught them. All three were repaired inside the pass (§3), so **0 nonsense and 0 unrepaired
FIX-WORDING ship**. The honest lesson is that a per-case pre-check is **not** a substitute for the
cold read plus the suite-wide sweep — which is exactly why Rule 28 makes 2b mandatory and
un-skippable.

**Where he would have a fair point, stated without spin:** these 10 cases are **desk-verified
only**. Filters has no QA branch, so nothing here has been run against a live build (Rule 22) —
every one stays `VIU-Pending`. A case that reads perfectly and has never been executed is not yet
proven useful. That is an environment gap, not a wording gap, and it is the top open item on the
project.
