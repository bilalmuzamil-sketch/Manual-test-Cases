Each story opens with its Jira ticket, its user story and what must be true before it applies. The prose between the rules says only what a rule cannot: what opens what, where a thing sits, and a reason where the reason changes what gets built.

Shared pieces such as the Mark complete modal, the enrolment modal and the certificate form are built once; the list is under [Reusable components](https://shopview.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/PM/pages/833290250/Maintenance+Reminders+V1#Reusable-components) on the main page.

# Part 1. Settings: maintenance schedules

Where a shop defines what maintenance means. Nothing in the feature exists until a schedule does. Delivery phase P1.

## S1: Create a maintenance schedule

**Jira: **[SV-10558](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10558)

**Phase: **P1

**Design:** [Maintenance Reminders design](https://claude.ai/design/p/4411588b-b915-46d5-a8b8-43628a02e02a)

As an admin, or anyone holding Settings Service, I want to create a maintenance schedule from scratch, so that I can define the services a class of asset receives.

**Prerequisites**

- The user has Settings Service enabled

### Where it lives

Settings, Maintenance, beneath Inspection Templates. Schedules belong to the whole organization; each keeps its canned lines at its home location, the location it was built at, per S1-R1.

- **S1-R1:** Settings will carry a Maintenance entry with a single list of maintenance schedules, shared by the whole organization: every location sees every schedule and can enrol onto it. Each schedule keeps its canned lines at its home location, the location it was built at, because canned lines are kept per location. There is no reminder settings tab, because the email carries no shop-editable content
- **S1-N2: **A user without Settings Service cannot reach the tab

### Nothing there yet

The first screen every shop sees has one thing to press.

- **S1-N1: **A schedule with no services cannot be saved. It shows an empty state prompting the first service

### New schedule

New schedule goes straight to an empty editor. There is no modal and no template to choose; the schedule exists from its first Save.

- **S1-R3: **New schedule opens an empty editor titled Untitled schedule. There is no modal and no template
- **S1-R4: **A schedule exists from its first Save. Leaving the editor before that leaves nothing behind

### The editor

A name at the top and the services beneath it. Reminder timing lives on each service, in S5.

- **S1-R6: **The editor will carry only a name and an ordered list of services
- **S1-R7: **The title will read Untitled schedule with inline edit, following the DVI pattern
- **S1-R8: **The editor will carry Cancel and Save. Save is disabled until the schedule has at least one service. The name, the services and their order are saved only when Save is pressed; Cancel discards every unsaved change, asking first when there are any
- **S1-N3: **A schedule cannot be saved with a blank name, and an inline rename cannot be left blank. Both revert to the previous name rather than erroring
- **S1-E2:** Two schedules cannot have the same name, as with inspection templates. A name already in use is refused inline: A schedule with this name already exists. A duplicate is named with (Copy), then (Copy 2), per S6-R12

### The service table

- **S1-R9:** Services are listed as a table: Service, Interval and Canned Lines. The interval renders each trigger separated by a dot, never by or, for example 15,000 mileage · 3 months, or 30 days, and the canned-lines cell carries the line count and how many came from covered services, for example 4 lines · 3 covered
- **S1-R10: **Services will be reorderable by drag and by Move up / Move down

### The list, once schedules exist

Nothing is ever deleted. A schedule made by mistake is archived like any other, and an archived schedule can still be opened and read before it is restored.

- **S1-R2: **The schedule list has two tabs, **Active** and **Archived**, each with a count. Its columns are Schedule, Services and Assets, and each sorts from its header; the default is Schedule, A to Z. A search above the table matches schedule and service names. An active schedule's menu offers Edit, Duplicate and Archive; an archived schedule's offers Restore. Clicking an archived schedule opens it read-only, per S1-R13
- **S1-R12: **Each schedule shows how many assets are enrolled on it as plain read-only text, as the inspection templates list does, never as a badge. The count opens nothing; a list of enrolled assets is out of this release
- **S1-R13: **An archived schedule opens read-only all the way down, its services, triggers and lines, so a shop can find what it archived and see it before restoring it
- **S1-R14:** For an organization with more than one workplace, the schedule list and the editor header show each schedule's home location beneath its name, for example Lines from Calgary South. With one workplace it is not shown
- **S1-E1: **A schedule created by mistake is archived, like any other. There is no delete anywhere in the feature and no draft state to abandon, so a schedule that was never wanted sits archived rather than disappearing

## S2: Add a routine service to a schedule

**Jira: **[SV-10559](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10559)

**Phase: **P1

**Design:** [Maintenance Reminders design](https://claude.ai/design/p/4411588b-b915-46d5-a8b8-43628a02e02a)

As an admin, or anyone holding Settings Service, I want to define what triggers a service and how often, so that the system knows when each asset is due.

**Prerequisites**

- A schedule exists

### Add service

- **S2-R1: **Add service opens a blank service form

### The form, in order

- **S2-R2:** The form sequence will be: name, is this compliance, triggers and intervals, services also covered (when shown, per S2-R18), canned lines, reminder timing. There is no send switch anywhere in the form or in the service table
- **S2-R3: **A service will carry exactly one name. There is no customer facing name, so the name a shop gives a service is the name its customer reads in a reminder
- **S2-N4: **A service cannot be saved with a blank name

### Triggers and intervals

The calendar is always there, because four units in five have no usable meter history and would otherwise never come due. Mileage and engine hours are added to it, never used instead of it.

- **S2-R4: **The calendar interval will always be present, always editable, and required. It shows as a ticked checkbox that cannot be unticked, with a hover explaining that every service carries a calendar interval
- **S2-R5: **Distance and engine hours will be optional additional triggers, each revealing its own interval row when selected
- **S2-R6: **Each interval row has an operator, "**Every**" or "**At**". "**Every**" repeats and counts again from each completion. "**At**" is a fixed point: on the calendar it is a day of the year and falls on that day each year, which is how seasonal work such as winterizing is set; on a meter it is one reading
- **S2-R7: **On the calendar row, choosing "At" turns the value into a (calendar) day-and-month picker, for example 15 Nov. "**At**" is never a month on its own
- **S2-R8: **The calendar interval carries a number and a unit, days or months, both chosen from a list rather than typed. Months run 1 to 12 and days are whole numbers. Either rebases on completion per S2-R6
- **S2-R10: **There is one distance unit and its name is the word **mileage**, written in full wherever a unit is named. mi and km never appear: the single unit covers both, so an abbreviation would claim one of them
- **S2-R15: **Engine hours are written **hours** in full wherever the unit is named, never hrs, on the same rule as mileage in S2-R10
- **S2-N1: **A service cannot be saved with the calendar interval empty
- **S2-N3: **Selecting no additional trigger is valid. Calendar alone is a complete service
- **S2-E1: **Distance and engine hours selected together still produce one calendar row, not two

### Services also covered

The shop says what a larger service includes, for example PM-C covers PM-A and PM-B. Nothing is guessed from shared canned lines.

- **S2-R16: **A service can name the other services on its schedule that it also covers, in a step before canned lines: a multi-select of the schedule's other services. PM-C covering PM-A and PM-B means that doing PM-C does both
- **S2-R17:** The covered services' canned lines arrive in the canned lines step pre-filled and editable, each marked with the service it came from, for example from PM-A. Changing or removing them here changes this service only, never the one they came from. For a user without access to the home location they show pre-filled and read only, like the rest of the lines, per S4-R9
- **S2-R18:** Services also covered shows only when the schedule already has at least one other routine service. On the first service, and on every compliance service, the step is not shown and the steps after it move up by one
- **S2-R19: **The step carries one line of guidance: build the smallest service first. A service can only name services that already exist on the schedule
- 
    - 
- **S2-N8:** A compliance service is never offered in the picker and cannot name services of its own. It covers nothing and is covered by nothing, per S12-R13
- **S2-N9:** A service cannot cover a service that already covers it, directly or through another; the list does not offer it. Covering is not inherited: a service names every service it covers. In the canned-lines count, N covered counts the lines that came from covered services
- **S2-E6: **A service may cover nothing, and most will. Covering is never inferred from shared canned lines or from interval length; a shop states it

### What the number fields accept

- **S2-R9: **Interval values are whole numbers, minimum 1, with no decimals and no negatives. Mileage is capped at 999,999, engine hours at 99,999 and days at 999. An interval longer than 12 months is set in days
- **S2-N2: **A decimal interval value is rejected inline
- **S2-N5: **A number field accepts digits only. Letters and symbols do not enter the field, so there is no error to show
- **S2-N6: **An interval of zero is rejected inline, on the same rule as a decimal and a negative
- **S2-N7: **A calendar "At" row cannot be saved without a day and a month

### What the form tells you back

- **S2-R11: **The service comes due at whichever trigger arrives first. The triggers block states this once, in one plain line beneath its heading. There is no or between the triggers and no warning panel

### How the next date is worked out

Days and months are not two spellings of the same thing. Days drift through the calendar; months land on the same day each time.

- **S2-R14: **A months interval resolves to the same day of a later month, counted from the last completion, and produces a specific date rather than a month. A days interval counts days and drifts relative to the calendar, which is what a shop choosing days is asking for
- **S2-E5: **Where the anchor day does not exist in the target month, the date falls on that month's last day. A service completed on 31 January and set to one month comes due 28 February, and the following cycle counts from that date
- **S2-E2:** A service needed at two fixed points in the year is set up as two services, each with its own At
- **S2-E3: **There is no one-time flag. A meter "**At**" row covers the one-off case

## S3: Add a compliance inspection service

**Jira: **[SV-10560](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10560)

**Phase: **P1

**Design:** [Maintenance Reminders design](https://claude.ai/design/p/4411588b-b915-46d5-a8b8-43628a02e02a)

As an admin, or anyone holding Settings Service, I want to mark a service as a compliance inspection, so that it is tracked against a certificate rather than a meter.

**Prerequisites**

- A schedule exists

### The switch

Asked before the triggers, because the answer replaces them.

- **S3-R1: **The service form will ask Is this a compliance inspection? before the trigger, because it changes the trigger
- **S3-R2: **Turning it on will replace the trigger block with the compliance block. The two never appear together
- **S3-N1: **A compliance service has no meter triggers and no calendar interval

### Type

Type is whatever the shop calls it. The examples in the hover help a shop that is unsure; nothing is chosen for it.

- **S3-R3: **Type is free text and required. An (i) beside it shows examples: Annual safety, Annual state, Annual federal, Emissions test

### Term and Remind before expiry

A compliance service warns in months rather than days, because a lapsed certificate takes a truck off the road.

- **S3-R6: **The term is chosen on its own. The type fills in nothing
- **S3-R7: **Term will be in months, from 1 to 60, selected from a list, and is required. A compliance service without a term cannot produce a due date
- **S3-R8: **Remind before expiry sets how long before a certificate expires the service starts to read due soon. It is in months, defaults to 1 month for a term up to 12 months and 2 months above that, and can be changed. It cannot be longer than the term; a longer value is refused inline: Remind before expiry cannot be longer than the 6-month term
- **S3-E2: **A bus and a tractor may carry the same type with different terms, 6 and 12 months

### What it does not ask for

Dates belong to the certificate on the asset, in S8, not to the service in Settings.

- **S3-R9: **No certificate date will appear on this form. Certificate dates belong to the record on the asset

### How it behaves afterwards

- **S3-R10: **A compliance service will be orange, never red
- **S3-N2: **A compliance service with no matching record on the asset cannot come due, reads No record, and is excluded from every count

## S4: Attach canned lines to a service

**Jira: **[SV-10561](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10561)

**Phase: **P1

**Design:** [Maintenance Reminders design](https://claude.ai/design/p/4411588b-b915-46d5-a8b8-43628a02e02a)

As an admin, or anyone holding Settings Service, I want to attach the lines a service performs, so that adding it to a work order brings its work with it.

**Prerequisites**

- A schedule is open in the editor
- The shop has canned lines defined

### The picker

It opens over the service form and never leaves it. It shows hours, not prices.

- **S4-R4: **The empty state will read No lines on this service yet on one line, with the button beside it
- **S4-R1: **Add canned lines will open a picker with search
- **S4-R2: **The picker will show hours per line, and no price
- **S4-R3: **Selected lines will be ordered and reorderable by drag
- **S4-E3: **The picker does not offer creating a new canned line. That stays in Settings

### What the service is worth

- **S4-R6: **A service will invent no prices and estimate no time. Its hours are the sum of its lines' hours, and it shows no value anywhere in this feature
- **S4-R7:** The picker lists the canned lines of the schedule's home location, whichever location is chosen in the header. It has no Location field
- **S4-R8:** The Canned lines step carries the helper: Set up at Calgary South, where this schedule was built. Other locations add the same work at their own rates
- **S4-R9:** Only a user with access to the home location can change a service's canned lines. For anyone else the step is read only, with no Add canned lines, no remove and no reordering, and an (i): These lines belong to Calgary South. Only someone with access to Calgary South can change them. The other steps stay editable

### The line count

A hover card rather than a tooltip, because the lines have to be readable. It lists the services covered before the service's own lines.

- **S4-R5: **The line count is not a link and opens nothing. Hovering it opens a card styled as a small modal that lists the services it covers and then its canned lines, one per row, and closes when the pointer leaves

### Living without them, and changes later

Canned lines are optional. Many shops type every line by hand, and the feature works for them unchanged.

- **S4-N1: **A service with no canned lines saves normally and is fully functional
- **S4-N2: **A shop that types every line freeform never uses this and is not prompted to
- **S4-E1: **A canned line edited later in Settings changes what the service adds from then on
- **S4-E2: **A canned line deleted in Settings leaves the service with one fewer line, without error

## S5: Reminder timing

**Jira: **[SV-10562](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10562)

Phase: P1 for the rows, which sit in the service form; P5 for the email they will drive

**Design:** [Maintenance Reminders design](https://claude.ai/design/p/4411588b-b915-46d5-a8b8-43628a02e02a)

As an admin, or anyone holding Settings Service, I want to set when a service starts to read due soon, so that the shop has time to book it.

**Prerequisites**

- A service form is open

### The rows

The last step of the service form. Each service carries its own rows, because a timing belt and an oil change do not need the same notice.

- **S5-R1: **Reminder timing is set on each service, as one list of rows, each reading \[ before | after \] \[ n \] \[ days \]. Rows are in days and nothing else. Services on one schedule may warn differently, because a large job needs weeks of notice where a routine one needs days, and one schedule with per-service timing keeps a manufacturer's schedule whole rather than splitting it in two
- **S5-R2: **Every new service starts with three default rows: 14 days before, on the due date, and 7 days after. The shop changes them per service
- **S5-R3: **The due date row cannot be deleted. Its delete control is disabled, not hidden
- **S5-R4: **The ceiling is five rows, after which Add reminder disables
- **S5-R12:** A reminder row's days run 1 to 365. Two identical rows cannot both be saved. A before row must be shorter than the service's calendar interval: for a service every 7 days, a before row runs 1 to 6 days. A before row of the interval or longer is refused inline. For an interval in months, a month counts as 30 days, so a 1-month service allows a before row of 1 to 29 days
- **S5-R13:** Where a service's calendar interval is 14 days or shorter, its default 14 days before row is left out, because S5-R12 would refuse it. A note beneath the rows names the limit, for example A reminder before the due date must be shorter than the 7-day interval

### What the rows drive

The worklist and the asset tab, and nothing else in this release.

- **S5-R8:** The rows drive when a service reads due soon on the asset tab, the worklist and the panel. They do not decide whether a row is listed on the worklist, which S13 does. In v1 they email nobody
- **S5-N1:** Nothing in v1 emails a customer automatically. There is no send switch on the schedule or on a service. A reminder reaches a customer only when someone presses Send reminder on the contact card, per S14; automatic sending is deferred to v2. Reminders are not labelled internal-only
- **S5-E1:** Rows after the due date carry no internal effect in v1: a service is overdue from its due date onward whatever they say. They are kept for automatic sending, deferred to v2

### Compliance is the exception

- **S5-R11: **A compliance service has no reminder rows. Its form shows Remind before expiry, per S3-R8, in their place

## S6: Edit and archive a schedule

**Jira: **[SV-10563](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10563)

**Phase: **P1

**Design:** [Maintenance Reminders design](https://claude.ai/design/p/4411588b-b915-46d5-a8b8-43628a02e02a)

As an admin, or anyone holding Settings Service, I want to change a schedule without disturbing the assets already on it, so that editing is safe.

**Prerequisites**

- A schedule exists, with or without assets enrolled

### Editing is silent

Enrolment copies a schedule onto an asset rather than linking to it. That is why an edit changes nothing already enrolled.

- **S6-R1: **Applying a schedule to an asset copies its services onto that asset
- **S6-R2: **Editing a schedule afterwards will not touch assets already carrying it. They are not notified, not recalculated, not migrated
- **S6-R3: **Editing will be silent. No interstitial, no affected asset count, no conflict preview
- **S6-R4: **Assets enrolled later receive whatever the schedule says at that time
- **S6-N1: **There is no re-apply or refresh action for already enrolled assets
- **S6-N2: **In this release a change to a schedule does not reach assets already on it. Removing the asset and enrolling it again, or archiving the schedule, moves it to the schedule's current form

### Editing or removing a service

The confirmation opens over the service editor and says what happens: assets already enrolled keep the service, assets enrolled later get the schedule as it now stands.

- **S6-R11: **A service can be edited or removed from a schedule. Both ask for confirmation over the service editor, naming the service and the outcome: assets already enrolled are untouched, assets enrolled from now on get the schedule as it now stands. Removing a service that another covers names that service, for example PM-C covers PM-B. Removing PM-B takes it off PM-C; the lines already copied into PM-C stay
- **S6-N3: **Removing a service never removes it from an asset already carrying it. There is no retroactive path, by design, and nothing warns as though there were

### Duplicating

- **S6-R12:** An active schedule can be duplicated. The copy is named after the original with (Copy), then (Copy 2) and so on, as inspection templates are, lands in the list beside it, opens for editing, and carries no enrolled assets. An archived schedule offers Restore alone

### Archiving and restoring

Archiving takes every asset off the schedule, and restoring puts none of them back. The confirmation says so and its button is red.

- **S6-R5: **A schedule can be archived, never deleted
- **S6-R6: **Archiving deactivates the schedule and unenrolls every asset on it. The confirmation says so in these words: Archiving deactivates the schedule. All assets enrolled in the schedule will be unenrolled. Its action is red
- **S6-R7: **An archived schedule cannot be applied to new assets
- **S6-R8: **Restoring an archived schedule makes it available to enrol again. It enrols nothing: the assets archiving unenrolled stay unenrolled until someone enrols them again

### Removing one asset from a schedule

From the asset's Maintenance tab, one unit can be taken off one schedule without touching anything else. This is how a unit enrolled by mistake, sold or moved to another schedule is handled.

- **S6-R13: **Remove from schedule, in the schedule's menu on the asset's Maintenance tab, takes that one asset off that schedule. It asks first: Remove 402 from Highway Tractor PM? Its maintenance history is kept. Its reminders from this schedule stop. The action is red and needs the same permission as enrolling
- **S6-R14: **After Remove from schedule, that schedule's rows leave the asset tab and the worklist. History stays, per S6-E1; the asset's certificates stay, per S8-R8; an open work order is not changed. Enrolling the asset again later counts from its last completion, per S6-E2. Changing an asset's schedule is Remove from schedule, then Enrol

### What survives

- **S6-E1: **Removing a schedule from an asset does not destroy history. Cycles belong to the asset and service pair
- **S6-E2: **Applying a new schedule afterwards anchors each service from the last completion of the service of the same name on that asset, otherwise from now
- **S6-E4: **Archiving unenrolls without destroying history. Each asset keeps its completed cycles per S6-E1, so enrolling it again later anchors from the last completion per S6-E2
- **S6-E5:** A duplicate keeps the original's home location and its canned lines, whichever location it is duplicated from
- **S6-E3: **A new regulatory requirement is the known weak point. The shop must apply the schedule again per unit and nothing warns it

## S7: Enrol an asset onto a schedule

**Jira: **[SV-10564](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10564)

**Phase: **P2

**Design:** [Maintenance Reminders design](https://claude.ai/design/p/4411588b-b915-46d5-a8b8-43628a02e02a)

**As a** service advisor, **I want** to put an asset on a schedule, **so that** its services start being tracked.

**Prerequisites**

- A schedule exists
- The asset has a customer
- The user has view and edit customers

### Where it starts

Three places open the same modal: the asset's Maintenance tab, the maintenance panel on a work order, and the customer's Assets tab for several units at once.

- **S7-R1:** Enrolment starts from the asset's Maintenance tab, from the maintenance panel on a work order, or for several assets at once from the customer's Assets tab. All three open the same modal, which offers every active schedule of the organization, whichever location is chosen in the header, each with its home location per S1-R14 where there is more than one workplace
- **S7-R23: **Enrolment is for one of the asset's customers. Where the asset is linked to more than one customer, the modal asks which, preselected where there is only one. That customer's notification setting, preferred contact and name govern the asset on every surface, and the asset appears once
- **S7-N6: **Where the organization has no schedules, the enrolment modal says so plainly and offers the route to create one rather than presenting an empty control. This is the state every shop starts in
- **S7-E8: **A schedule the asset is already on is not offered in the modal

### Choosing the schedule, then the services

Picking a schedule fills the modal with its services. Each offers a date for when it was last done, filled in from the unit's history where there is one.

- **S7-R2: **The modal will collect, in order: the schedule, then one optional last service date per service. Current readings are not collected here, because the asset header and the work order already carry them
- **S7-R4:** Each last service date is filled in with the most recent completion on this asset of a service with the same name, from any schedule, and is otherwise blank. The advisor can change it. A renamed service has no match. Blank means counting starts today, stated in the modal. The date cannot be in the future. Names match ignoring capitals and surrounding spaces. Where two services match, the latest completion wins
- **S7-R7: **The modal title will be static. The unit and customer belong in the body at normal content weight
- **S7-R8: **(optional) will sit in brackets inline with its label
- **S7-R15: **Where a service watches a meter that has no usable readings, its row carries a badge naming the meter, Needs mileage reading or Needs engine hours reading, so the advisor sees what the schedule cannot yet measure. The calendar date still shows
- **S7-E4: **An asset may be enrolled on more than one schedule. Overlapping services are allowed and produce separate rows
- **S7-E5: **Enrolment applies the schedule as it stands. Nothing is skipped or negotiated

### A compliance service in that list

A compliance row shows the certificate the asset already holds for a service of that name, or offers to add one without leaving enrolment.

- **S7-R6:** A compliance service will show its existing record's number and End date, or No record with + Add record

### Several assets at once

From the customer's Assets tab, beside New Asset. One customer at a time, found with the same search the tab already has, for example every Heavy Truck.

- **S7-R24:** The customer's Assets tab has Enroll in Schedule beside New Asset. It opens the enrolment modal with an Assets section: every asset of that customer with a checkbox and Select all, and a search matching type, make, unit and VIN
- **S7-R25: **In the Assets section an asset already on the chosen schedule is greyed, cannot be ticked, and reads Already on this schedule. The action reads Enroll N assets
- **S7-R26: **Bulk enrolment asks for no last service dates and no certificates. Each asset takes its dates from its own history, per S7-R4, and the modal says how many have one, for example 8 of 12 have a last service on record; the rest count from today. Certificates are added on each asset afterwards
- **S7-N7: **Bulk enrolment works for one customer at a time, from that customer's Assets tab. There is no enrolment across customers
- **S7-N4: **There is no bulk path from the schedule side. Enrolment starts from an asset, never by applying a schedule outward to many customers at once

### The customer's notification setting

The setting belongs to the customer and is shown here because this is where an advisor notices it. Changing it writes the customer's own field.

- **S7-R13: **The enrolment modal will show the customer's Send preventive maintenance notifications setting and let it be changed there, writing the customer's own field. An advisor who finds it off mid-enrolment does not have to leave the modal to change it
- **S7-R14: **Enrolling an asset on a schedule and enrolling a customer in notifications are two separate acts. A shop may track a unit internally while the customer receives nothing
- **S7-R17:** The setting is on by default, for existing and new customers alike. It is an exception a shop applies rather than a permission it must collect: where it is off, Send reminder is unavailable for that customer, per S14-R9
- **S7-R18: **The field lives on the customer as a live toggle, Maintenance notifications, on the customer information card, and as a checkbox in the enrolment modal per S7-R13. It is not in the customer edit dialog, and it is not a tab, a page or a preferences matrix

## S8: Compliance inspection records on the asset

**Jira: **[SV-10565](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10565)

**Phase: **P2

**Design:** [Maintenance Reminders design](https://claude.ai/design/p/4411588b-b915-46d5-a8b8-43628a02e02a)

As a service advisor, I want to record the certificates a unit holds, so that its compliance inspections have a real end date to track.

**Prerequisites**

- The asset exists
- The user has view customers, and edit customers to add or change a record

### Where records are reached

A record belongs to the asset. It is reached from the Compliance section on the asset card, whether or not the asset is on a schedule, and from the service row that uses it. The same form opens from enrolment and from the work order.

- **S8-R13:** The asset card carries a Compliance section below Contact, one line per current record, for example CVIP · AB-4471902 · ends 14 Oct 2026, each opening the record form, with + Add record beneath. It shows whether or not the asset is on a schedule
- **S8-R5: **One record form will serve two entry points: the enrolment modal's record row and the work order
- **S8-R2:** A record will carry type, term, Start date, End date, an optional certificate number and one optional attachment
- **S8-R6: **Field widths will follow one scale, half or full
- **S8-R7: **Where a record exists, the row will show it with Edit, and + Add record stays beside it for a renewal
- **S8-R12:** A record belongs to the asset and to the compliance service it was added for. An asset can hold several records for a service; the one with the latest expiry is current and drives the service. Renewing adds a new record; earlier ones stay read-only beneath the current one. Edit corrects the current record only. When the asset is enrolled on another schedule, a record added for a service of the same name carries over. A renewal drives the service as soon as it is entered, even when its Start date is still ahead

### Term and the two dates

A certificate has a Start date and an End date. Start date plus the term gives End date, End date less the term gives Start date, and a certificate is valid through its End date. Decided in review on 2 October 2026, replacing effective and expiry months, because a term counted in months leaves open which day a certificate ends on.

- **S8-R10:** Term, Start date and End date derive from one another: Start date plus the term fills End date, and End date less the term fills Start date. Both are days, picked from a calendar, so nobody has to guess whether a certificate ends on the same day or at the end of a month. A date a person typed always wins over a derived one. End date lands on the same day number: 14 Oct 2025 plus 12 months is 14 Oct 2026. Where that day does not exist in the end month, it is the last day of that month, so 31 Jan plus 1 month is the last day of February
- **S8-R11:** A record needs the term and at least one of the two dates. It cannot be saved with neither. A certificate in hand always shows when it ends; when it started is sometimes nowhere on the document
- **S8-E2:** A certificate is valid through its End date and lapses the day after. A weekend or holiday does not move it
- **S8-E1:** A certificate arrives already months old, issued by another shop. The Start date is entered by hand

### The number and the file

- **S8-R3: **The number field is labelled Certificate number
- **S8-R4: **The number field is optional but always present. Every v1 type produces a document
- **S8-R9:** A record carries one optional file: PDF, JPEG or PNG, up to 10 MB. It can be replaced or removed; replacing it replaces the record's only attachment

### What a record belongs to

- **S8-R8: **A record belongs to the asset and survives the removal of any schedule
- **S8-N1: **A record can exist with no schedule and no enrolment
- **S8-N2: **A compliance service with no record cannot come due and is excluded from every count
- **S8-N3: **There is no Certificates tab in v1. Records are reached from the asset card, per S8-R13, and from the service row that uses them
- **S8-E3: **A work order closing two compliance inspections asks for details per record, not once for the work order
- **S8-E4: **Adding a record for a service that is overdue moves its due date immediately

### Confidence, as the cards show it

Each card grades its own estimate. The rules belong to Chunk 2, in S11, and are copied here word for word so nobody building the cards has to look for them. The table was locked on 1 October 2026 with the design authority and Fabian.

**The confidence table**, locked on 1 October 2026 with the design authority and Fabian. Graded separately for mileage and for engine hours, per S11-R10. Rows are the age of the last recorded reading, columns the usable pairs.

| Last recorded reading | 1 usable pair (2 visits) | 2 or 3 pairs (3 or 4 visits) | 4 or more pairs (5 or more visits) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 30 days | Medium | High | High |
| 31 to 90 days | Low | Medium | High |
| 91 to 180 days | Low | Medium | Medium |
| 181 to 365 days | Low | Low | Medium |
| Over 365 days | Low | Low | Low |

**What each level shows**

| Confidence | A due date from this meter shows |
| --- | --- |
| High | a month |
| Medium | a month |
| Low | a month, with Low confidence beneath it |
| No data (no usable pair in 24 months) | no candidate from this meter; the calendar date governs, per S11-N2 |

**Evidence. **Measured on production on 1 October 2026, across 520,526 units linked to a customer. Of units with a mileage estimate, Low falls from 70% to 49%, Medium rises from 19% to 29% and High from 11% to 22%. 2,923 units move from Low to Medium and 1,911 from Medium to High; 7,673 whose readings are all older than 24 months move from Low to No data. 82% of return visits come within 90 days and 48% within 30, which is why the first row is 30 days. 94% of units have no usable mileage pair at all; for them the calendar governs whatever the table says.

- **S11-R10: **Distance and engine hours will each carry their own confidence, computed independently
- **S11-R17: **No data is a separate state, not a confidence level. It applies where there is nothing to estimate from, the calendar governs, and the row says the meter has no basis yet
- **S11-R22:** Confidence comes from one table that reads two things together: how old the unit's last recorded reading is, and how many usable pairs it has. Every combination has its own cell, given in S11-R24 and in the table beneath these requirements. Nothing is graded separately and nothing caps anything
- **S11-R23:** A usable pair is two consecutive readings that survive the guards in S11-R4, S11-R5, S11-R19 and S11-R20, after S11-R6 has removed impossible readings. Only readings from the last 24 months count, per S11-R26. No usable pair is No data. In a clean history one pair is two visits, two or three pairs are three or four visits, and four or more pairs are five or more visits. Where a guard discards a pair the count drops with it
- **S11-R24:** By age of the last recorded reading, then usable pairs. Up to 30 days: one pair Medium, two or more High. 31 to 90 days: one pair Low, two or three Medium, four or more High. 91 to 180 days: one pair Low, two or more Medium. 181 to 365 days: four or more Medium, fewer Low. Over 365 days: Low. A value shown In the shop per S10-R11 is not yet recorded and does not refresh the age until its work order is invoiced
- **S11-R25: **Measured from N visits, where a card shows it, counts the readings that took part in at least one usable pair. A visit whose only pairs were discarded does not count
- **S11-R26:** Readings older than 24 months are not used, for the rate or for the pair count. A unit whose readings are all older than that reads No data and the calendar governs, as for a unit never read
- **S11-R27:** The meter's hover ends with: This is the system's best estimate from this unit's past readings. Check its history if in doubt. Beside it, View work orders opens the asset's Work Orders tab
- **S11-E5:** Two visits, the last one yesterday: one pair, under 30 days, Medium. Six visits, five pairs, the last reading 120 days old: Medium. Twelve visits over three years, the last one eight months ago: Medium. The same unit fourteen months after its last visit: Low. Every reading older than 24 months: No data, and the calendar governs

- **S18-R17:** After Mark complete a toast reads PM-A marked complete · next due counts from 2 Oct 2026, with Undo. - **S14-R4:** Send reminder sends the email of S19 for that one asset, with its current state, through the application's existing send email dialog, per S19-R3. It carries none of the customer's other units, per S14-R6
- **S14-R6: **A send from a row will cover that one asset only, never the customer's other units
- **S14-E2: **Sending a reminder writes an audit entry and updates last sent

### What the button says

- **S14-R5:** The send control reads Send reminder. Nothing is emailed automatically in v1, so there is no Resend
- **S14-R12: **A line beneath the address reads last sent \<date\> once a reminder has been sent by hand for that asset, so nobody sends twice without knowing
- **S14-R13:** Send reminder is offered whenever at least one of the customer's contacts has an email address, even where the asset's preferred contact has none. It opens the send email dialog of S19-R3, where the user picks who receives it from the customer's contacts. Send reminder is never disabled for having nothing due, per S19-R7

### When sending is not possible

A customer who opted out is something the advisor can see and change, so the button stays in view. A missing permission is not, so the button is not shown.

- **S14-R9: **Where the customer's notification setting is off, the row still appears and the send action is unavailable, naming that setting as the reason. An advisor must never press send and get silence
- **S14-N1: **Where the customer's notification setting is off, Send reminder is disabled with the reason shown beside it, not hidden
- **S14-R10: **Sending by hand follows the permission to edit a customer, the same one that governs the notification setting per S7-R22. A user without it does not see Send reminder at all, hidden rather than disabled, because it is not something they can fix from the card
- **S14-N5: **Nothing is hidden from the worklist because of the notification setting. It affects sending and nothing else

### Missing contact details

- **S14-R8: **The card will render a state for a contact with no email, and a state for a contact with no phone number
- **S14-N2:** A contact with neither email nor phone shows both empty states. There is no send action unless another of the customer's contacts has an email address, per the rule above
- **S14-N4: **An asset with no preferred contact shows an empty state in place of the contact details, with an action to set one. There is no send action until a contact exists
- **S14-E1: **The empty states are common rather than exceptional, so the card must read as complete in every one of them

### What is not tracked

- **S14-E3: **Read receipts are not tracked

## S21: Audit and traceability

**Jira: **[SV-10576](https://shopview.atlassian.net/browse/SV-10576)

**Phase: **every phase

**Design:** [Maintenance Reminders design](https://claude.ai/design/p/4411588b-b915-46d5-a8b8-43628a02e02a)

**As a** shop owner, **I want** every change recorded, **so that** a disputed date can be explained.

**Prerequisites**

- None. This applies across the feature

### Requirements

- **S21-R1: **Every state change produces an entry with an actor and a timestamp: readings, corrections, completions and their undo, skips, compliance record changes, cycle-date corrections in the step after invoicing, enrolments, removals from a schedule, schedule edits, archive and restore, and sends
- **S21-R2: **Adding a service to a work order will write both a work order note and an audit entry, naming the schedule and service it came from
- **S21-R3: **A completion will record where it happened, including when that is another shop
- **S21-R4: **A reading correction will keep both values, each with its timestamp and author
- **S21-R5: **Sends will be logged with message, recipient and timestamp
- **S21-R6:** Audit entries will outlive the objects they describe. Maintenance records, readings, certificates and audit entries are never hard deleted. An asset itself is deleted as ShopView deletes it today; its maintenance history stays in the data, with no screen to view it in v1
- **S21-R7: **A change to a customer's notification setting is recorded with who made it and when. A shop asserting a customer's consent on their behalf is the one thing that later needs a name and a date against it
- **S21-N1: **A work order link is never hard deleted, or the trail goes with it
- **S21-N2: **An unenrolled asset and an archived schedule keep their entries
- **S21-N3:** No screen shows the audit in v1. Every entry is recorded from release, and a screen to read them follows later
- **S21-E1: **Where the note and the audit entry record the origin, a per line tag is cosmetic and can be deferred
- **S21-E2: **A due date that moved weeks after a new reading must be traceable to the reading that moved it
