# Business Case

Preventive maintenance is work a shop already knows is coming, and today it loses most of it. A unit's PM falls due, nobody notices, and the truck comes back three months later for something else. The shop finds out it missed the work when the customer has already had it done elsewhere, or when the unit arrives with a failure that a scheduled service would have caught.

There is no list. Shops that track maintenance at all track it in a spreadsheet, in a diary, or in one advisor's head. Compliance inspections are worse, because a lapsed certificate means the truck cannot legally run and the shop is the last to know.

The feature exists to answer one question every morning: **which units need attention, and who do I call.**

# Feature Overview

A shop defines **maintenance schedules** in Settings. A schedule is an ordered list of **services**, each with the intervals that bring it due: a calendar interval which is always present, and optional distance and engine-hour triggers. A service can instead be a **compliance inspection**, tracked against a certificate on the asset rather than against a meter.

An asset is **enrolled** onto a schedule. Enrolment copies the services onto the asset, so later edits to the schedule never disturb units already carrying it.

From then on the system computes when each service comes due, and shows it in three places: the **asset Maintenance tab** for one unit, the **Maintenance reminders worklist** under Customers for the whole shop, and the **maintenance panel on a work order** for the truck physically in the bay.

In v1 a customer is emailed only by hand, with Send reminder on the contact card, one asset at a time. Nothing in the schedule builder switches anything on, and automatic sending is deferred to v2.

# Jobs to be Done

- As a service advisor, on a Monday morning, I want to know which units are overdue and which are coming up, so I can spend the morning booking work rather than searching for it.
- As a service advisor, when a truck is in the bay, I want to know what else it is due for, so the visit is not wasted.
- As a service manager, I want maintenance policy defined once and applied to many units, rather than remembered per customer.
- As a shop owner, I want to know that no unit's compliance inspection has quietly lapsed.
- As a shop owner, I want to know what this earned, so I can tell whether it was worth building.

# Key Decisions

**The calendar interval is mandatory and cannot be switched off.** Measured on production data, **82% of units have no usable meter history at all**: 73% have not one pair of readings surviving the guards. Without a calendar interval, four units in five would never produce a reminder. The calendar is not the fallback, it is the mechanism; the meter is the refinement.

A meter estimate says how confident it is, and the UI never claims more than it knows. A recorded reading shows an exact date. An estimate shows a month with its confidence beneath it. No meter basis at all means the calendar date governs, and the row says so. Confidence comes from one table that reads how fresh the last reading is together with how many usable pairs the unit has, from the last 24 months only; it was locked on 1 October 2026 after measuring it on production. The table is in S11, on Chunk 2 MR.

**Enrolment copies, it does not link.** Editing a schedule never touches units already on it. This makes editing safe and silent, at the cost that a new regulatory requirement must be applied again per unit.

A service restarts from the date confirmed in the step after invoicing, per S18-R13: the day its lines were all closed, the day it was marked complete, or the invoice date where there is nothing better. It resets only once the work order is invoiced, because the invoice is the moment the shop and the customer both treat as the work being done.

**Nothing blocks a reading.** A gauge and an ECU legitimately disagree, and a replacement engine resets the hours. Implausible values are questioned in orange and saved on confirmation, never refused.

Enrolling a fleet sends nothing. A shop recording history it kept on paper creates a backlog of already-overdue services. Those appear internally, and nothing in v1 emails a customer automatically.

Compliance inspections are their own type. A lapsed certificate takes the truck off the road, which is a different consequence from a missed oil change, so it is warned about months ahead (Remind before expiry) and is orange rather than red.

**There is no internal email in v1.** The worklist is the only internal surface. An internal digest needs group and notification-preference work that does not exist yet.

**Mileage is the only distance field, and its unit is the word mileage.** Kilometres and miles do not exist as separate concepts: the single unit covers both, which is why it is always written in full and never abbreviated to `mi` or `km`, an abbreviation would claim one of them. A second distance unit is a future consideration, deliberately out of scope. Throughout this spec, distance names the trigger and mileage names the field, the unit and the number in it.

Existing permissions govern this feature; no new one is introduced. Permissions are page-scoped, so each surface names the gate it already sits behind: the worklist and the asset Maintenance tab behind view customers; enrolling, removing from a schedule, entering a reading, skipping, marking complete and sending a reminder by hand behind create and edit customers; creating a work order and adding a service's lines behind create and edit work orders; the Settings tabs behind Settings Service. Whoever holds a bundle can do what it gates; no default role is changed for this feature, so out of the box schedules are built by admins and office users. Every gated action is hidden from a user without its permission, as Send reminder is; a technician sees the work order panel read-only.

**The feature has no QuickBooks or Customer Portal consequence.** It stops at creating a work order or an estimate; the only integration-adjacent thing it does is read a work order's invoice date to reset a cycle. Existing sync behaviour is unchanged. The data model and API surface do change, because the feature introduces new entities, and that is owned by engineering rather than settled here.

**The interval ceilings are arbitrary.** Mileage is capped at 999,999 and engine hours at 99,999 to catch a slipped digit, not to encode a domain rule. A shop asking for more is the signal to revisit them.

**A unit cannot borrow its rate from the customer's other units.** Tested against production on 2026-09-15 and rejected: 82.4% of units belong to a customer whose own units disagree on rate by more than 50%, so a fleet figure would describe none of their trucks. The instinct was sound and the coverage would have been large, 116,263 units have a sibling with usable history against 32,414 that have their own, but the customer is not the unit of usage. A mixed fleet spans the whole range. Measured weekly mileage on production runs 38 for a parked unit, 237 local and vocational, 636 regional, 1,614 long haul, and 6,045 for team operation, and that tenfold spread inside one customer is exactly why an average across it fails. The same measurement puts the local and vocational band at 237 miles a week against the 237 the US DOE single-unit figure implies, so the industry bands the feature leans on are confirmed rather than assumed. Grouping by customer, by make or by model should not be proposed again without new evidence.

Where a unit has no history, nothing is estimated. The calculation engine measures a rate from a unit's own readings and 73.3% of units never produce a usable pair, so for those the calendar carries the service and the row says the meter has no basis yet. An earlier version had the shop supply a rate at enrolment; it was removed on 2026-09-23 for two reasons that each stand alone. It broke the floor of two visits set in the design review of 15 September, being an estimate from none. And it is not safe on units: a measured rate compares a meter against itself and therefore holds whether a shop counts miles or kilometres, while a figure a person typed carries no such protection and lands far out for any shop counting in the other one. A unit earns its rate on its second visit, and until then the calendar is the honest answer.

One email, fixed wording, and consent on the customer record. Three emails, one per state, were cut in review on 15 September: a single unit can be due soon, due today and past due at once, and an owner should not have to open three messages to find one truck. One email carries everything inside the window, with the state on each row, and the wording is phrased so it never needs a singular and a plural form. The shop-editable subject, opening and closing lines were cut with it, a later version, not this one. The unsubscribe link went too, because honouring it properly is consent management and that is a feature of its own. Consent instead lives as a setting on the customer record, deliberately separate from enrolment, so a shop can track a unit internally while its customer receives nothing. Decided on 2 October 2026: in v1 the email is sent only by hand from the contact card, one asset per send, through the existing send email dialog, from the organization's name with replies going to the person who sent it. Automatic sending waits for v2.

A service states what it covers. PM-C covering PM-A and PM-B is set by the shop in the service form, never inferred from shared canned lines or from interval length, because shared lines say nothing about which service is the larger one.

Archiving a schedule unenrolls every asset on it, and restoring enrols nothing back. Decided in review on 25 September as the simplest behaviour that can ship; remembering past enrolments waits until customers ask for it.

The worklist shows no money in this release. An honest figure would have to account for customer and default labour rates, tax, fees and discounts, and the product's existing money figures are not yet reliable enough to add another.

The work order does not change. A maintenance service arrives as its canned lines, appended as ordinary lines; there is no nesting, no parent line and no new column. Which service added which line is stored, not drawn.

**Schedules belong to the organization; canned lines stay where they were built.** Decided on 2 October 2026. Canned lines, labour types and parts are kept per location in ShopView, so a schedule tied to one location's lines would be useless at the others. A schedule is visible, and can be enrolled onto, at every location; its canned lines stay at its home location. A work order at the home location adds the lines as today. Anywhere else Add Service adds the same work, names, descriptions and hours, at that location's rates, without prices or parts, and each copied line notes the parts the home location uses. Picking another location's lines in the schedule was considered and rejected: it would carry one location's prices and parts into another.

**Nothing emails a customer automatically in v1.** Decided on 2 October 2026. A reminder is sent by hand from the contact card, through the application's existing send email dialog: from the organization's name over the platform address, with replies going to the person who sent it, signed with their name and the location's telephone. Automatic sending, its daily schedule and its queue wait for v2; their rules are kept at the end of S19.

**No feature flag.** Decided on 5 October 2026. The feature ships to every organization at release. Past work order and imported readings are loaded once, so estimates work from the first day.

Every new list, tab and panel uses the application's standard loading and error behaviour: a loading state while it fetches, never a zero that reads as nothing due, and the standard error with Retry when a load fails. A failed save keeps the form open with its values.

Downstream surfaces are unchanged: estimate and invoice PDFs print a service's lines as any line; the customer portal shows an estimate raised from the worklist as any estimate; notifications send only what the shop's existing estimate and note settings send; exports and global search are untouched.

Dates follow the organization's date format setting. Examples in this PRD, such as 15 Nov, are illustrative.

Every screen in this release works on a phone. Tables become cards, dialogs open full screen, row and header menus open as action sheets, and every hover opens on tap.

# Assumptions

- Shops record mileage on most work orders. Measured at **72% in 2025 and 56% in 2026**, so the trend is against us and §7.4's collection work matters more than the estimation maths.
- Engine hours are recorded on roughly one visit in six, so hours-triggered services will lean on the calendar in practice.
- A customer's preferred contact on the asset is a good enough addressee for reminders.
- Shops operate in one timezone each. Only **25 organizations of 796** span more than one.
- Fleet customers are a meaningful share: **987 customers hold roughly a third of all vehicles**, so one email per customer is load bearing.
- Roughly half of contacts carry an email address, so the worklist and the phone carry the feature, not the email.

## Release

There is no feature flag. The feature ships to every organization at release. From release, readings are recorded for every organization, and the mileage and engine hours on past work orders and in imported history are loaded once as readings, so estimates work from a shop's first day, per S10.

# Delivery phases

The feature is built in five phases, each matching one surface so the spec, the design and the build stay on the same boundaries. Phases are a delivery order, not a change to what any story means; a story carries its phase and nothing else moves.

- P1: Settings, maintenance schedules. S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6. The root: nothing in the feature exists until a schedule does, and it depends on nothing else. S5's reminder rows are built here and drive the worklist from the start; they email nobody in v1.
- **P2: The asset. **S7, S8, S9. Enrolment, compliance records and the asset Maintenance tab. Requires P1.
- **P3: The worklist and the engine behind it. **S10, S11, S12, S13, S14. The largest phase and the one carrying every piece of data risk. It may be cut in two: the worklist on calendar triggers alone, then readings, rate and confidence unlocking the meter triggers. No screen is drawn twice either way.
- **P4: The work order. **S16, S17, S18. The panel, the two ways to satisfy a service, and the reset. Until this exists nothing ever completes and the worklist only grows, so this is the first point at which the feature is usable by a shop rather than a milestone for QA.
- P5: The customer email. S19, sent by hand from the contact card of S14. Automatic sending is deferred to v2.
- **Across every phase. **S21 and S22 have no surface of their own and are built with whatever phase they describe.

# Consent, gathered

Consent is one boolean on the customer, on by default, and its rules are spread across four stories because each belongs to a different surface. Nothing below is a new requirement; it is where to find them when consent is reviewed as one subject.

- **S7: the field itself. **The default, where it lives on the customer, the permission to change it, its scope across every unit the customer owns, and what happens when a unit moves to another customer.
- **S14: the contact card. **The row still appears when the setting is off; only sending is unavailable, and it names the setting as the reason.
- S19: the email. A customer whose setting is off cannot be sent a reminder for any of their assets, and there is no unsubscribe link, because consent is managed on the record rather than by a link in the mail.
- **S21: the audit. **Who changed the setting and when. A shop asserting a customer's consent on their behalf is the one thing that later needs a name and a date against it.

# Open Questions

Each is owned by someone who can answer it. The spec is not finished while any remains open.

- Which permission gates sending a reminder to a customer. Answered 2026-09-25: create and edit customers, per S14-R10
- Fast follow, not blocking the release: whether the reminder email, now sent by hand like an invoice, needs a postal address or an unsubscribe in its footer, and whether the customer setting is a sufficient consent basis (owner: legal). Until answered, the footer ships as specified in S19-R15
- The compliance inspection type list. Answered 2026-09-28: Type is free text with examples in a hover; suggestions by location follow in v2
- The data model and API surface consequence of the new entities (owner: engineering)
- How automated mail is sent: provider, whether mass send is possible, from and reply-to (owner: engineering). Answered 2026-10-02 for v1: sent by hand through the existing send pattern, from the organization's name over the platform address with Reply-To the sender, per S19-R17 and S19-R18. Mass sending waits for v2
- Whether the asset Maintenance table carries an interval column, and whether Last done stays on it. Answered 2026-09-28: yes to both, per S9-R6 and S9-R12
- Whether a completed, uninvoiced worklist row offers Invoice. Answered 2026-09-28: yes, per S13-R26
- How many assets are linked to more than one customer today, per S7-R23 (owner: engineering, read-only data)
- Whether a compliance certificate records Start and End. Answered 2026-10-02: yes, as days, with End filled from Start plus the term, per S8-R10
- How the work order maintenance card works on a phone, where the asset card sits behind Show details. Deferred from engineering's Chunk 2 review on 5 October 2026 (owner: product)
