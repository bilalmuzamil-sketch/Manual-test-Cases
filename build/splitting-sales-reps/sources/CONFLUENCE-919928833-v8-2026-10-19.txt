1. Business Case
A shop that pays commission on parts and labor separately usually has two people selling on the same job: a parts person who sells the parts and a service writer who sells the labor. Today a work order has one sales representative, the invoice copies that one name, and the Sales By Representative report credits the whole invoice to that person. The shop cannot see what each person sold, so it rebuilds commission in a spreadsheet. This is the request that started the Sales By Representative work (SV-4854: "A breakdown of parts and labor associated to the reps").
2. Feature Overview
Core functionality
- A work order card has one Sales Representative field. A + button beside it adds a second field, so the card shows "Sales Representative - Labor" and "Sales Representative - Parts". A remove x beside the Parts field takes it away again.
- A shop that never adds a field sees one Sales Representative field, as today. The one person is credited with the whole invoice.
- Adding the Parts field copies the person onto both fields, so adding it moves no credit. Someone then changes one field.
- A customer has the same field in Edit Customer and New Customer. Its + adds "Sales Representative - Service Parts" (the first field then reads "Sales Representative - Service Labor") and "Sales Representative - Part Sales", each on its own. New work orders and part sales start with the customer's representatives. They are also used when a work order or part sale is invoiced with nobody on it.
- Every representative field is the app's native dropdown. "Unassigned" is its first option, and picking it clears the field. There is no x inside a field.
- When a work order is invoiced, both representatives are captured on the invoice and do not change afterwards.
- Sales By Representative credits the labor to the Labor representative and the parts to the Parts representative, and shows which with a Sales Type: Labor, Parts or Part Sales. Shop supplies go with Labor. Whole-invoice fees and discounts are shared by the size of each side.
- A part sale keeps one representative, who is credited with the whole sale.
- On a phone the fields work like every other dropdown on the card, and the + and remove x buttons are always shown.
Out of scope
- More than two people on one work order, and per-line representatives.
- A second field on a part sale. Its card keeps one Sales Representative field, with no + button (Part Sales Update v1, SV-10266).
- Commission amounts or rates. ShopView reports what each person sold; it does not calculate pay.
- The Sales Follow-Up report. It is not in the Reports menu and does not load today, on staging or production, so no shop can open it. Nothing in it changes.
- The public API. It does not expose sales representatives today, and still does not.
- The customer-facing invoice and estimate documents. They do not show a sales representative today, and still do not.
- QuickBooks. No sales representative is sent to QuickBooks today, and still none is.
- Credit memos in Sales By Representative. The report does not include them today, and still does not.
- Imported work orders. They have no sales representative and are unchanged.
3. Jobs to be Done / Goals
- When a parts person and a service writer both sell on one job, I want to credit each of them with the side they sold, so I can pay commission from ShopView instead of a spreadsheet.
- When one person sells the whole job, I want the work order to work exactly as it does today, so I do not have to learn anything new.
- When a customer always buys parts from one person and service from another, I want both set on the customer once, so every work order for that customer is credited correctly without anyone remembering to set it.
Goals
- Every dollar of an invoice is credited to exactly one side, and the report's totals never change because of a split.
- A shop that never splits sees no visible change.
4. Key Decisions
- One field that grows, not two fields. The work order shows one Sales Representative field until someone adds the Parts field with the + beside it. Rejected: separate Labor and Parts fields on every card, which would double the card for every shop to serve the shops that use both.
- An added field starts with the person already credited. Adding the Parts field copies the person onto Labor and Parts, so adding it on its own moves no credit; someone changes one field afterwards. The new field's dropdown stays closed. Rejected: starting the new field "Unassigned", which could move credit if the work order is invoiced before anyone picks.
- Removing a field keeps the field that stays. Removing the Parts field leaves the Labor person as the one Sales Representative, credited with the whole work order. Rejected: putting back the person the work order had before the Parts field was added, which could bring back someone who is not on the screen.
- A customer has up to three fields: Service Labor, Service Parts and Part Sales. Service Parts and Part Sales are added on their own. Without a Part Sales field, part sales follow Service Parts, or the one representative. Rejected: two customer fields, which would make a part sale guess between the parts and the labor person.
- The work order card follows the design authority's work order card design, with the labels in this PRD: the + with its menu, the remove x beside an added field, and every box and button shown at once while the rows are hovered. Decided in the design review of 2026-10-13.
- An empty side stays empty. Picking "Unassigned" on one side of a work order leaves it Unassigned. It is not handed to the other side and not refilled from the customer. After a work order is created, the customer's representatives apply only when it has nobody on either side at invoicing. Rejected: refilling an empty side, which silently undoes a deliberate change.
- New work orders and part sales start with the customer's representatives, copied when they are created, so the card shows from the start who will be credited. Changing the customer afterwards never changes them, so a deliberate pick is never lost. A work order or part sale made from another one keeps that one's representatives. Rejected: applying the customer's representatives only at invoicing, as ShopView did before, which left the card reading "Unassigned" and hid who would be credited until the invoice.
- Shop supplies go to the Labor side. ShopView calculates shop supplies from the labor total, so the whole charge, including an amount typed by hand, is credited to Labor. Whole-invoice fees and discounts are shared by the size of each side. Fees and discounts on a part line or a labor line already belong to that side.
- A part sale keeps one representative and one Sales Type, Part Sales.
- Native fields. Every representative field is the same dropdown Lead Technician and Service Advisor use: "Unassigned" is the first option and there is no search box, because shops have few sales representatives. There is no x inside a field; picking "Unassigned" clears it. Rejected: a clear x inside the field, which the native fields do not have.
- Sales Type, not Role. The report column and filter say where the money came from: Labor, Parts or Part Sales. The column is off by default on screen, because the row's figures already show it and the report is wide. The Expanded exports always carry it, because that is where people filter and pivot.
- Past invoices do not change. Each existing invoice's one name is copied to both sides, so the report for any past date reads exactly as before.
- Always on. Every shop gets the field and the + button. There is no setting to turn them on or off.
5. Terminology
- Labor side / Parts side: the two halves of a work order's money. The card labels them "Sales Representative - Labor" and "Sales Representative - Parts". Labor holds labor, hours and shop supplies; Parts holds parts.
- Sales Type: where a row's money comes from on Sales By Representative. "Labor" and "Parts" are the two sides of a work order; "Part Sales" is a part sale. On Sales By Customer, Sales Type is the kind of invoice: "Part Sales" or "Service Sales".
- Split: a work order that shows the "Sales Representative - Labor" and "Sales Representative - Parts" fields, or a customer that shows the "Sales Representative - Service Parts" field. "Not split" means a work order with the one "Sales Representative" field, or a customer without the Service Parts field.
- Following: a customer with no Part Sales field gives its new part sales the Service Parts person, or its one representative when it is not split.
- Whole-invoice amounts: fees and discounts added to the invoice as a whole rather than to one part line or labor line.
- Sales representative: a staff member whose Sales Representative switch is on in their staff record. Only they can be picked.
6. Release day
What every shop sees on the day this ships. Nothing needs to be switched on.
- Every existing customer keeps its one sales representative and is not split, so the customer page reads as before (S4-E1). One wording changes: a customer with no representative reads "Unassigned" instead of "N/A" (S4-R19).
- Every open work order keeps its one sales representative on both sides and is not split, so its card reads as before.
- Past invoices do not change. Sales By Representative for any past date shows the same totals as before (S5-E2).
- New: from release day, a new work order or part sale starts with the customer's sales representatives already filled in (S4-R24 to S4-R26). Before, it started with nobody, and the customer's representative was only used at invoicing. A shop that has no representative on a customer sees no change for that customer.
- New: the New Customer window has a Sales Representative field (S4-R1). Before, a representative could only be set from Edit Customer.
- New: the + button beside the Sales Representative field adds a separate Labor and Parts field on a work order, and separate Service Parts and Part Sales fields on a customer (Stories 2 and 4).
- Sales By Representative filters by Sales Type (Labor, Parts, Part Sales), and the Sales By Customer filter labeled "Product Type" now reads "Sales Type" (Story 8).
- Each user's saved column choices and sort on Sales By Representative reset once to the defaults. A saved link that narrowed Sales By Representative by Product Type opens with every Sales Type selected.

Story 5: Credit each side on the invoice and in Sales By Representative
As a shop owner, I want each representative credited with the side they sold so that I can pay commission from the report.
Design: Splitting Sales Representatives design canvas, page 4
Jira: SV-11269
Prerequisites
- User has 'Reports — Page Access' enabled. It governs the Sales By Representative report on screen and its exports.
Requirements
- S5-R1: When a work order is invoiced, the parts representative and the labor representative in effect are both captured on the invoice, by name.
- S5-R2: The names captured on the invoice do not change afterwards, including when a staff member is renamed or deactivated. That is what the invoice stores. The report labels each person with their current name, as it does today, so a renamed representative stays one row; it shows the captured name only when the staff record no longer exists.
- S5-R3: The report reads every invoice as one row per representative per Sales Type: "Labor" for a work order's labor side, "Parts" for its parts side, and "Part Sales" for a part sale.
- S5-R4: A Labor row is credited with Labor Invoiced, Labor Margin, labor-line fees and discounts, Hrs Worked, Hrs Invoiced, Labor Delta and all of the invoice's Shop Supplies, including an amount typed by hand.
- S5-R5: A Parts row is credited with Parts Invoiced, Parts Margin and part-line fees and discounts.
- S5-R6: Whole-invoice fees and discounts are shared between the Labor and the Parts rows in proportion to each side's labor or parts dollars after that side's line fees and discounts, measured by size whatever their sign. The adjustment that stops a non-taxable discount from taking the subtotal below zero is a whole-invoice amount and is shared the same way. Example: labor $600.00, parts $400.00, shop supplies $30.00, whole-invoice discount ($50.00). The Labor row gets $30.00 supplies and ($30.00); the Parts row gets ($20.00). Subtotals $600.00 and $380.00 add up to $980.00.
- S5-R7: A cent left over by rounding the share goes to the Labor row.
- S5-R8: A whole-invoice amount on an invoice with no labor and no parts dollars goes to the labor representative. If the labor side is empty and the parts side is not, it goes to the parts representative. If both are empty, it is Unassigned.
- S5-R9: A part sale is one "Part Sales" row holding every dollar of the invoice.
- S5-R10: When one person, or Unassigned, holds both the Labor and the Parts side of a work order and the Sales Type filter includes both, the two show as one row whose Sales Type reads "Labor, Parts" and whose figures are the sum of both sides.
- S5-R11: Two different people on one work order are always two rows.
- S5-R12: With the Sales Type filter narrowed to Labor or to Parts, only that side's row shows (Story 8).
- S5-R13: A representative counts an invoice once, even when they have two rows for it. When two people split an invoice, each counts it once, so the representatives' invoice counts can add up to more than the shop's.
- S5-R14: With Show Unassigned on, the report's totals are the same whether invoices are split or not. With it off, a side credited to Unassigned is left out of the totals, as Unassigned money is today.
- S5-R15: On a Labor row the parts columns show a dash, and on a Parts row the labor, hours and Shop Supplies columns show a dash, not $0.00. $0.00 is shown only for a real zero.
- S5-R15a: On a Part Sales row, Shop Supplies shows the part sale's shop supplies, or a dash when it has none.
- S5-R16: A representative's summary row always shows real sums, never dashes.
- S5-R17: On an invoice row, the Margin % uses only the side the row is credited with.
- S5-R18: A side with no representative is credited to Unassigned, which shows when Show Unassigned is on, as today.
- S5-R19: An invoice row carries a "Sales Type" column, after Status, reading "Labor", "Parts", "Part Sales" or "Labor, Parts".
- S5-R20: The Sales Type column is offered in the report's column selector, first in its list.
- S5-R21: The Sales Type column is off by default on screen, for every user.
- S5-R22: A user who turns the Sales Type column on keeps it on for later visits.
- S5-R23: The Expanded CSV and the Expanded PDF always carry the Sales Type column, after Status, whatever the screen shows. In them, the cells of the side a row is not credited with are blank in the CSV and show a dash in the PDF.
- S5-R24: The Sales Type column holds no money, so the report's existing rules for who may see money apply to the other columns unchanged.
Negative cases
- S5-N1: An invoice totaling $0.00 adds $0.00 to each representative it is credited to, and counts once for each, as a single representative's $0.00 invoice does today. On a split work order, a side with no dollars (for example no parts at all) still gives its representative a $0.00 row, and the invoice counts once for them.
- S5-N2: A negative amount is always credited to a representative, never dropped or left out. A returned part on the invoice reduces the parts representative and a labor credit reduces the labor representative, the same way positive amounts are credited.
Edge cases
- S5-E1: When one side is negative, the shares are still measured by size. Example: labor $600.00, parts ($100.00), whole-invoice discount ($70.00). The shares are six sevenths and one seventh, so the Labor row gets ($60.00) and the Parts row ($10.00). Subtotals $540.00 and ($110.00) add up to $430.00, the invoice total.
- S5-E2: Every invoice created before the release has its one representative on both sides, so the report for any past date reads exactly as before, with that person's Labor and Parts rows shown as one "Labor, Parts" row (S5-R10). Invoices created before 2026-07-29 have no representative recorded; they stay Unassigned on both sides, and none is added.
* Context note: the report already adds up labor and parts separately on every invoice. This story changes who is credited with each part, not how the money is counted.
