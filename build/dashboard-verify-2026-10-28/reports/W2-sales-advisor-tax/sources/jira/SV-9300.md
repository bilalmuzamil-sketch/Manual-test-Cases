## Summary

Sales Tax Collected report is pulling incorrect Parts Total values for specific work orders (e.g., S-2366, S-2262), leading to reported parts and tax-adjustment fields being based on overstated parts amounts.

## Steps to Reproduce

1\. Open the Sales Tax Collected report in ShopView and review the totals for the relevant period (customer referenced 61 paid July invoices).

2\. From that report, identify Work Order S-2366 (Markham Landscape Products, July 31) and compare the report’s Parts Total to the Work Order’s own parts sell price/details shown in ShopView.

3\. From that report, identify Work Order S-2262 (Solar Farms Inc, July 6) and compare the report’s Parts Total to the Work Order’s own parts sell price/details shown in ShopView.

## Expected Behavior

The **Parts Total** in the Sales Tax Collected report should include only parts that were actually invoiced and should reconcile with the corresponding Work Order/Invoice values. Declined, removed, or other non-invoiced parts should not be included.

## Actual Behavior

The Sales Tax Collected report is showing significantly higher Parts Total values for certain invoices. Examples:

* **S-2366 – Markham Landscape Products:** Work Order Stats show **$56.45** in Parts Sell Price, while the report shows **$7,606.46** in Parts Total. The entire invoice subtotal is only **$463.70**.
* **S-2262 – Solar Farms Inc:** Work Order Stats show **$0.00** in Parts Sell Price, while the report shows **$977.10** in Parts Total. The entire invoice subtotal is only **$798.55**.

This suggests the report may be including parts from **declined, removed, or otherwise non-invoiced lines**, resulting in overstated parts sales and inaccurate reporting/accounting reconciliation.

## Impact

Customers/finance users reviewing the Sales Tax Collected report may have incorrect “Accurate Taxable Parts Sales” and “Adjusted Labor Sales” results due to overstated Parts Total for specific work orders.
